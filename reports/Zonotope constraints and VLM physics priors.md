# Turn confident VLM guesses into safe sets

The literature supports a one-paper plan, but the open gap is narrower than "VLM priors plus zonotopes". The paper the user remembered is **Phys2Real (arXiv 2510.11689, ICRA 2026)**. It comes from Mac Schwager's Stanford lab with Jiajun Wu, not from Jeannette Bohg's lab. It fuses a GPT-5 guess of an object's centre of mass with an online estimate. But it treats the guess as one Gaussian value, admits the VLM "may be confidently wrong", and has **no safety constraints and no uncertainty sets**. Other papers each own one more piece. RigPI uses a VLM to box a parameter search, but offline. Brunke et al. turn language relations into point-cloud safety sets at 45 Hz. The ARMTD family enforces zonotope constraints in real time with a brake-to-stop fail-safe, and WAITR adds friction-uncertain objects on a tray. Two 2026 papers from Amr Alanwar's group already map language to zonotope-type sets. **We found no paper that joins a VLM physical prior, a calibrated parameter set, a hard set constraint in a real-time controller, and online shrinking of that set, and none that does it for a bimanual payload.** The user's HJ claim is half right. HJ reachability accepts any failure shape and gives the largest safe set. But it is slow to compute, and safety for all time needs only *some* set the controller can stay inside, such as a brake-to-stop set. Polynomial annihilation is a sound jump detector that nobody has used on geometry. It should split shapes where a zonotope wastes volume, not where edges are sharp. The project's own BOED test gives the paper its thesis. Knowing the ball's rolling resistance made carries **about 26% faster at no extra drops**. A probe rule that acted on the belief's average risk added **6.2 points of drops**. So the claim to test is this: acting on the worst case over a calibrated set keeps drops fixed, and shrinking the set buys speed.

## One problem, one mechanism

**Problem.** A VLM's guess of a payload's physics is sometimes confidently wrong, and a real-time bimanual controller that acts on that guess as a single best value will drop, spill or collide before online adaptation can correct it.

**Mechanism.** The paper answers with five steps. First, a VLM names the object and gives a prior over its hidden parameters: mass, centre of mass, friction, rolling resistance. Second, **conformal prediction** widens that prior into a **parameter set**. Conformal prediction turns a model's errors on a held-out calibration set into a range with a stated hit rate, for example "contains the true value for 95% of objects". Third, the parameter set and the point-cloud geometry become one family of constraints built from **zonotopes**. A zonotope is a centrally symmetric convex shape, written as a centre plus a sum of line segments called generators. Adding two sources of uncertainty (the Minkowski sum) just means listing both sets of generators, which is why zonotopes suit a single constraint language. Fourth, the controller enforces these sets as hard rows in its real-time **quadratic program (QP)**. On the arms that QP is **FL-MPC**, the project's feedback-linearized model predictive controller: τ = M·a + c makes each joint a double integrator, so the plan's prediction is exact. On the tray the same job falls to the analytic set filter **F5**. Fifth, the set shrinks online from the robot's own motion and, when it pays, from a probe that is **certified safe before it runs**. The VLM may set or widen the prior. It never certifies safety.

**Claim.** The project's BOED test already shows what information is worth [P]. Over 1,800 tracked tray carries, a policy that knew the true rolling resistance (P5) finished non-dropped carries in **14.3 s against 19.2 s** for the best single speed without information (P6), at **26.5% vs 26.7% drops**. A probe at pickup followed by a speed choice from the posterior's predicted drop rate (P3) was 17% faster, but dropped **+6.2 points more** (95% CI +4.7 to +7.8). The belief itself was calibrated: its 90% interval covered the truth 96% of the time. The extra drops sat in the mid-range of rolling resistance, at the decision boundary of the speed rule. 0 of 1,800 probes dropped ([RESULT_BOED](/common/users/ss5772/bimanual_data/tray_rep/boed/RESULT_BOED.md)). The paper's claim follows directly. Information buys speed, not safety. So safety must come from the worst case over a set, and information should only shrink that set.

## Phys2Real owns the prior-and-adapt loop but adds no safety

The remembered paper is **"Phys2Real: Fusing VLM Priors with Interactive Online Adaptation for Uncertainty-Aware Sim-to-Real Manipulation"**. The authors are Maggie Wang, Stephen Tian, Aiden Swann, Ola Shorinwa, Jiajun Wu and Mac Schwager, and the arXiv comment says it was accepted to ICRA 2026 [V] ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11689)). The project page does not mention Bohg, and Schwager's MSL lists it as its own [V] ([project page](https://phys2real.github.io/); [MSL](https://msl.stanford.edu/bibliography/wang2026phys2real.html)). Bohg's lab page for 2024 to 2026 shows no paper that pairs a VLM physical prior with online adaptation [V] ([IPRL publications](https://iprl.stanford.edu/publications)). The mix-up is easy to make: Bohg and Schwager co-wrote "Get a Grip" (2410.23701) and "Breaking Lock-In" (2604.23121) [V].

The method is simple. **GPT-5** estimates an object's vertical centre of mass from 5 views, 5 queries per image. The prior's spread is the mean of the VLM's *self-reported* uncertainty. The authors did not use the spread of its answers, because that gave poor uncertainty and "the VLM may be confidently wrong" [V] ([arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11689v2)). Online, an ensemble of 10 adaptation networks in the style of Rapid Motor Adaptation (RMA) reads a 10-step history and predicts the parameter with an uncertainty. An inverse-variance weighted average then fuses prior and estimate [V]. On a UFactory xArm pushing objects, it beat domain randomization: **100% vs 79%** on a bottom-heavy T-block, **57% vs 23%** on a top-heavy one (the project page says 24%), and a hammer push 15% faster [V] ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11689)). Note one nuance. The online part infers one parameter with a network trained like RMA, which suggests training in simulation. The robot does not learn a residual model online. The notes could not confirm this [I].

The paper's own limits section draws our map. It lists **no safety constraints, no safe exploration and no uncertainty sets**. It names **conformal prediction** as future work for calibrating the VLM. It says many parameters are hard to scale to, and that the prior matters less in contact-rich tasks that give dense feedback [V] ([arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11689v2)). Our step two is their future work, and our steps three to five are outside their scope. That makes Phys2Real the baseline to beat and also a scoop risk.

Three other lines hold set-shaped priors but use them for no safety purpose. **Scan, Materialize, Simulate** (SMS, 2505.14938) asks a VLM for density, friction and restitution, then plans open-loop with no uncertainty handling [V] ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14938v1)). **RigPI** (2606.25212) uses the VLM for "informed initialization and a constrained search space", then fits inertia and friction offline in a differentiable simulator. It reports real-robot tests and no safety use [V] ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25212)). Its venue line reads "IROS 2027", probably a typo, so the venue is unconfirmed. **DrEureka** (2406.01967) bounds domain-randomization ranges by what a policy survives in simulation and lets an LLM choose inside them. Its only "safety" is a line in the reward prompt [V] ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.01967)). Adami et al. (2601.08454) wrap VLM-planned sensing in a behavior tree that aborts on torque thresholds, with "no formal uncertainty quantification" [V] ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08454v2)). Safe-learning methods do use sets, but their priors are hand-set or meta-learned, never from a VLM [S/V] ([Rothfuss et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.00762v3); [Safe Stochastic Explorer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00868)).

## A BRT gives the largest safe set, not the only one

Some definitions first. **HJ (Hamilton-Jacobi) reachability** solves a partial differential equation over a grid of states. It finds the states from which failure cannot be avoided. That set over time is the **backward reachable tube (BRT)**. A **control-invariant set** is a set of states that the controller can keep the system inside forever. The complement of the infinite-horizon BRT is the *largest* such set.

**Verdict: half right.** The true part is that HJ accepts any failure set, even a learned classifier, and returns the largest safe set with its best safe policy. The claim "solved fast" is wrong. And a BRT is not *needed* for guarantees over an infinite horizon.

**Any control-invariant subset gives safety for all time.** The Hsu–Hu–Fisac survey says many methods "forego the maximal safe set and instead seek a smaller safe set" that still gives "a reliable, if more restrictive, safety filter". In the extreme case, the set of collision-free states at rest is valid, though it freezes the robot [V] ([arXiv 2309.05837](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05837)). Wabersich et al. say an MPC terminal set only has to be control invariant, and "a trivial choice … is any equilibrium point" [V] ([IEEE CSM 2023](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10473415-data-driven-safety-filters-hamilton-jacobi-reachability-control-barrier-functions-predictive-methods-uncertain-systems)). gatekeeper proves that following a short, forward-propagated safe path keeps the system safe "for all future time" [V] ([arXiv 2211.14361](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14361)). A **backup control barrier function (backup CBF)** gets its safe set by rolling out a known backup law, such as braking, over a short horizon. With an aggressive LQR backup on a 3D Dubins car, its set was "almost identical" to the HJ set [V] ([Chen et al., arXiv 2104.11332](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.11332)). Gurriet et al. ran backup filters on a Segway, an industrial manipulator, a quadrotor and an exoskeleton [V] ([IEEE Access 2020](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2020.3025248)).

**HJ is fast only to look up, not to solve.** On the 3D Dubins car, grid HJ took **37.3 s offline and 0.5 ms online**. The backup CBF needed no offline step and took 1.4 ms of rollout plus a 3.6 ms QP. On a 16D quadrotor, grid HJ could not run at all, while the backup CBF took 4.92 + 28.33 ms. The authors put HJ's limit at 4 to 5 state dimensions [V] ([arXiv 2104.11332](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.11332)), and the survey puts it at six [V] ([arXiv 2309.05837](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05837)). That is where the claim contradicts itself. A constraint that is *arbitrary and new at run time* forces HJ to recompute, which is exactly what HJ cannot do fast. Latent Safety Filters lists the "need to recompute a safety value function for any new additional constraints" as a limit [V] ([arXiv 2502.00935](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00935v3)). Every speed-up gives up part of the guarantee. DeepReach reached 9 and 10 dimensions, and an MPC-supervised successor 40 [V] ([DeepReach](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.02082); [Feng et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03830)). But a learned value "may not be safe", and the fix gives only a probabilistic guarantee after scenario checks [V] ([Lin & Bansal, arXiv 2209.12336](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.12336)). The HJRNO neural operator runs in 3 to 5 ms instead of about 1 to 3 s, but was shown only in 3D and 4D, trains on grid solutions, and gives no guarantee [V] ([arXiv 2504.19989](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19989v2)).

**"Arbitrary constraint" does not set HJ apart.** HJ handles nonconvex constraints "in theory", but in practice it has been limited to hand-built, low-dimensional collision models [V] ([arXiv 2502.00935](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00935)). Rollout-based filters only need to *evaluate* a constraint along a predicted path, so any computable constraint works [I]. What is unique to HJ is the backward, largest, game-theoretic set. **HJ is the right tool** when the state is small (about 5 to 6 dimensions) or splits into small parts, the dynamics are nonlinear or face an adversary, the largest set matters, and the constraint is fixed or can be parameterized ahead of time [I, synthesized from the surveys]. Latent HJ is the natural form for failures like spills that cannot be written down. Its authors say it "lacks formal assurances" [V] ([arXiv 2502.00935](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00935v3)).

For this project, two arms of 6 joints with positions and velocities give 24 states, far beyond grid HJ [I]. FL-MPC already makes the joint dynamics linear, so set methods are close to exact there [I]. The literature's standard route to safety for all time is a brake-to-stop terminal constraint, as in ARMTD and SPARROWS [V] ([ARMTD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.01591); [SPARROWS](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.08857v1)). On the real Piper that terminal set barely exists (see the risks). One place remains where grid HJ could fit if F5 proves too cautious: the ball on the tray. It has 4 states in the tray frame, plus rolling resistance as a parameter, plus tray tilt if the slew limit matters, so 5 to 7 dimensions. That is at the edge of grid reach. Parameter-conditioned HJ handles such a parameter as an extra "virtual state" solved offline [V] ([Borquez et al., arXiv 2209.14976](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.14976)). The user's plan, "HJ only if analytic sets are not enough", matches the literature.

## Zonotope planners assume their obstacles, and language-to-set work assumes its physics

**The ARMTD family proves real-time zonotope constraints but never perceives its obstacles.** In ARMTD, ARMOUR, SPARROWS and CROWS, zonotopes describe the *robot's* swept volume. Obstacles are given as zonotopes: motion-capture boxes in ARMTD, axis-aligned cubes in SPARROWS and CROWS [V] ([ARMTD PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.01591); [CROWS](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.09924v1)). Every ARMTD plan ends in a braking fail-safe. Building its constraints takes 10 to 20 ms for 20 obstacles, and the IPOPT solve takes 100 to 200 ms [V]. **ARMOUR** adds interval bounds on mass and inertia and a robust controller. It says "if a portion of the scene is occluded, then one can treat that portion of the scene as an obstacle" [V] ([ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2301.13308)). Shape choice matters. With 40 obstacles at the higher acceleration bound, sphere-based SPARROWS succeeded on 40 of 100 problems against 0 of 100 for zonotope ARMTD, and planned in 0.20 s against 0.45 s [V] ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.08857v1)). CROWS uses conformal prediction, but on its neural network's error, not on perception error [V]. **WAITR** (2309.03111) is the closest rival on payloads. It covers uncertainty in "the object dynamics, and contact parameters such as the coefficient of friction" [V] ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.03111)). Per a snippet, it bounds the contact wrench with polynomial zonotopes to prevent sliding and tipping of objects on a tray [S]. Its full text must be read before any claim about payload constraints. The only perception-built model in the family is SPLANNING, which bounds collision risk against a Gaussian splat, not zonotopes [V meta, S content] ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.16915v1)).

**Language-to-set work exists, but with smooth shapes and no physics.** **Brunke et al.** (2410.15185, Schoellig lab) is the closest precedent. An LLM outputs pairs such as (laptop, above). Each pair becomes a **superquadric** unsafe set fitted to the object's segmented point cloud. For "above", they copy the cloud, move the copy's height out of the workspace, and fit the union. They support 12 relations and handle nonconvex objects as unions of superquadrics. The filter runs at **45 Hz** on a Franka FR3 [V] ([arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.15185)). Two limits matter for us. The sets constrain the end-effector point only. And their "cup of water" rotation rule is linearized each step and then **softened into a cost**, switched on by the LLM [V]. **VLSA** (2512.11891) has a VLM pick one critical obstacle and fits a minimum-volume ellipsoid to its point cloud, for a CBF-QP at 0.356 ms per step. It constrains only the end-effector and has no perception-uncertainty model [V] ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.11891v2)). **VLPSA** (2609.22462) builds Poisson safety functions from perception over the full body *and the grasped object*. On SafeLIBERO it raised collision avoidance from 23.1% to 91.2% [V abstract] ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22462)). The only bimanual VLM-to-MPC hit, GenerativeMPC, outputs scalar speed limits and margins, not sets [S] ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.19522v1)).

**The research threads conflict on one point, and it narrows the novelty.** The set-representation thread found no work where language produces zonotope-type sets for manipulation. The decomposition thread found two. **CompCPZ** (2608.17717) composes "per-primitive constrained polynomial zonotope enclosures along the language parse tree" with conformal coverage, for language-guided manipulation [V abstract] ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17717)). **Dwedar et al.** (2609.27816) map VLM output to "inflated obstacle sets, safe corridors, and goal regions" checked by a "zonotope reachability gate", for multiple robots [V abstract; date conflict in metadata] ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27816)). Both were read at abstract level only. The safe reading is that **"language to zonotopes" is taken**. The open part is a *physical* parameter prior made into a calibrated set, combined with perceived geometry and payload dynamics, enforced in real time, and shrunk online.

**From point cloud to QP rows.** CoACD needs a mesh, not a partial point cloud [S] ([GitHub](https://github.com/sarahweiii/CoACD)). For live clouds, the working options are superquadric fits (EMS, SuperDec), minimum-volume ellipsoids, or convex hulls of segmented points [S]. C-IRIS certifies collision-free polytopes in configuration space and was shown on **12-DoF bimanual** arms, but it builds them offline [V] ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12219v2)). We found no pipeline that goes from a point cloud to a zonotope to a manipulator constraint. None of the pipelines carries fit uncertainty into the safety margin. Linear rows are easy to keep. For a zonotope with centre c and generator matrix G, the **support function** (how far the set reaches in direction n) is nᵀc + ‖Gᵀn‖₁. This is a standard identity that the notes did not re-source [I]. Fix n each step, for example as last step's closest-point normal. The clearance row then stays affine in position and linear in FL-MPC's acceleration variables. Liu, Huang and Belta build such rows from supporting hyperplanes with millisecond solves [V abstract] ([arXiv 2603.05916](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.05916)). A 2025 set-based CBF turns a zonotopic invariant set into a barrier inside one QP, with a worst-case time of **28.04 µs** [V] ([arXiv 2507.07805](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.07805)). Rotation is the hard part. A rotated zonotope stays a zonotope only for a fixed rotation. ARMTD handles rotation exactly, but the result is nonsmooth and polynomial and needs IPOPT [V]. Brunke linearizes it and then softens it [V]. Brunke's extrusion trick also gives zonotope relation sets for free: "above(laptop)" is the laptop's zonotope plus one vertical segment generator [I]. The project already has this layer in code. `sets/ir.py` compiles relations such as Inside, Upright, Clear and Support into margins and linear rows through support functions, and `sets/reach.py` builds the ball's reachable set for F5. Neither has results yet [P] ([ir.py](/common/users/ss5772/bimanual/world_model/v2/tray_rep/sets/ir.py); [reach.py](/common/users/ss5772/bimanual/world_model/v2/tray_rep/sets/reach.py)).

## Split shapes where a zonotope wastes volume, not where edges are sharp

**Polynomial annihilation (PA)** is an edge detector for scattered samples. It takes m+1 nearby samples and weights them so that every polynomial of degree below m cancels. The output is near zero where the function is smooth and near the jump size at a jump. A "minmod" over several orders damps oscillations and helps tell jumps from steep slopes [V operator, via Glaubitz & Gelb] ([arXiv 1903.03844](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1903.03844)). The original paper is Archibald, Gelb and Yoon (SIAM J. Numer. Anal., 2005) [S] ([ORNL listing](https://www.ornl.gov/publication/polynomial-fitting-edge-detection-irregularly-sampled-signals-and-images)). Later work extends it to kinks, meaning jumps in the derivative [S]. PA is used in uncertainty-quantification surrogates, shock capturing and imaging. **We found no use of PA on point clouds, robot geometry or safe sets.** The nearest use is Gorodetsky and Marzouk. They seed labels with PA, then refine the boundary with an active-learning SVM, in up to 11 dimensions [V] ([arXiv 1402.2845](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.2845)). In many dimensions PA gets expensive. Its tensor stencils make "the number of function evaluations grow exponentially" [V] ([Zhang et al.](https://people.math.sc.edu/Burkardt/publications/zwgb_2015.pdf)).

**The pushback holds: split on fit error, not on edges [I].** A box has sharp edges, so an edge detector fires on it. Yet a box is a perfect zonotope and needs no split. A smooth bowl has no edges, so the detector stays silent. Yet a bowl is far from convex and must be split. Zonotopes add one more demand: they are centrally symmetric. So a convex but lopsided piece, such as a wedge or cone, still wastes volume. A good split score therefore measures over-coverage directly. Use the zonotope's excess volume over the piece, or the excess clearance in the directions the QP constraints use. Concavity and fit error are the right signals, and raw geometry is not. Althoff's reachability work already splits by error. It halves the generator whose two halves most reduce the linearization error [V] ([CDC 2008](https://skoge.folk.ntnu.no/prost/proceedings/cdc-2008/data/papers/0299.pdf)). Swap "linearization error" for "fit excess" and the same machinery applies. High-order differences also amplify depth-camera noise, so only low orders are usable on raw ZED clouds [I].

**PA earns its place in two narrower jobs [I].** The first is 1D profiles along candidate cut planes: cross-section width, hull defect, or fit residual as a function of offset. Kinks in such a profile propose plane offsets that can prune CoACD's search. The second is boundaries in *state or parameter* space. The BOED drop table over speed and rolling resistance has a drop/no-drop boundary, and P3 failed exactly at it [P]. That is the Gorodetsky–Marzouk setting. **The VLM's job is to say where precision matters, not where to cut [I].** Per-part tolerances should be tight at the gripper, rim and handle and loose on the far side. Empart shows the value of per-region tolerances, set by a human: 69% less simulation time in pick-and-place [V] ([arXiv 2509.22847](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22847)). Part segmenters such as PartField give region seeds with fuzzy boundaries [S] ([arXiv 2504.11451](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.11451)). Learned convex decomposition (CVPR 2026) is purely geometric [V] ([arXiv 2603.09285](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09285)). No published method joins VLM parts, a geometric split detector and per-piece zonotopes. For this paper that is a component, not the headline.

## Eight risks could sink the paper

| Risk | Why it bites here | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **VLM calibration needs a calibration set** | Conformal coverage needs held-out objects with *measured* parameters, drawn like the test objects. Phys2Real fell back on self-reports because answer spread was poor [V]. No such set exists for rolling resistance, and a sim ball's value is invisible in a render. | Build a small labelled set (sim objects plus a few measured real ones). Report coverage. Let the VLM widen sets, never certify them. |
| **Unobservable parameters keep the set wide** | A 0.12 rad probe tilt cannot start a ball above μ ≈ 0.0036, so it reads "sticky", not "how sticky". Balls at μ ≤ 0.001 drop in 34 to 83% of carries at *every* speed. P1 and P6 both fell back to the slowest speed [P] ([boed README](/common/users/ss5772/bimanual/world_model/v2/tray_rep/boed/README.md); [RESULT_BOED](/common/users/ss5772/bimanual_data/tray_rep/boed/RESULT_BOED.md)). | Report results by parameter bin. For these bins, claim speed, not safety. Do not promise the set will shrink. |
| **Wrapping effect and conservatism** | The wrapping effect: over-approximations grow with every step of a nonlinear rollout. The ball also switches between sticking and rolling. Zonotope ARMTD solved 0 of 100 problems where spheres solved 40 [V]. A wide set freezes the carry. | F5 already uses the hull of rollouts at low, middle and high μ plus a stopping bound, not a plain box [P]. Keep horizons short and measure path progress, not drops alone. |
| **Rotation is nonlinear** | A rotated zonotope stays a zonotope only for a fixed rotation. ARMTD's exact treatment needs IPOPT at 100 to 200 ms [V]. F5 linearizes only the centre in the first command [P]. | Linearize each step with an error buffer that is checked on VAL replays. Say plainly that the guarantee holds per step. |
| **Piper's ±8 Nm cap** | Out-of-range feed-forward commands are *dropped*. Gravity on j1/j2 is about 7.35 Nm at the grasp pose, which leaves about 0.6 Nm, and a payload adds more. Shrinking FL-MPC's torque box to ±8 made 10% of QP steps infeasible [P]. A brake-to-stop set must hold gravity and brake inside the box, so it barely exists. Position-offset encoding moves the limit to the motor's peak torque, which is unknown [P]. | Claim the terminal set in sim only until someone measures the motor peak. Hardware work needs the user's explicit approval in that session. Consider a "slow down" terminal set instead of a full stop. |
| **The arms block the camera** | The torso ZED mount is proposed, not confirmed (AGENTS.md). Both arms sit between camera and tray. During track loss the ball's set must grow at its top speed and soon covers the tray. ARMOUR treats occluded space as an obstacle [V]. The earlier report found no calibrated filter that grows its set during track loss. | Grow sets during dropouts. The tracker model already has 2% dropout and 60 ms latency [P]. Report how often the filter freezes. Test camera placement in sim. |
| **The probe certificate is optimistic** | The BOED certificate runs a shadow copy from the *true* MuJoCo state. A robot cannot do that [P]. | Certify from the set: run F5's reachable set at the set's worst μ, from the tracker estimate (Experiment C). |
| **Scoop** | The Alanwar group (Hafez et al., CompCPZ, Dwedar et al.) is moving fast on language → zonotope reachability. Phys2Real lists conformal calibration as its next step. | Lead with the failure analysis and the payload dynamics, not with the set language. Re-check arXiv before submission. |

## Three experiments that could kill the idea

All three run on the tray-and-ball testbed in the bridge harness. They use the tracked setup (`piper_trk`: 3 mm noise, 60 ms latency, 30 Hz, 2% dropout), 600 TEST carries and 3 value seeds. They are CPU-only, offline, and pre-registered before TEST is touched, as BOED was. The rolling resistance μ is hidden and drawn log-uniform in [0.0005, 0.006] m per carry [P] ([bridge README](/common/users/ss5772/bimanual/world_model/v2/tray_rep/bridge/README.md)).

### A. Is the analytic set filter good enough to skip HJ?

This is the gate. Run F5 with the full prior range of μ, the tracker's covariance, and a model-error box calibrated on VAL. Compare it with the bridge's existing arms on drops, intervention rate, normalized deviation and path progress. The existing (not pre-registered) TEST numbers set the bar [P] ([RESULT_bridge_trk](/common/users/ss5772/bimanual_data/tray_rep/bridge/RESULT_bridge_trk.md)). No filter (F0) drops 36.5%. The SafeBimanual-style keypoint cost (F1) drops 24.3%. The learned latent-HJ filter F3 (Rc3k) drops **13.6% at deviation 0.061**. The hand rule F2 drops 1.7% but overrides 67% of ticks at deviation 0.467. The oracle F4 drops **1.0% at deviation 0.114**. **Prediction:** F5 drops at most 13.6% at deviation ≤ 0.12, with path progress no worse than F3 and a cost per tick under the 20 ms tick (F3 needs about 8 ms). **Kill:** if F5's drop rate exceeds F3's with a confidence interval that excludes zero, or it matches F3 only by freezing (progress below F2's), then analytic sets are not enough. HJ comes back, on the small ball subsystem. Two free extras come with this run. First, run F5 once with the *uncalibrated* friction map: MuJoCo's effective rolling friction is 0.6 to 0.87 of μ, and the raw model is 2.6 mm rms off [P]. That is a built-in sim-to-sim gap, a small version of the sim-to-real gap. Second, report drops by μ bin, so that balls no filter can save do not hide the effect.

### B. Does a calibrated set beat a confident point prior?

This is the headline, and it tests the problem statement directly. A VLM cannot see μ in a render, so the "VLM prior" must be synthetic and its error controlled. For each carry, draw a log-normal prior whose centre is off by a heavy-tailed factor. A share ρ of the draws are "confidently wrong": far off, with a small σ. Then run five arms, all with F5. **(1) Point:** Phys2Real-style inverse-variance fusion of the prior and the online estimate, with F5 run at the fused value. **(2) ±2σ band** from the self-reported σ. **(3) Conformal set** calibrated on VAL carries. **(4) Uninformed set:** the full population range. **(5) Oracle μ.** Sweep ρ over {0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.4}. **Prediction:** as ρ rises, arms 1 and 2 drop more than arm 3, by at least 3 points at ρ = 0.2. Arm 3 keeps its stated coverage on TEST and makes more path progress than arm 4. **Kill:** if arm 3 is not safer than arm 1 at ρ = 0.2 (CI includes zero), then "sets beat points" fails on this testbed. If arm 3's progress is within 2% of arm 4's, the VLM prior adds nothing once it is calibrated, and the paper's prior step is dead weight. In both cases, report it. This experiment is synthetic by design. It tests the mechanism, not a real VLM. A real VLM test needs real balls with measured μ.

### C. Does a worst-case speed rule turn information into speed without drops?

This re-runs the BOED kill test with one change: the decision rule. BOED's P3 picked the fastest speed whose *posterior-predicted* drop rate was at most 2%, and it lost 6.2 points of drops [P]. Add arm **P7**. It probes at pickup, then picks the fastest speed whose drop rate at the **lowest μ in the posterior's 95% credible set** is at most 2%, with F5 on. Its probe certificate is also set-based: F5's reachable set at the set's worst μ, from the tracker estimate, not the true state. Add arm **P8**, which runs no probe. It shrinks the set only from the carry's own motion, and re-picks speed as the set shrinks. Compare both with P6 (best single speed, 26.7% drops, 19.2 s) and P5 (oracle, 26.5%, 14.3 s) on the frozen BOED protocol. **Prediction:** P7 drops at most 1 point more than P6, with a time ratio of 0.90 or less against P6, which captures at least 40% of the oracle's 26% gain. P8 captures less time but costs no probe. **Kill:** if P7 drops more than P6 by over 2 points (P3's failure repeats), or saves under 5% time, then shrinking the set does not buy speed safely here. The step "the set shrinks online" would then have no support. The hard ceilings are known in advance. Low-μ balls cannot be made safe, and high-μ balls cannot be identified [P]. So the gain can only come from the middle band, where P3 failed.

## Conclusion

The literature changes the paper's centre of gravity. The novelty is not a set language, since language → zonotopes now exists. It is not a VLM prior with online adaptation, since Phys2Real has that. And it is not HJ, which this problem does not need unless F5 fails. The novelty is a **measured failure and its fix**. VLM physical priors fail by being confidently wrong. Acting on a point or an average risk turns that error into drops. A worst case over a calibrated set holds safety constant. The project's own BOED result already supplies half the evidence: the belief was calibrated, and the *decision rule* still lost. That suggests the paper's sharpest sentence is about decisions, not estimates. Safety must come from the set's worst case, and information is worth only the speed it frees up.

Two things follow. First, the VLM's value in this paper is economic, not protective. A good prior makes the starting set small enough to move fast. A bad prior, once calibrated, makes the set wider and the robot slower, but not unsafe. That framing is honest, and none of the cited works makes it. Second, hardware is the weak link, not theory. A brake-to-stop terminal set on a Piper with about 0.6 Nm of shoulder headroom, and a tray seen past two arms, are where the guarantees thin out. Measure the motor's peak torque and the occlusion rate early, with the user's approval for any hardware contact. Those two numbers decide whether "real-time bimanual" in the title is earned.
