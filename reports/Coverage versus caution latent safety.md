# Prior art owns the slogan, not the measurement

**Our reframed claim is partly novel, and the novel part is narrower than the slogan.** No paper found in four parallel literature sweeps (latent HJ filters, empirical offline RL and safe RL, coverage theory, data-side fixes) scoops the full claim: that for a learned discounted HJ safety value trained on nominal data, five deployment-time cautions fail to repair the greedy fallback while off-policy counterfactual action data does, with the data-selection rule mattering little. But each ingredient is already published somewhere. The mechanism (a max over actions exploits value or model error on unsupported actions) is stated by UNISafe, LatentCBF, FISOR and RAMBO. The slogan "conservatism alone cannot replace data" is stated almost verbatim by UNISafe, RECON and PROCO. And as a principle it is a near-corollary of offline-RL partial-coverage theory: pessimism competes only with covered comparators. What no one has published is the head-to-head: a pre-registered, quantified ladder (78.6% / 39.7% / 20.8% / 10-23% / 1-3% failure on physics-savable states) that isolates the max-over-actions fallback as the failure source and compares caution to coverage on one value. Literature also contradicts parts of our framing: UNISafe shows pessimism and data acting as substitutes at low data, FISOR and V-OCBF report in-support restriction suffices on benchmarks, and a 2026 boundary-sampling paper finds directed selection beats uniform. We should claim the measurement and a scoped instantiation of the coverage barrier, not the idea, and should state "did not, in the forms tested" rather than "cannot." The planned confirming-falsifier arm fits well with published CEGIS/adversary practice, but the literature warns about three things it must control: set shrinkage, matched true-simulator budget, and leakage between falsifier and test states.

## The mechanism and the slogan are established; the quantified head-to-head is open

The diagnosis that a learned latent value is over-optimistic where nominal data is thin is common ground. UNISafe reports that latent safety filters "may miss novel hazards and even fail to prevent known ones, overconfidently misclassifying risky out-of-distribution (OOD) situations as safe" ([UNISafe, arXiv 2505.00779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00779)). LatentCBF comes closest to the unseen-action mechanism, noting that RL critics trained solely on safety-policy data "yield inaccurate value estimates for nominal policy actions, precisely where CBF filtering needs them", and that conservative critic updates "maintain high-performance without addressing the inaccuracies of the critic itself" ([LatentCBF, arXiv 2511.18606](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.18606)). In offline safe RL, FISOR states the same exploitation structure for an HJ-style value, where the min over next actions "could query the out-of-distribution (OOD) actions, potentially leading to severe underestimation" ([FISOR, arXiv 2401.10700](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10700)). Offline model-based RL stated it earlier still: RAMBO warns that when data does not cover the state-action space, "naive policy optimisation on a learnt model ... can result in model exploitation" ([RAMBO, arXiv 2204.12581](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.12581)). Novelty therefore cannot rest on the diagnosis.

The "caution is not coverage" slogan also exists. UNISafe finds that "offline RL with conservative losses performs even worse than LatentSafe, indicating that conservatism alone cannot replace failure set identification", though that concerns training-time CQL/COMBO conservatism rather than deployment-time caution ([arXiv 2505.00779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00779)). RECON, posted 27 September 2026, is the single nearest statement: "Conservative model use is not sufficient for model acquisition," because a conservative policy leaves uncertain regions "insufficiently sampled" ([RECON, arXiv 2609.33336](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33336)); it is model-based imitation learning, not a safety value, and it uses an explorer policy rather than off-policy counterfactual branches. PROCO (May 2026) is the nearest in vocabulary: offline safe RL with an HJ-feasibility Bellman form, arguing that existing conservative methods "treat all samples equally safe and overlook safe-yet-infeasible states", and fixing it with model-generated counterfactual unsafe samples that reportedly improve safety by over 400% on safe-only data ([PROCO, arXiv 2605.01356](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01356)). PROCO's counterfactuals come from the learned model (so exposed to the same exploitation we diagnose), its states are low-dimensional, and it never equalizes caution strength against data amount. In generic offline RL, "change the data, not the algorithm" is folklore for task return: vanilla TD3 on diverse exploratory data matches or beats offline-specific algorithms ([ExORL, arXiv 2201.13425](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.13425)), OGBench shows performance falling from 99% to 6% when expert actions carry no noise ([OGBench, arXiv 2410.20092](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.20092)), and for learned world models, expert-only data induces an imagination-versus-reality mismatch that exploration data repairs ([Chen et al., arXiv 2509.05735](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05735)). None of these touches a safety value.

Theory makes the bare claim a corollary, and a theory-minded reviewer will say so. Every pessimism guarantee has the form "competes with the best policy the data covers": Jin et al. call the learned policy "the 'best effort' among all policies" given the data ([arXiv 2012.15085](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.15085)), and Rashidinejad et al.'s minimax bound is meaningful only for finite single-policy concentrability ([arXiv 2103.12021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.12021)). MOReL's Proposition 4 gives a lower bound where a small fraction of optimal-policy occupancy on unseen state-actions costs any learner a suboptimality amplified by 1/(1-gamma)^2 ([MOReL, arXiv 2005.05951](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.05951)). The separation we observe between DAgger-style and counterfactual data is predicted by Xiao et al., who prove that policy-induced data costs A^min(S-1,H) episodes while state-action sampling is polynomial ([arXiv 2106.09973](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09973)), and by Ross and Bagnell, whose guarantee holds only against policies close to a separate exploration distribution ([arXiv 1203.1007](https://arxiv.org/abs/1203.1007)). Our own reading (an inference, not a published result) adds a safety-specific twist worth stating as a proposition: a filter fires exactly where nominal behaviour fails, so the usual "pessimism falls back to the behaviour policy" floor is the unsafe floor, and nominal data avoids the evasive region by construction. No source states this for filters or HJ values, and no theorem covers error propagation through the discounted max-min operator from offline data; both gaps are real openings but should be labelled as our derivation.

The closest-prior-art register, ordered by scoop risk, is below.

## Contradicting evidence says pessimism sometimes works, and directed data sometimes wins

The literature is not one-sided, and a hostile reviewer will assemble the following. First, UNISafe's own data-size sweep points the opposite way on its metric: balanced accuracy of the monitor is 0.50 versus 0.84 at zero random trajectories for LatentSafe versus UNISafe, but 0.97 versus 0.97 at 500 and 1000, so pessimism closes the gap at low data and data alone matches it later ([arXiv 2505.00779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00779)). Our result is distinguishable on three counts: their added data are random trajectories rather than evasive counterfactual branches, their metric is monitor accuracy on a grid rather than closed-loop fallback success on savable states, and their pessimism is training-time reachability over an augmented OOD failure set rather than deployment-time caution on a nominal-trained value. Second, LatentSafe's own ablation, with a world model trained on only two actions, finds missing action coverage makes the filter conservative: it "pessimistically classifies states as unsafe if they require a right turn to avoid collision" ([LatentSafe, arXiv 2502.00935](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00935)). That is the opposite sign to our 79% over-optimistic failure and must be reconciled explicitly, most plausibly as a hard-coded two-action set versus a learned continuous extrapolation.

Third, a body of offline safe RL reports that in-support pessimism suffices. Survival Instinct argues pessimism gives an incentive to stay within data support and that "offline RL is safe as long as the data only contains safe states" ([NeurIPS 2023](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/c3e969ea20542a6a11e6caeac736a0b9-Paper-Conference.pdf)); FISOR, V-OCBF and SafeFQL restrict critic learning to dataset-supported actions and claim fewer violations ([V-OCBF, arXiv 2512.10822](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.10822); [SafeFQL, arXiv 2603.15136](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15136)); an ensemble-UCB cost critic reportedly keeps cost limits across DSRL tasks ([arXiv 2502.12391](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12391)). The likeliest reconciliation, which we should state and ideally test, is that those benchmarks' data contain violating trajectories or safe-in-support behaviour, whereas a filter must act outside the nominal support, where in-support restriction has no evasive action to choose. Model-based offline RL is likewise split: COMBO finds ensemble uncertainty "is not accurate" ([arXiv 2102.08363](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08363)), while Lu et al. find larger uncertainty penalties improve D4RL returns ([arXiv 2110.04135](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04135)) and a real-robot study reports penalties working ([arXiv 2504.16680](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16680)). Our 40%-failure ensemble arm lands in a contested literature. A mechanistic reason it may be structurally weak in latent models: ensemble disagreement need not track compounding RSSM error, because rollouts are drawn "toward well-supported latent regions, where uncertainty diminishes despite increasing discrepancies" ([Berger et al., arXiv 2604.25416](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25416)).

Fourth, theory carries a scope warning. Lower bounds are worst-case over model classes, and a smooth pendulum-like proxy with a model that extrapolates action effects could in principle succeed. Safe-learning theory (SafeOpt, Berkenkamp et al.) shows structural priors such as Lipschitz or RKHS regularity can substitute for coverage given a safe seed ([Berkenkamp et al., arXiv 1705.08551](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08551)); Foster et al. add that coverage plus realizability may still not suffice for generic function classes ([arXiv 2111.10919](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.10919)). So "counterfactual data fixes it" is consistent with theory, not implied by it, and any necessity statement must be relative to the model class.

## Directed versus random selection: the null is consistent with some work and in tension with the best-matched precedent

Our selection-rule null is the weakest-supported part of the claim. No paper tests random versus uncertainty-directed versus value-directed counterfactual action data for a safety value. The nearest direct precedent is adverse: Dallas et al. find that boundary sampling for a learned safety value reduces failure "from 0.66 to 0.3 (2-layer) and from 0.50 to 0.45 (3-layer)" at 30k epochs, though the gap narrows with longer training and the sampler uses known dynamics and samples states, not actions ([arXiv 2604.13325](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.13325)). RECON likewise finds gated, directed collection beats both random and ungated uncertainty collection ([arXiv 2609.33336](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33336)). On the other side, several results predict a null: Ross and Bagnell's DAgger-for-models is "more robust to the choice of nu" than batch collection ([arXiv 1203.1007](https://arxiv.org/abs/1203.1007)); counterexample-guided augmentation in vision finds uniform, Halton, cross-entropy and distance-constrained generators within a point or two of each other, all beating classical augmentation ([Dreossi et al., arXiv 1805.06962](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.06962)); RENEW reports uniform sampling adequate in small state-action spaces, with ensemble disagreement a noisier estimator in continuous spaces ([arXiv 2607.14180](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14180)); and active learning for linear systems can show small benefit even for an oracle design ([arXiv 2509.11907](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11907)). A pattern runs through both camps: directed selection wins when the selector uses ground-truth-confirmed information (PMP boundaries, recoverability, verifier counterexamples) and loses or ties when it relies on the model's own uncertainty. Our killed arms were model-reliance directed, which fits the null; this is an inference from the pattern, since we did not test a truth-confirmed selector. Theory gives the same qualitative prediction (once coverage of the fallback's relevant region is finite, targeting changes constants, not feasibility), though no theorem says targeting gives no gain.

Two data points of ours need careful phrasing. Random branching at 1k beat the directed arms (6.8% versus about 20%), and random reached 1.0% at 30k while directed arms reached 2.75% and 2.16% at 3k. The safe statement is that, at the budgets tested in this proxy, pre-registered directed selection showed no advantage over random; it should not be generalized to "selection does not matter," and the notes recommend a branch-count curve showing where directed and random converge, since Dallas et al. suggest the gap is a low-budget phenomenon.

## Conformal calibration and predator-prey forms help only if they see the fallback's actions

Conformal methods calibrate three different targets, and only one can see a wrong fallback. Latent-error calibration (Cao and Bansal, SLS2) measures world-model error on executed nominal actions. Cao and Bansal concede their Theorem 1 "assumes exchangeability between deployment and calibration trajectories, which can be violated under safety filtering if recovery actions are absent from the calibration set" ([arXiv 2609.34300](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34300)); that is the published reason an ACI-style arm would stay flat at 78.6%, though the mechanism for our result is our inference. Value calibration (Sibai et al. Phase 1) shifts V by a conformal quantile and shows violations do not fall monotonically with added conservatism, because the fallback encounters OOD states under more conservative switching ([arXiv 2511.07899](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07899)). Outcome calibration (Lin and Bansal; Sibai Phase 2) rolls out the fallback on the true system from i.i.d. sampled states and bounds the empirical violation fraction via a Beta distribution ([arXiv 2312.08604](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.08604)). Only this third family can detect a bad fallback, but it needs a simulator or deployment rollouts and detects rather than repairs: it is a verifier of coverage, not a substitute. Weighted conformal under covariate shift needs a likelihood ratio that is unbounded on unsupported actions ([Tibshirani et al., arXiv 1904.06019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06019)); that link to concentrability is our inference. Filter-induced exchangeability breakage is acknowledged by Cao and Bansal, Sibai et al. and SLS2, and treated as the main subject by Lindemann et al., whose remedy is episodic recalibration, effectively on-policy data ([arXiv 2606.15366](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15366)). An outcome-level conformal arm is therefore a strong baseline to add, since a reviewer's best alternative is exactly this one.

Predator-prey forms (ISAACS, Gameplay Filters, Q-CBF, RARL) place an adversary where model error would be. They are set by a designer-chosen uncertainty bound, not calibrated error, and they rely on a runtime forward rollout rather than the learned value, because the value and the learned disturbance are admitted unreliable. Gameplay Filters call the learned reach-avoid critic "often unreliable and/or overly conservative" ([arXiv 2405.00846](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00846)), and ISAACS notes that a suboptimal disturbance policy "may lead us to erroneously conclude that a state is safe" ([arXiv 2212.03228](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.03228)). They beat domain randomization, with Gameplay Filters arguing random disturbance simulation misses worst cases at a rate that falls exponentially in disturbance dimension, but none shows a matched-budget comparison against random counterexamples. Nobody has combined a conformal error bound with the disturbance player of a latent-WM HJ game; Cao and Bansal's minimization over a calibrated latent ball is a one-step version of it. Our reading is that a robust game still needs the world model correct on the fallback's actions, and for game values offline-RL theory demands the stricter unilateral coverage (Cui and Du show single-strategy concentration is insufficient and unilateral concentration necessary for Nash equilibria, [arXiv 2201.03522](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.03522)), which does not apply to a single-agent HJ value.

Keeping these fixes from collapsing into over-conservatism is a documented problem, and the record gives usable rules. The costs are real: UNISafe's best variant intervenes about five times more often (37.7% filtered versus 7.2%) and still fails 20% in block plucking ([arXiv 2505.00779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00779)); ISAACS reports a 20.4% false-unsafe rate ([arXiv 2212.03228](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.03228)); a verifiable Q-filter paper reports zero-sublevel-set shrinkage in counterexample-guided fine-tuning ([arXiv 2506.15693](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.15693)); and Kim et al. note conformal calibration works "albeit with increased conservativeness" ([arXiv 2510.06492](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06492)). No paper reports a false-intervention multiplier like ours (6.8% for ensemble pessimism against 0.24-0.57% for the value-directed and ACI arms, i.e. roughly 12-28 times). The published mitigations are as follows. Weight training to penalize optimistic errors more than pessimistic ones and use robust scenario variants that trade a sliver of confidence for volume: Lin and Bansal move from 99.999% to 99.974% confidence and raise assured safe volume from 0.56 to 0.81. Let confirmed data buy back permissiveness, as Dyna-SAuR does ("better models expand the set of safe and certain states, reducing filter conservatism", [arXiv 2604.25508](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25508)) and as Cao and Bansal do by starting the pessimism radius at maximum and shrinking it. And treat false intervention and a hold-still comparator as primary metrics, not secondary ones. The structural lesson is that caution and data are complements: caution is a bridge while coverage is thin, and data is what lets the caution relax.

## Framing the claim, and what the literature implies for the confirming-falsifier arm

The safest statement is an instantiation, not a discovery. Something like: in a pre-registered proxy test, a discounted latent HJ value trained on nominal data produced a greedy fallback that spilled on 78.6% of physics-savable states versus 5.6% for holding still; four deployment-time cautions (ACI pessimism, ensemble pessimism, in-data-restricted max, DAgger-style rollouts) left 10-79% failure, while off-policy counterfactual action branches at 3k-10k brought it to 1-3%; and, at the tested budgets, value-directed and uncertainty-directed branch selection did not beat random. This instantiates the partial-coverage barrier for a max-min value, with three guardrails. Do not claim a new theorem or a new diagnosis. Do not say pessimism is known not to work for safety (the in-support camp says otherwise on data that contain violations) or that no one has argued missing unsafe knowledge causes offline safe-RL failure (PROCO, OASIS and FOSP do); say they argue it without testing caution against coverage on one safety value. And disclose the proxy caveat, since ground truth equals the labeller, so the result cannot speak to label noise, to a model class that extrapolates, or to real-sim gaps; three seeds and one proxy make the numbers a kill-test, not a distributional claim. Positioning against RECON, PROCO and UNISafe is mandatory; the differentiators are action-level counterfactual coverage, true-simulator relabelling, closed-loop failure on a savable-state set, five cautions side by side, and a pre-registered null on selection.

For the confirming falsifier, the literature is encouraging but sets conditions. Running the value's greedy fallback on the true simulator, keeping confirmed failures and branching alternative actions from them combines the two properties the evidence says matter: confirmation by true dynamics (as in ISAACS, Gameplay Filters, neural-certificate CEGIS and Dreossi et al.) and off-policy action coverage (the Xiao et al. state-action-sampling regime). It also differs from the killed value-directed arm, which pointed data where the model relied on itself without confirming anything, and from GALILEO's model-exploiting adversary, which reweights existing data with no confirmation ([arXiv 2206.04890](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.04890)). Prior evidence predicts it will match or beat random branching, with gains concentrated at small budgets (Dallas et al. and RECON favour directed selection at low budget; Dreossi et al. find confirmed generators roughly equal). The experiment should therefore be pre-registered with a budget sweep (including the 300-1k region where random beat directed arms) and a stated kill criterion for the claim that confirmation, rather than selection, is what helps. Matched budgets must count the extra true-simulator rollouts the falsifier uses to find failures, otherwise it hides a cost, and a confirmed-random arm (random branches also relabelled by the true simulator) is the necessary control for separating confirmation from selection.

Four risks need explicit controls. First, shrinkage: CEGIS-style loops shrink the certified set, and an arm that harvests only failures biases the data toward the failure region, so report false interventions on held-out non-savable and clearly safe states against the 0.24% reference, and mix in non-failure branches. Second, leakage and exchangeability: falsifier states must be drawn from a pool disjoint from the 1989 test states, or the falsifier will just discover the test set; Lindemann et al. and Sibai et al. show filter-induced shift breaks exchangeability, so iterative predator-prey rounds should be capped and the number of rounds reported. Third, vacuous confirmation: because the proxy's truth equals the labeller, confirmation costs nothing and cannot expose false-confirm risk; the result will not transfer to a setting with a sim-to-real gap or noisy labels, and the claim should say so. Fourth, the fallback's own coverage: failures arise where the greedy action is bad, but a bad alternative-action grid recreates the original coverage hole, so the branched action set must span evasive actions broadly (state-action sampling, not just perturbations of the failed action, in the spirit of DART's contrast between off-policy perturbation and on-policy aggregation, [arXiv 1703.09327](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.09327)). Adding an outcome-conformal arm on the greedy fallback to the same comparison would give the strongest rebuttal to the calibration alternative.

## Conclusion

The literature changes what we should claim more than whether we can claim it. "Caution cannot substitute for coverage" is a recognized idea with theory behind it, so the paper's value is a quantified, pre-registered instance on a latent discounted HJ value that isolates the max-over-actions fallback as the failure and shows that the cautions most likely to be proposed as fixes fail in different ways: ACI pessimism is blind to actions it never calibrated on, ensemble pessimism is both noisy and trigger-happy, and in-data restriction and on-policy aggregation cannot reach evasive actions the nominal data never contains. The selection-rule null is a secondary, budget-scoped observation, and the pattern across the literature suggests it will reappear only for model-reliance selectors, not for truth-confirmed ones.

Implications going forward: the falsifier experiment is where real novelty could still be earned, because the confirmed-versus-model-directed contrast, tested at matched budget, is open in the literature and its outcome is informative either way. Two caveats limit confidence.
