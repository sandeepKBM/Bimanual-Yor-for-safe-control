## 3. Thesis statement

**Progress-preserving certified recovery for bimanual manipulation under parameter uncertainty.**
A motion is admitted only if a certified backup reaches a rest state from which the task can resume (task-ready), for every parameter
value in the current belief. When no progress motion is admissible, the planner chooses a recovery, in order: null-space self-motion →
in-place regrasp → role swap / handover → set-down on a support. A small diagnostic probe is admitted when it is itself certified and
its outcome changes the decision.

**Headline (the one falsifiable claim).** Certified systems whose fallback is "stop" (braking fail-safe: ARMTD, ARMOUR, our earlier
gatekeeper) either freeze or lose task progress in traps. Certifying the backup to a *task-ready* set and choosing a recovery restores
completion with zero violations.
**Decisive test:** the Demo C trap, same scene and skills, n ≥ 20 seeds with 95% CIs, against (a) stop/brake (ARMOUR style),
(b) fixed reset, (c) set-down-only recovery, (d) ours. Falsified if (d) does not complete more often than (a)–(c) at zero violations.

## 8. Positioning (verified 10-26, two independent literature passes)

- Short horizon + fail-safe stop + replan is standard (ARMTD RSS 2020: 1.0 s plans, 0.5 s replans, static obstacles; Althoff group;
  Pek & Althoff T-RO 2021). Our contribution is not the pattern but the **task-ready backup and recovery choice**.
- "Stop is always admitted" is **passive safety** (Fraichard ICRA 2007; Bouraine et al. AuRo 2012). Say so; it is why the mobile case is out.
- PDDLStream assumes a static, deterministic world; SS-Replan (ICRA 2020) replans after each observation and defers motions not needed
  before the next replan — our receding window matches that.
- Robust mass-interval contact constraints and the brake fail-safe are ARMOUR / WAITR's; C3 must be pitched as force closure + mode
  switch (which contacts exist) + realisability, with WAITR's future work being our Demo C.
