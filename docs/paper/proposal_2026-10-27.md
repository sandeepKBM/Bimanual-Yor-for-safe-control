# Thesis Proposal: Certified Recovery Under Parameter Uncertainty

Draft 2, 2026-10-27. Built from Sandeep's draft of 2026-10-26, with the owner review fixes applied. The changes are listed at the end.
Current claims reference: this file plus `claims_2026-10-27.md`.

## Abstract

A two-arm robot often handles objects whose mass, centre of mass or friction it cannot see. Partway through a task the planned motion
can become unsafe, and the right recovery then depends on those hidden values. Recent systems use vision-language models (VLMs) to plan
and to propose recoveries, but they treat the object as known and trust the recovery they pick; if the guess is wrong, the recovery
itself can drop the object. We propose a planner that admits a motion only if, for every parameter value the robot cannot yet rule out,
the motion is safe and one certified way back exists to a resting configuration from which the task can still continue. The VLM
proposes task steps and recovery options, and it may only widen a fixed prior range of the parameters. Joint torque readings narrow
the range; a consistency monitor detects readings that no value in the range explains. When no progress motion is certified, the robot
takes the way back, then any recovery that passes the same check, such as reconfiguring its arms, regrasping, handing the object over or
setting it down, and resumes. We will prove that admitted motions are safe under stated assumptions, and give conditions under which a
recovery always exists. We will show the full fall-back-and-finish cycle on the YOR bimanual mobile manipulator in cooking tasks, where
the robot handles pans and pots whose contents it cannot see.

## Related work

No prior work we found certifies a recovery against uncertain object parameters while keeping the task able to continue.

**Vision-language models in planning.** VIZ-COAST [1] has a VLM extract constraints that rule out task plans before motion refinement
fails. The task plan is found by a Z3 SMT task planner (SATPlan-style); grasps and trajectories come from COAST streams. OWL-TAMP [2] and
VLM-TAMP [3] use VLM constraints and subgoals in the same way. All treat feasibility as known geometry and check it at plan time. Our
certificate sits below such a task layer: it decides whether each step is physically safe over a parameter range.

**Recovery with foundation models.** AgentChord [4] adds recovery branches to a task graph before execution and keeps only branches
whose remaining cost-to-go is below the failure state's, on real bimanual hardware (GPT-5 proposer). Its monitors detect failures
after they happen. Code-as-Monitor [5] and ReKep [6] detect failures or re-solve constraints online. None models uncertainty in the
object, and none checks that a recovery is physically safe before it runs.

**Backup-based safety filters.** Gatekeeper, backup control barrier functions and model predictive shielding [7] admit a motion only
if a backup reaches a known safe set. Van Wijk et al. [8] require a timely return to a set where the task can continue (recurrence);
they prove return, not task completion. Both assume known dynamics and a fixed target set. "Stopping is always admitted" is passive
safety [16].

**Hybrid and belief-space control.** Borquez et al. [17] compute reach-avoid sets over discrete modes with full state. Enwerem et al. [9]
control a hybrid system in belief space and stop when no admissible action remains. Prajapat et al. [10] learn unknown dynamics online
while staying safe and returnable, but only within one connected safe region, on cars and drones. Vahs et al. [11] and Naveed et al. [12]
add information gathering under safety, without manipulation or grasp changes.

**Safe arm planning and grasp selection.** ARMOUR [13] and Zhang et al. [14] plan safely for one arm under payload uncertainty, braking
as the fallback. WAITR [18] adds contact-wrench constraints for an unsecured object over parameter intervals, also braking; changing
contacts is its stated future work. Holladay et al. [15] choose grasps robust to parameter ranges, open-loop.

We combine what these leave apart: a backup that ends where the task can continue, a target set that depends on parameters the robot is
still learning, and recoveries that switch between disconnected safe regions by changing how the object is held or supported.

## Problem formulation

The robot must finish a task while every motion it executes keeps one certified way back to a resting state from which the task can
continue, for all object parameters it cannot yet rule out.

**State and grasp mode.** The state x holds both arm configurations, the base pose and the object pose. A discrete grasp mode σ records
what holds the object: both hands, left only, right only, a support surface, or a hand plus a support.

**Unknown parameters.** The pan is known; its contents are not. We write the contents' inertial parameters as π = (m, s), with mass m
and first moment s = m·c (c = centre of mass). Gravity and inertial loads are linear in π, so every set below stays a polytope and
corner checks stay exact. Friction μ has its own interval, which torque readings do not narrow (friction shows only when slipping).
θ = (π, μ).

**Prior range.** Θ_prior is a fixed engineering range (real-world masses and the pan's footprint). The VLM proposes a range Θ_VLM and
may only widen the prior:

$$\Theta_0 = \operatorname{hull}(\Theta_{\text{prior}} \cup \Theta_{\text{VLM}})$$

So a wrong VLM guess can only remove admissions (Lemma 1); it cannot make the robot less safe.

**Torque update.** With the rigid-body regressor Y (load torques linear in π) and the arm's own model τ_0:

$$\Theta_{t+1} = \Theta_t \cap \{\theta : \lvert \tau_t^{\mathrm{meas}} - \tau_0(q_t,\dot q_t,\ddot q_t) - Y(q_t,\dot q_t,\ddot q_t)\,\pi \rvert \le \varepsilon\}$$

The inequality holds per joint; ε is a per-joint vector that bounds model and sensor error. Readings are used at rest or in slow motion,
or with the full Y; a quasi-static model during fast motion would push the inertia terms into ε.

**Food leaving the pan.** Between scoops, θ is constant. A scoop removes up to Δ of food from anywhere the spatula reaches (region P),
and the spatula may push the rest by up to d_max. After a scoop the range is widened:

$$W(\Theta) = \Theta \oplus \{-\delta\,(1, p) : \delta \in [0, \Delta],\ p \in P\} \oplus \{(0, m_{\max} d) : \lVert d \rVert \le d_{\max}\}$$

(⊕ adds every point of the right-hand set to every point of the range.) The redistribution term matters: pushing food toward the far
rim raises the twist on the handle. Both added sets are convex, so W keeps a polytope a polytope.

**Consistency monitor.** If no value in Θ_t explains a reading (the update would leave the set empty), an assumption has failed. The
robot counts the event, runs the guard backup b_G and sets Θ ← Θ_G, a wider fallback range. The guard step is certified only if b_G
was checked for Θ_G when the current motion was admitted; otherwise it is an uncertified, counted event. The monitor catches failures
that show up in the readings, not all of them: an ε that is too small can exclude the true value while the set stays non-empty.
(Open: decision 3, a calibrated ε with the monitor only reporting; see Open decisions.)

**Constraints.** g(x, u, θ) ≤ 0 collects collision, joint and torque limits, a bound f_s,max on the spatula contact force, a base
acceleration limit a_max during transport, and the grasp condition. The grasp condition uses a contact model that is validated against
contact physics: in our box study, "some balancing force exists" admitted 2 falling one-hand holds on a fresh test set; the compliant
model admitted none. Contact dynamics and slip over time are not modelled; the grasp is checked quasi-statically with a margin. For a
trajectory φ, g(φ, θ) ≤ 0 means at every time along it.

**Rest set.** S(Θ) is the set of states at rest where holding still is safe for every θ in Θ: the object on a support, or a static
hold that passes the grasp condition. Since θ does not change at rest, such a state is safe indefinitely.

**Safe admission.** A motion a from x is safely admitted under Θ if one backup b works for every θ:

$$\mathrm{Adm}_S(a \mid x, \Theta) \iff \exists b\ \forall \theta \in \Theta:\ g(\phi_a(x,\theta),\theta) \le 0 \ \wedge\ g(\phi_b(x_a(\theta),\theta),\theta) \le 0 \ \wedge\ x_b(\theta) \in S(\Theta^+)$$

Here x_a(θ) and x_b(θ) are the end states of a and of b, and Θ⁺ = W(Θ) if a removes food, else Θ. The order ∃b ∀θ matters: the backup
cannot depend on the value the robot does not know.

**Task-ready set.** R(Θ) is the set of rest states from which a finite sequence of safely admitted actions reaches the next subgoal:

$$R(\Theta) = \{x \in S(\Theta) : \exists a_1,\dots,a_n \text{ reaching the next subgoal},\ \mathrm{Adm}_S(a_i \mid x_{i-1}, \Theta_{i-1}) \ \forall i\}$$

with Θ_i the range after a_i (widened if a_i removes food). In practice the planner computes R_K, which uses sequences of length at most K.
R uses only safe admission, so the definition is not circular. R depends on Θ: a smaller range gives a larger R.

**Admission rule.** A motion is executed only if it is safely admitted and its backup lands where the task can continue:

$$\mathrm{Adm}(a \mid x, \Theta_t) \iff \mathrm{Adm}_S(a \mid x, \Theta_t) \text{ with } x_b(\theta) \in S(\Theta^+) \cap R(\Theta^+)\ \forall \theta \in \Theta_t$$

**Recovery.** If no progress motion is admitted, the robot runs the committed backup and lands in S ∩ R for its current range, which
has only narrowed since admission (Lemma 1). Membership in R means a certified sequence to the next subgoal exists; the recovery is
its first step. A recovery can change anything the robot controls: arm configuration, grasp mode σ → σ′, support surface, approach or
base pose. Examples are null-space reconfiguration, regrasp, handover and set-down. Each passes the same admission rule before it runs.
A recovery is never retried from the same (state, range); a subgoal gets at most K recovery attempts.

**Probing.** A probe is an action that is itself admitted and whose readings add rows to the torque update. The robot probes only when
the admission verdict for the next motion differs across Θ_t and the probe's readings can separate those values. A null-space elbow
motion with the object held still does **not** qualify: the load at the grip is unchanged, so it adds no information about π (it only
averages noise). The pan probe is a pivot lift: the claw raises the handle a few millimetres while the far rim stays on the stove.
The pan is then a beam on two supports, so the claw carries only a vertical force F and almost no twist, which is safe even for a
weak claw. Each pivot reading gives one linear constraint on π, F·L = g·(m_tot·x_rim − s_tot,x), with lever L from claw to rim and
m_tot, s_tot the totals for pan plus contents.

**Roles.** The VLM proposes subgoals, recovery options, support surfaces and the range Θ_VLM. Classical planners generate motions. The
admission rule decides what runs.

## Assumptions

- A1. The true θ* lies in Θ_prior.
- A2. At the readings used, model and sensor error stay within ε (per joint).
- A3. θ changes only by modelled removals and redistribution, bounded by W.
- A4. Motion is quasi-static up to the acceleration bounds included in g (a_max for the base, the planned arm accelerations).
- A5. The grasp condition is conservative relative to the true contacts (validated in contact simulation before hardware).

## Claims

Three theoretical claims and two experimental ones.

1. **Soundness.** Under A1–A5, θ* ∈ Θ_t for all t (by induction through the updates and widenings), so every executed motion satisfies
   the constraints.
2. **Recovery exists.** We state conditions under which the start state lies in R_K(Θ_0) and every certified landing state stays in
   R_K. This covers moves between disconnected safe regions (one-claw hold vs two-arm support vs on the stove), which continuous safe
   exploration cannot reach [10].
3. **Completion or certified refusal.** With finitely many subgoals, finitely many proposed options, the no-retry rule and bounded
   probing, the robot either finishes the task or reports a certificate that no proposed option is admitted for the current range.
   This is completeness relative to the proposed options and the planner's depth K, not in general.
4. **Fall back and finish, on hardware.** On YOR the robot takes its backup when the next step proves unsafe, lands in a task-ready
   state, recovers and completes the task, with no drops.
5. **Probing pays only when it changes the decision.** The robot probes only when the verdict differs across Θ_t; compared with no
   probe and with always probing, this saves unnecessary set-downs without adding violations. Learning only what the task needs
   follows dual control [10, 12]; the contribution is choosing a certified probe (here, a supported pivot) for a grasp decision.

**Lemma 1 (supporting, not a headline claim).** Widening Θ can only remove admissions, and narrowing can only add them; R is
antitone in Θ. Hence between removals a backup certified for Θ_t stays certified as the robot learns, and a VLM that only widens
cannot make the robot less safe. Prajapat et al. [10] prove a related result for growing safe sets. The lemma fails when A2 or A3
fails, which is why it is paired with the consistency monitor.

**Not claimed.** The quality of the VLM's proposals; contact dynamics or slip over time; completeness beyond the proposed options;
detection of every assumption failure; the backup idea or the monotonicity lemma on their own.

## Demonstrations

The main demonstration is serving from a pan: one claw holds the pan by its handle while the other arm uses a spatula. It exercises
all five claims and the consistency monitor. A second task, moving a pot into the oven, adds the mobile base.

### Main: serve from a pan

**Task.** A pan with solid food sits on the stove. The robot lifts it by the handle with one claw and serves with a spatula held by the
other arm. The food's amount and position are hidden, and they set how hard the pan twists around its handle.

**The cycle.**

1. **Pivot probe.** The claw raises the handle a few millimetres while the far rim stays on the stove. Admitted for every θ in Θ_0,
   because the stove carries part of the load and the claw carries no twist. The readings narrow where the food sits.
2. **Decide.** If the one-claw hold plus the spatula force is certified for the narrowed range, the robot lifts and serves. Before
   each scoop, the scoop is admitted against the widened range W(Θ).
3. **Fall back.** If the next step is not certified (food far out, or the range widened by a scoop), the robot runs the backup: it sets
   the pan back on the stove.
4. **Land task-ready.** Pan supported, both arms free, range narrowed. The state is in R because a certified sequence to "served" exists.
5. **Recover.** Regrasp the handle closer to the pan and lift, or serve with the pan resting on the stove while the claw arm steadies
   the rim.
6. **Serve.**

Food amount and position are randomized each trial, so in some trials the robot serves straight away and in others the fallback fires.
The admission rule decides, never a script.

| Claim | Use in the pan task |
|---|---|
| 1. Soundness | Every pivot, lift, hold, scoop and set-down is checked for every food amount and position still possible |
| 2. Recovery exists | Set down, regrasp closer, or steady the rim; one-claw hold, rim-steadied pan on the stove and stove support are separate safe regions |
| 3. Completion or certified refusal | Finite recovery options with the no-retry rule; if none is certified, the robot reports it |
| 4. Fall back and finish | Lift, find the food far out, set down, regrasp or steady, serve |
| 5. Probe only when it matters | The pivot lift runs only when the hold verdict differs across the range |
| Consistency monitor | If food shifts beyond the redistribution bound and readings stop matching, the event is counted and the pan goes back on the stove |

**Making caution costly.** The only free surface near the stove is the stove itself, and a planner that always steadies the pan with
both arms serves more slowly. We therefore report unnecessary set-downs and time as well as drops.

**Sizing (must be done first).** Pan weight, handle length and the food range must be chosen so that a one-claw hold plus spatula force
is certified in some trials and not in others, and the pivot probe is certified in all of them. This needs the claw's grip force and
opening and the NERO payload at reach. A rough estimate with the 10 N claw in the model gives a twist capacity of about 0.4–0.8 Nm,
against about 2–3 Nm for a 1 kg pan with 0.3 kg of food, so a small, light pan is needed unless the real claw is stronger.

### Second: move a pot into the oven (uses the base)

A lidded pot with two side handles and solid contents goes from the stove into the oven. The oven door springs shut, so one arm must
hold it open. The robot starts a one-handed carry by one side handle only if that carry is certified after a pivot probe; otherwise it
sets the pot back down, props the door open, takes both handles and slides it in. The robot drives from stove to oven, so base motion
is part of the certified moves. Sizing as above: a pot carried by one side handle is a long lever, so the one-handed carry may never be
admitted with the claws we model; the sizing study decides whether this branch exists.

### Role of the mobile base

The base adds a cheap recovery (drive closer instead of changing the grip), makes cautious set-downs costly when the nearest surface is
metres away, and gives larger ways back. It also adds base position error and inertial loads. We use it only as a recovery option and
for slow transport with a stated acceleration limit a_max; base position error enters as a fixed margin, not as a claim.

### Other candidates

1. **Groceries into the fridge.** A rigid two-handled basket, one claw per handle; one arm frees itself to open the fridge. The next
   base move, backing away for the door to swing, is certified only for light loads, which triggers the fallback. Needs a rigid basket.
2. **Open a jar.** One claw holds the jar, the other twists the lid. Lid tightness and jar grip are hidden; a small twist is the probe;
   the fallback braces the jar in a corner or holder. Slipping is at the edge of the quasi-static model.
3. **Oven tray.** One arm holds the oven door, one claw pulls the tray by its rim; the fallback rests the tray on the open door or rack
   and regrasps nearer the heavy end.

Dropped: the coffee canister on a high shelf. Demoted: the NERO box turnover (former Demo C), kept as evidence for the grasp
condition.

## Baselines

All methods use the same open-weight proposer (Cosmos Reason 2 or Qwen3-VL). The expected behaviours below are hypotheses to test.

| Method | Expected behaviour when the food sits far from the handle |
|---|---|
| AgentChord-style progress filter [4] | Holds one-handed; the pan twists and tips |
| Estimate, then act | Trusts a point estimate of the food position; tips when the estimate is wrong |
| Plan-time robust grasp [15] | Always uses two-arm support; no tips, but slow, and cannot serve one-handed |
| Fixed-target backup [7, 8] | Retreats to a fixed safe pose and stops; the task fails |
| Ours | Falls back, steadies or regrasps, serves |

AgentChord runs its own code against our open model if the code is released (the paper links only a project page); otherwise a
re-implementation of its progress filter. Ablations: no probe, stopped backup (backup ends at a stop, not in R), enumerated recoveries
(no VLM proposals).

## Metrics and settings

**Metrics.** Task completion, tips or drops, unnecessary set-downs, stops, certified refusals, probes run, time, how often the fallback
fired, and whether each fallback landed in R(Θ_t). Each run logs the backup margin at every control step; we expect ours to stay above
zero and baselines to reach zero before they fail.

**Settings.** MuJoCo first. The pan is held by a weld that breaks when the required grip wrench exceeds the claw's capacity at the true
food values. That rule is the certificate's own model, so the sim test alone cannot catch a wrong grasp model; a subset of trials uses
friction contact instead of the weld to check it. Then YOR with NERO arms, joint torque feedback and parallel-jaw claws, using a pan
with a movable weight. (Unverified: that the hardware YOR has NERO arms with joint torque sensing; our sim mounts NERO arms on the YOR
lift at an assumed mount.) The grasp check is validated in contact simulation before hardware trials.

## Open decisions

1. **Range update (decision 3).** Keep the consistency monitor with a guard backup and reset (as written above), or use a calibrated ε
   (scenario or conformal bound on torque residuals, soundness with probability 1 − δ) with the monitor only reporting. A calibrated
   bound should be set per trajectory (maximum residual over a run), because residuals within a run are correlated. The reset also
   conflicts with the 2026-10-24 rule of never resetting a belief to its original range.
2. **Sizing.** Pan, handle and food range against the claw's real grip force.
3. **Pot demo order.** Whether the one-handed side-handle carry is ever admitted.
4. **Hardware facts.** The real NERO joint torque and grip limits (the 100 Nm in the model is a placeholder), and whether torque sensing exists.
5. **References.** Check the exact titles of [11], [12], [14], [18] before submission.
