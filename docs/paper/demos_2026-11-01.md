### D1. Covered pot (main per 11-01; pending, claims §8 item 1)

**Task.** A pot with a loose lid and hidden solid contents sits on the stove. Both claws pinch the side handles, lift it, carry it to
the counter and set it down. The lid must stay on, the pot must not slip or tilt in the claws, and no arm may touch the stove or
counter. The contents' mass and position, the friction, and the exact pose are hidden.

**Cycle.**
1. **Observe.** ZED fit of the pot pose. The pot looks the same turned 180 deg, so the yaw is folded into (-90, 90] (MODEL,
   `segments._fold_c2`). The world stays in the prior box: sensing only narrows the aim error (bounds rule 4).
2. **Admit the plan.** Choose (grasp point, grip force, lift skill) from the plan menu (section 3). Admit it only if it is certified
   for every theta still possible.
3. **Probe, only if the verdict splits.** One claw raises its handle a few mm while the other side stays on the stove. The joint
   torques narrow the contents' mass and offset. Not built for the pot.
4. **Lift and carry.** The backup is armed throughout: lower until the stove carries the pot, then open and retreat.
5. **Fall back and recover.** Re-grasp at another point or force, switch the lift skill, or refuse with a certificate.

## 2. Test worlds: systematic design

**Problem.** Worlds drawn i.i.d. from the old box are easy: every plan in the menu holds. They cannot separate good plans or
proposers from bad ones.

**Wanted.** A frozen world set that:
- lies inside a stated, labelled box Theta;
- is discriminating: most worlds have some plans holding and some failing;
- has three tiers: easy, boundary and infeasible (infeasible worlds test refusal);
- labels each world by failure mode: in-claw rotation, in-claw translation, lid, torque, reach/clearance;
- is checked against simulator artefacts;
- is built from the plan menu only, never from a proposer's answers;
- has a held-out half.

**Method.**
1. **Theta and the plan menu.** Theta is written out in section 3; every new range is ASSUMED until the user approves it. The plan
   menu Pi is grasp point x grip force x lift skill.
2. **Margins, not pass/fail.** For each (world, plan), record:
   - slip limit minus slip in each claw;
   - lid limit minus lid motion in the pot frame;
   - torque headroom;
   - clearance.
3. **Model screen.** Use the analytic margins to drop worlds that are surely easy or surely infeasible, and to label the mode:
   - in-claw twist need m g x lever against capacity mu F x pad size;
   - payload torque, linear in (m, m s);
   - lid friction angle against the tilt.
4. **First pass.** A Sobol design over Theta (fixed seed), run with the corner plans of Pi.
5. **Adaptive boundary.** Sample where plans disagree, i.e. where the plan-menu outcome is split. This is our variant of
   boundary-focused sampling; the papers below target a single pass/fail threshold.
6. **Worst case per plan.** CMA-ES falsification for each plan (the `segcert.falsify` pattern). This gives one counterexample per
   plan, labelled by mode.
7. **Archive and selection.**
   - Keep cells of (failure mode x tier), as in a MAP-Elites archive.
   - Choose final worlds by how much the plans' outcomes differ (IRT-style item discrimination), so few worlds separate plans well.
   - Infeasible worlds: no plan in Pi holds.
8. **Artefact check.** Re-run boundary worlds with the more accurate contact setting:
   - 1 ms step, finger condim 4, torsional 0.003; the twist test gives 97 % of the analytic value (`physics_ab/RESULT.txt`);
   - or check them in Drake.

   Drop worlds whose verdict flips, or keep them with a note. Only the contact model changes; the task and scene stay fixed.
9. **Freeze.** Write a PREREG, split into dev and held-out halves, and compute the answer key: the set of plans that hold in each
   world. Only after that, run proposers.
   Scores:
   - hold rate;
   - regret against the best plan in the menu;
   - refusal accuracy on infeasible worlds;
   - unnecessary refusals.

**Prior art (checked 11-01).**

What follows:
- **Torque.** Score heavier contents against 16 Nm (fw >= 1.11), with 100 Nm as the sim column. The 8 Nm Piper cap does not apply.
  The 16 Nm figure is a command range, not a rated torque, so it is ASSUMED as the limit.
- **Grip.** If the 40 N rating holds, the sim claw is 4x weaker than the robot's. Every grip result so far (pan twist capacity,
  rim clamp, pot slip) is conservative for grip force. The plan menu forces would then be {10, 20, 30, 40} N, and D3 (serve from
  a pan) may come back: the one-claw twist capacity scales with grip force, so 0.4-0.8 Nm at 10 N becomes about 1.6-3.2 Nm at
  40 N, against 2-3 Nm needed. Using 40 N in the sim changes the MJCF actuator forcerange (a physics change): user decision.
- **Speed.** Joint speed limits are not enforced in the sim. The lifts are slow, but any carry or base motion must check them.
