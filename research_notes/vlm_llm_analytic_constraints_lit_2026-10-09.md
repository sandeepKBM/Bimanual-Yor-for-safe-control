## Question

Unbiased literature study (do NOT compare with or evaluate any particular new method; the goal is to map the frontier and its limits): What has been published, latest first (especially 2024-2026, plus the key 2023 foundations), on vision-language models (VLMs) or large language models (LLMs) that WRITE ANALYTICAL CONSTRAINTS for ROBOT MANIPULATION, in both SINGLE-ARM and BIMANUAL / dual-arm settings? "Analytical constraints" includes: keypoint/relational constraint functions (e.g. ReKep), cost and constraint functions for trajectory optimization or MPC (e.g. VoxPoser value maps, Language-to-Rewards), signal temporal logic / LTL / formal specifications generated from language, control barrier functions or safety filters specified by LLMs/VLMs, geometric constraints (collision, orientation, contact, stability, containment), and set-based representations (polytopes, zonotopes, convex regions). For each work report: (1) what constraint representation the model outputs and how it is grounded in perception (keypoints, point clouds, segmentation, scene graphs); (2) how the constraints are solved (optimizer, planner, MPC, CBF-QP, sampling); (3) exactly what it was tested on: single-arm or bimanual, which robots, simulation vs real, which tasks and objects, number of tasks and trials, success metrics and reported numbers; (4) stated limitations and failure modes (perception errors, constraint errors, non-convexity, real-time speed, dynamics/physical parameters, uncertainty). Include bimanual-specific works (e.g. ReKep's bimanual tasks, SafeBimanual, bimanual constraint/code generation papers) and safety-specification works (LLM-to-STL/CBF). Output: a structured map of what is done, what it is tested on, and where the evidence stops.

## Summary

SCOPE. This revision maps 14 works in which an LLM or VLM writes, selects or sets analytical constraints or costs for robot manipulation. They are ordered newest first by first arXiv submission date, which runs from June 2023 to December 2025, with revisions up to March 2026. Three more works were examined and set aside as edge cases (BiKC+, Jan 2026; VLBiMan, Sep 2025; a logic-aware schema, Dec 2025), because in them the model does not write a constraint. Six works were verified 3-0 by the pipeline: KUDA, GeoManip, OmniManip, ReKep, CoPa and MOKA. Eight were checked once by the reviser against primary text taken from critic-supplied links, without a vote, so they are rated low confidence: NL2SpaTiaL, SafeBimanual, UniDiffGrasp, SAFER, OWL-TAMP, Brunke et al., VoxPoser and Language to Rewards (L2R). Both 2023 foundations named in the question, VoxPoser and L2R, now have entries. Code as Policies appears only as a baseline inside other papers.

WHAT THE MODEL OUTPUTS.
- Free-form code. Keypoint or part cost functions (ReKep, GeoManip, KUDA), code that composes 3D voxel value maps (VoxPoser), and Boolean pose classifiers for a task-and-motion planner (OWL-TAMP).
- Template filling or selection. Relation-to-loss templates (CoPa); distance/angle constraints (OmniManip); points and waypoints (MOKA); weights and parameters of pre-defined reward terms (L2R); labels mapped to superquadric control barrier functions, or CBFs (Brunke); natural-language constraints parsed into CBFs (SAFER); a per-stage on/off mask over five author-written safety costs (SafeBimanual); and an object, part and arm-mode choice that becomes a point-cloud region (UniDiffGrasp).
- Formal logic. Spatio-temporal logic formulas (NL2SpaTiaL).
Grounding is mostly open-vocabulary detection and segmentation lifted with RGB-D. The exceptions are SAFER, which uses Vicon motion capture on hardware, NL2SpaTiaL, which does not say where its poses come from, and L2R, which uses ground-truth state in simulation.

HOW THE CONSTRAINTS ARE SOLVED. Methods include:
- Local nonlinear programs: ReKep, GeoManip, CoPa. OmniManip does not name its solver.
- Sampling-based search or MPC: VoxPoser, KUDA, and L2R, which runs MuJoCo MPC over the full MuJoCo physics model.
- Task-and-motion planning: OWL-TAMP.
- Hard CBF quadratic programs (CBF-QPs): Brunke at 45 Hz, and SAFER.
- Soft gradient guidance of diffusion models: SafeBimanual, UniDiffGrasp.
- Robustness-scored rejection of sampled rollouts from a vision-language-action (VLA) model: NL2SpaTiaL.

BIMANUAL EVIDENCE. Four works test model-set constraints on two arms. All coordination constraints found are per-arm or written by the authors.
- ReKep: 3 dual-arm tasks with 10 trials each; automatic 12/30, human-annotated 18/30. Its bimanual prompt gives each constraint function one arm's end-effector plus all keypoints.
- SafeBimanual: 8 RoboTwin simulation tasks with 100 episodes each, and 4 real tasks on a Galaxea R1 with two 7-DoF arms, as stated in the paper.
- UniDiffGrasp: dual-arm Baxter grasping of 4 large objects; grasp success 0.767.
- SAFER: one hardware run of two KUKA mobile manipulators carrying a cylinder together.
OWL-TAMP used dual-arm hardware, but does not say whether any task used both arms. No examined text has the model write closed-chain, load-sharing or timing constraints.

SAFETY. Here a 'safety term' means one whose stated purpose is collision avoidance, clearance or a hard safety envelope.
- In VoxPoser the LLM itself composes avoidance cost maps; these are soft costs.
- In Brunke, SAFER, SafeBimanual and OWL-TAMP the model chooses or states the constraints, and the authors design their analytic form.
- In ReKep, OmniManip, CoPa and UniDiffGrasp collision handling is written by the authors only.
- KUDA, GeoManip and MOKA have no collision term in the text read.
Only Brunke claims formal guarantees. It reports 0.00% constraint-violating time steps over 40 teleoperated trajectories per method on a Franka FR3: 8 scene/held-object combinations x 5. That figure holds only relative to the constraint set the LLM produced. On the authors' benchmark the multi-prompt LLM reached 60% precision and 99% recall, after the prompt was tuned on that same validation set.

WHERE THE EVIDENCE STOPS. All absence statements below mean 'not found in the texts read'.
- Real-robot results that report counts use 5-10 trials per task or setting. The exception is UniDiffGrasp, with 30 trials per method.
- Several works give no real-robot trial counts: SafeBimanual, OWL-TAMP and L2R. SAFER has one hardware run.
- The six verified real-robot papers share no benchmark. Simulation results do use existing benchmarks or simulators (RoboTwin, LIBERO, Meta-World, OmniGibson, SAPIEN, MuJoCo MPC), but the authors chose the task subsets and ran all the baselines.
- Formal specification is thin: only NL2SpaTiaL, which reports translation accuracy, was examined. LTL/STL works the critic named were not examined.
- CBF work is two papers, both with author-designed barrier forms.
- Set-based constraints appear only as author-designed sets: Brunke's superquadric unions and SAFER's table polygon.
- No explicit uncertainty modeling was found in the works that were keyword-searched.
- Most works examined date from before mid-2025, so 2025-2026 coverage reflects the search, not the field.

### F4 (confidence: low)

NL2SpaTiaL (Luo, Liang, Xia, Cai; arXiv 2512.13670, submitted 15 Dec 2025, v2 24 Mar 2026; no venue listed). A formal-specification work; embodiment not stated. (1) Representation and grounding: natural language is translated into SpaTiaL, a spatio-temporal logic built on STL. It has geometric atomic predicates (touch, closeTo, farFrom, overlap, partial overlap, enclosed-in, leftOf, between, oriented; above is also mentioned), temporal operators G, F and U, and Boolean operators. Formulas are generated in one shot as Hierarchical Logical Trees (nested JSON). The models are Phi-3.5-mini, Llama-3-8B and Qwen-2.5-14B fine-tuned with 4-bit QLoRA, plus GPT-5.2 few-shot (k=5). Predicates are evaluated on object poses and geometric attributes. In the downstream demos, predicates 'map to continuous kinematic observations (e.g., end-effector and object poses)'; how these are perceived is not described. (2) Use: there is no optimizer. The robustness signal monitors pi0.5 rollouts on LIBERO. In a generate-and-test loop, candidate pi0 short-horizon rollouts that violate the formula are rejected and the most robust is kept. (3) Testing: offline translation accuracy (Correct Match, 3 seeds, 150 instances per benchmark from 30 skeletons x 5 paraphrases). Fine-tuned tree generation scored 95.8-99.2 at depth 2 and 56.1-73.0 at depth 4; GPT-5.2 few-shot scored 96.6 and 42.0. Tree generation beat flat-generation baselines at depths 3-4. The LIBERO uses are qualitative figures (stage-wise robustness, repeated failed grasps, rejected rollouts); no task success rates or trial counts were found in the text, and no real-robot experiment is described. (4) Stated limitation: training supervises only the final nested JSON, so node-level annotations are underused.

Evidence: Reviser's single read of the abstract page, the arXiv HTML and the PDF text (Sec. F, Figs. 6-7). This entry shows translation accuracy and qualitative monitoring, not closed-loop manipulation performance.

Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.13670

### F5 (confidence: low)

SafeBimanual (Deng, Guo, Wang, Wu, Wang; arXiv 2508.18268, submitted 25 Aug 2025; CoRL 2025 per the critic, no venue on the arXiv page). Bimanual. (1) Representation and grounding: GPT-4o splits the task into stages. For each stage it infers the likely unsafe-interaction pattern from a fixed taxonomy and outputs a binary mask over five author-written safety costs: object collision, behavior alignment, gripper poking, gripper tearing and gripper-gripper collision. It also names relational keypoints per arm and stage-end conditions. The authors derived the cost set from 1,320 demonstrations over 65 tasks in 7 benchmarks, where 'over 96.2%' of unsafe interactions fell into five categories. The VLM selects and parameterizes costs; it does not write their analytic form. Keypoints: object keypoints from DINOv2 features, SAM2 masks, PCA and k-means (k=5), following ReKep, filtered at 4 cm and tracked by CoTracker3; object centers and axes from Omni6DPose; gripper tips from forward kinematics. Cameras: a ZED head camera, a wrist D435i and an L515 for point clouds. (2) Solving: test-time guidance. Cost gradients on the estimated clean action chunk steer the final DDIM denoising steps of a pre-trained diffusion policy. The text gives k <= 3 in Sec. 3.4 but M = 0.3K in Algorithm 1. Base policies: DP, DP3 and RDT-1b in sim; DP and iDP3 on the real robot, all trained or fine-tuned on 50 episodes per task. The guidance is soft; there is no hard constraint. (3) Testing, sim: 8 RoboTwin tasks, 100 episodes each. Success counts only episodes 'completed successfully without any unsafe behavior'. Table 1 averages (success, unsafe rate): DP 20.7 to 36.0% and 45.6 to 20.9%; DP3 53.9 to 67.3% and 35.3 to 19.1%; RDT-1b 36.4 to 48.9% and 50.8 to 35.3%. The headline differs by place: +13.7/-18.8 in the abstract, 13.8/18.9 in the caption, and 39.6 to 50.7% / 43.9 to 25.0% in the body. Testing, real: the paper says it 'use[s] the Galaxea R1 humanoid robot, featuring two 7-DoF arms (70 cm reach, 100 N force), a 4-DoF waist'. 4 tasks (pass banana, pour water, wipe bowl, place fruits) plus a long-horizon prepare-breakfast task; the paper reports success improved 'by 32.5%' and unsafe interactions down 30.0%. Whether these are relative changes or points is not stated, and trials per task are not in the text (results are only in Fig. 4). (4) Stated limitations: no complete safety guarantee; residual unsafe behavior in clutter; weak on semantic failures such as wrong stacking order; reliance on keypoint and pose estimates (occlusion, noise); the VLM 'occasionally generates incomplete or suboptimal constraints'.

Evidence: Reviser's single read of the PDF text (Secs. 3.4, 4.4, App. B.2, D.3, real-world setup). The 7-DoF figure is quoted from the paper's hardware section, so the critic's suggestion of 6-DoF is not adopted. Reviser arithmetic: the mean of the three Table 1 success gains is 13.7 points (37.0 to 50.7%), matching the abstract. The body's 39.6% baseline does not match.

Sources: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.18268

### F6 (confidence: low)

UniDiffGrasp (Jilin University authors; arXiv 2505.06832, submitted 11 May 2025; no venue in text read). Edge of scope: a VLM sets a geometric region constraint for grasp generation. Single-arm and dual-arm. (1) Representation and grounding: GPT-4o maps the instruction and an RGB image to {target object, target part, operation mode (single-arm or dual-arm)}. Grounding DINO + SAM segment the object and VLPart the part, giving a global point cloud P and a part cloud P_t (RealSense D435). The part cloud is 'leverag[ed] ... as a geometric constraint'. In dual-arm mode, two target regions come from semantic splitting (e.g. a pot's two handles) or, if the VLM gives no semantic cue, a geometric left/right split. (2) Solving: part-guided diffusion of a Constrained Grasp Diffusion Field samples 6-DoF grasps on P_t while respecting P. Single-arm selection is by minimal energy. Dual-arm pairs pass author-designed filters: energy thresholds, an inter-gripper collision check, a force-closure stability threshold, then maximal distance between gripper centers. (3) Testing, real only: a dual-arm Baxter with parallel-jaw grippers. Single-arm: seven household objects, '30 trials per method' (per object or in total is unclear). Grasp success (lift and stable hold) 0.876 and collision-free rate 0.900, above GroundedSAM+GraspNet and ThinkGrasp. Dual-arm: four large objects (basin, keyboard, laptop, pot). Grasp success 0.800/0.767/0.767/0.733, mean 0.767, against 0.475 for an author-run FPS+KNN region baseline. Per-object values are multiples of 1/30. The metric is grasping, not task completion. (4) No limitations section was found in the text read.

Evidence: Reviser's single read of the PDF text (Secs. III-A, III-C, IV, Tables I-II). The arXiv ID came from the critic's text, not a URL field.

Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06832

### F7 (confidence: low)

SAFER (Khan, Andrev, Murtaza, Aguilera, Zhang, Ding, Hutchinson, Anwar; arXiv 2503.15707, submitted 19 Mar 2025; preprint). Multi-robot task planning with a CBF safety layer. (1) Representation and grounding: a task-planning LLM outputs instruction sequences. A separate Safety Planning LLM outputs natural-language constraints per robot (e.g. 'Robot 1 must not collide with table A') and feedback on conflicts and missing preconditions. A parser turns the constraints into barrier functions; the paper does not say who wrote the parser, and the safe-set designs follow the authors' earlier work. Hardware grounding uses Vicon motion capture and a tracking helmet for users. Models: GPT-4o and DeepSeek-r1; which one serves as the Safety Planning LLM is not specified. (2) Solving: a CBF-QP that minimally changes the nominal input. It covers joint position, velocity and torque limits, obstacles, workspace, singularity and inter-robot collision. Hardware examples include a static polygon for a table (a set-based safe-set representation) and a moving keep-away zone around users. (3) Testing: COHERENT simulation, 40 tasks from 5 scenes with heterogeneous agents (arms, a quadrotor, a robot dog); repetitions not stated. Safety metric: violations in generated plans, scored by an LLM-as-a-Judge against 15 risk criteria. Averages: baseline GPT-4o 34.66, SAFER-GPT-4o 18.28 (stated -47%), SAFER-DeepSeek-r1 7.84 (stated -77.5%, relative to the GPT-4o baseline). Hardware: two KUKA iiwa 14 arms with Robotiq grippers on Clearpath Ridgeback bases carrying a cylinder together, one reported run. (4) Stated or evident limitations: 40 tasks because of API cost; a single hardware demonstration; no DeepSeek-r1 baseline without SAFER; S-4o and S-r1 labels mixed up; the LLM judge is not validated; latency described only as a 'minimal increase'.

Evidence: Reviser's single read of the abstract page and the arXiv HTML (carried over from the previous revision; not re-fetched). The averages were computed by the fetch summarizer from Table I.

Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.15707

### F8 (confidence: high)

KUDA (Liu, Zhang, Li; arXiv 2503.10546, 13 Mar 2025). The robot arm is not named and bimanual use is not mentioned, so 'single-arm' rests only on that absence. (1) Representation and grounding: SAM masks are farthest-point sampled into labeled keypoints and reference points on the RGB image. GPT-4o writes code giving each target as a reference point plus an offset (p = r + [dx,dy,dz]). The cost is the unweighted sum of keypoint-to-target Euclidean distances, with keypoints lifted to 3D and matched to the nearest object point. ## Caveats

COVERAGE. Fourteen works are mapped, and three edge cases are examined but set aside (BiKC+, VLBiMan, the logic-aware schema). Only six of the fourteen passed 3-vote verification: KUDA, GeoManip, OmniManip, ReKep, CoPa and MOKA. The other eight (NL2SpaTiaL, SafeBimanual, UniDiffGrasp, SAFER, OWL-TAMP, Brunke, VoxPoser, Language to Rewards) come from critic-supplied links. The reviser checked each once against the primary text, without a vote, so they are rated low confidence.

Depth of those reads:
- Full PDF text (pdftotext): NL2SpaTiaL, SafeBimanual, UniDiffGrasp, Brunke, VoxPoser, L2R, plus ReKep and GeoManip for the additions marked [L].
- arXiv HTML: SAFER; OWL-TAMP (first 100k of 163k characters); VLBiMan (all of it, across three reads); BiKC+ (first 100k of 138k characters).
- Abstract only: the logic-aware schema.
UniDiffGrasp's arXiv ID appeared in the critic's text, not in a URL field.

Not examined: Plug in the Safety Chip (the supplied page returned HTTP 429 and then a bot-check page), Lang2LTL, NL2TL, AutoTAMP, PRoC3S and SafeTap, all named by the critic. Code as Policies appears only as a baseline in other papers.

CRITIC ISSUES. All were accepted, with these partial qualifications:
- Galaxea R1: the critic suggested the arms may be 6-DoF. The paper's hardware section says 'two 7-DoF arms ... a 4-DoF waist', so 7-DoF is kept and quoted.
- VLBiMan: it does use both arms in mixed sync/async mode, but its VLMs only segment objects. The 'geometric feasibility constraints' are author IK checks, so it is listed as an edge case, not as model-written bimanual constraints.
- BiKC+: the critic's 'VLM-assisted coordination-mode identification' holds. The VLM outputs contact graphs, and rules derive the modes, so there is no analytical constraint.
- Benchmarks: whether COHERENT and RAVENS-YCB are third-party benchmarks was not checked.
- SafeBimanual's CoRL-version numbers were not fetched. The arXiv text gives three different headline versions, and all three are now in the map.

## Open questions

- What did 2025-2026 publish beyond the works examined here? In particular, other bimanual constraint or code-generation papers, and LLM-to-LTL/STL work (e.g. Plug in the Safety Chip, Lang2LTL, NL2TL, AutoTAMP, PRoC3S, SafeTap) that feeds a planner or MPC with hard constraints and reports closed-loop robot success.
- Does any work have the model write bimanual coordination constraints (closed-chain or relative pose, load sharing, handover timing, inter-arm clearance), rather than per-arm constraints (ReKep) or the selection or parameterization of author-written ones (SafeBimanual, UniDiffGrasp)? Do ReKep's automatically generated bimanual constraints ever reference keypoints held by the other arm?
- Does any VLM-constraint system model uncertainty explicitly (perception error bounds, keypoint or pose covariance, unknown mass or friction), or use model-written set-based representations (polytopes, zonotopes, convex regions)? The only sets found are author-designed (Brunke superquadrics, SAFER table polygon), and Brunke's guarantees hold only relative to LLM labels with 60% precision and 99% recall, measured on the set the prompt was tuned against.
- What do Code as Policies' primary experiments show? Are there third-party re-evaluations of VoxPoser, L2R or ReKep under a shared benchmark, instead of author-run re-implementations on each paper's own tasks?
- Unresolved details: SafeBimanual's real trials per task, whether its 32.5% is relative or in points, and its CoRL-version numbers; OWL-TAMP's real trial counts and bimanual use; L2R's real robot model and trial counts; NL2SpaTiaL's closed-loop results; Brunke's kitchen success rates; how MOKA turns points into motion; UniDiffGrasp's trial split.

## Refuted claims

- All testing was on real robots: a wheeled single-arm Franka platform with 4 tasks (Pour Tea, Recycle Can, Stow Book, Tape Box) and a stationary dual-arm platform of two Franka arms with 3 bimanual tasks (Fold Garment, Pack Shoes, Collaborative Folding). Each setting had 10 trials with randomized object poses. (0-3; https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01652)
- No optimizer, MPC or constraint solver turns the points into motion. The grasp comes from a DexNet-style sampler of about 30 antipodal proposals, and the system runs the one closest to the VLM-predicted grasp point. The function keypoint is then moved through the pre-contact waypoint, the target point and the post-contact waypoint. (1-2; https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03174)
