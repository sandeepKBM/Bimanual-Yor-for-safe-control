# Learned / latent HJ safety filters: handling of world-model and value errors on unseen actions, and whether caution can replace action-coverage data (coverage to 2026-10-01)

Method and provenance. Full texts were read (pdftotext of arXiv PDFs; 7 already in /common/users/ss5772/bimanual_data/lit/pdfs, the rest downloaded there). Every quote below is from a full-text read unless tagged UNVERIFIED. No code or experiments were run; web reading only, no hardware or live-service contact. 2026 papers are flagged **[2026 - CONCURRENT]**. Labels: SCOOPS / PARTIAL / BACKGROUND.

**Bottom line (adversarial):** I found NO paper that SCOOPS the full claim ("deployment-time caution cannot substitute for off-policy counterfactual action-coverage data, and the selection rule matters little" for a learned latent HJ safety value). The pieces are scattered, and several papers hold one or two of them:
1. The mechanism (value/model is optimistic on unsupported actions, and the max over actions exploits it) is stated plainly by UNISafe, LatentCBF, Sensitivity-Shaping, FISOR, RENEW and others. Novelty cannot rest on the diagnosis.
2. "Conservatism/caution alone is insufficient" is stated in UNISafe (training-time CQL/COMBO conservatism "performs even worse than LatentSafe"), PROCO 2026 (conservative estimation "fails to adequately constrain the policy"), and Sibai et al. (more conservative switching sends the fallback into OOD states, so violations do not fall monotonically). The slogan is not new; the clean head-to-head across five deployment-time cautions against counterfactual-branch data is.
3. "More/diverse data fixes it" is the explicit remedy or future work in LatentSafe (limitations), LatentCBF (mixed-policy data), Sibai et al. (future work: diverse switching-threshold data), PROCO (model-generated counterfactual unsafe samples), WorldSync (distributional coverage). No paper compares data against caution on one safety value with a quantified failure rate over many savable states.
4. Closest threats, in order: UNISafe (E.1 data-size sweep vs pessimism, plus Table 2), PROCO 2026, Sensitivity Shaping 2026, Sibai-Tabbara-Yang CP paper, Cao and Bansal (limitations), LatentCBF. A reviewer who knows these will say "UNISafe already showed pessimism helps only when data is thin; PROCO already showed counterfactual data beats conservatism." The defensible novelty is therefore (a) the quantified 79% / 40% / 21% / 10-23% / 1-3% ladder on physics-savable states, (b) the filter's max-over-actions exploitation mechanism isolated as the cause, (c) selection-rule insensitivity (the weakest-supported part; see Q4, where one paper points the other way).

## Q1. Which latent / learned-HJ safety papers diagnose value over-optimism or exploitation on OOD actions or states?

### Takeaway
Diagnosis is well established. UNISafe, Cao and Bansal and LatentSafe attribute failures to the world model being optimistic on OOD scenarios. LatentCBF and Sensitivity Shaping come closest to the specific mechanism (the critic or dynamics is wrong on actions off the training policy's support). Nobody quantifies the max-over-actions exploitation on a savable-state set as we do.

### Cited Findings
- **UNISafe** (Seo, Nakamura, Bajcsy; CoRL 2025; arXiv 2505.00779; v2 23 Sep 2025). PARTIAL. Abstract: "latent safety filters built on top of these models may miss novel hazards and even fail to prevent known ones, overconfidently misclassifying risky out-of-distribution (OOD) situations as safe." Body: "the uncertainty-unaware safety filter LatentSafe optimistically treats uncertain actions as safe, leading to failure." Also: "LatentSafe overestimates the safety of OOD actions, leading to unsafe action proposals." Setup note: Dubins data is 1000 expert trajectories that never enter the failure set plus 50 random; "the dataset exhibits low density within the unsafe region, resulting in high epistemic uncertainty ... and leading to overly optimistic world model imagination near critical decision boundaries." This is the same "nominal data -> optimistic imagination" regime, but the diagnosis is about failure-set coverage / OOD imagination, not an explicit "max over actions exploits error on unseen actions" statement. — [arXiv 2505.00779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00779)
- **LatentCBF** (Nakamura, Bishop, Man, Johnson, Manchester, Bajcsy; arXiv 2511.18606, Nov 2025; L4DC June 2026 per search listing, not verified). PARTIAL, the closest statement of the "unseen-action value error" mechanism. Abstract: "reinforcement learning (RL) approximations trained solely on safety policy data yield inaccurate value estimates for nominal policy actions, precisely where CBF filtering needs them." Related-work paragraph (verbatim): "out-of-distribution actions are known to produce poor value estimates (the problem of overestimation bias), existing approaches to this problem instead introduce underestimation (Fujimoto et al., 2018) or conservative critic updates (Kumar et al., 2020) that maintain high-performance without addressing the inaccuracies of the critic itself. This is a non-issue in standard sum-of-reward RL ... but is unfavorable in our setting where we wish to use the critic for safety filtering." Fix is data: "we fit our critic using a mixture of both the safety fallback actions from pi^safe and nominal task-oriented actions from pi^nom"; the NoMix ablation "permits erratic actions and spills in 20% of trials". — [arXiv 2511.18606](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.18606)
- **Cao and Bansal, "When World Models Lie"** (John Cao, Somil Bansal; arXiv 2609.34300; 28 Sep 2026) **[2026 - CONCURRENT]**. PARTIAL. "If the world model is incorrect, the resulting value function can inherit its errors and produce overconfident safety estimates." Intro: "An optimistic world model may imagine benign outcomes for unsafe actions, causing the value function to become overconfident and delaying safety interventions precisely when they are needed most." Case study 1 trains the WM on "1500 nominal trajectories collected without the slippery zone" (nominal-only data). Their diagnosis is error under dynamics shift on the nominal policy's actions, not unseen evasive actions. — [arXiv 2609.34300](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34300)
- **LatentSafe** (Nakamura, Peters, Bajcsy; RSS 2025; arXiv 2502.00935; v1 Feb, v3 30 Apr 2025). PARTIAL (ablation exists, but the opposite sign: conservative, not exploited). Intro: "While world models require diverse coverage (success, play, and/or failure data) to accurately predict the dynamical consequences of robot actions ...". Fig. 3 ablation "Effect of Incomplete Knowledge of the World": WM trained on a biased dataset with action space limited to {0, a_max}; "Figure 3 shows that the bias of the world model affects the robot's understanding of safety: since the world model did not learn about the possibility of turning right, LatentSafe pessimistically classifies states as unsafe if they require a right turn to avoid collision." Limitations: "the safety filter can only protect against outcomes that the world model can predict. This means that the world model needs to be trained on some amount of unsafe data". NOTE: this is a qualitative single-figure Dubins ablation in which missing action coverage shrinks the safe set (conservative) rather than producing optimistic exploitation. It is directly relevant prior art for "action coverage matters" and a possible contradiction in sign to reconcile with our 79% over-optimistic result (a hard-coded 2-action set vs a learned continuous extrapolation). — [arXiv 2502.00935](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00935)
- **Sensitivity Shaping for Latent Modeling** (Hongzhan Yu, Chenghao Li, Ruipeng Zhang, Henrik Christensen, Sicun Gao; arXiv 2606.14585; 12 Jun 2026) **[2026 - CONCURRENT]**. PARTIAL, mechanism very close. "We show that these surrogates can fail when the dynamics are locally insensitive to critical action choices: unsupported control actions may produce latent predictions that resemble demonstrated transitions, suppressing OOD signals despite large true predictive errors." Intro: "the scale of the training data alone does not guarantee coverage of task-critical counterfactuals or reliable generalization beyond the training manifold"; "calibrate surrogates scores with conformal prediction which bounds the false rejection rate for in-distribution inputs ... Yet, safe model-based control also requires the complementary property: genuinely unsupported transitions should be reliably rejected. This property is not guaranteed by calibration alone". Setting: vision-based Dubins and manipulation with a model "trained on failure-free data"; one-step/OOD filters, not a Fisac discounted HJ value. Fix: a model-side regularizer (control-Jacobian sensitivity), neither more data nor pessimism. — [arXiv 2606.14585](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14585)
- **FISOR** (Zheng et al.; ICLR 2024; arXiv 2401.10700; author list from memory, not extracted). PARTIAL for the mechanism in an HJ safety value: "the min_a' operation in Eq. (7) could query the out-of-distribution (OOD) actions, potentially leading to severe underestimation issues in offline setting" (cost convention, so under-estimating cost means over-optimism). Fix: a data-support-constrained min (IQL-style), the "restrict the max to in-data actions" condition we test. Also: "CPQ ... assigns high-cost values to both OOD and unsafe actions, which distorts the value function and may cause poor generalizability" (pessimism criticised). State-based Safety-Gymnasium, no world model. — [arXiv 2401.10700](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10700)
- **HJ offline RL for emergency collision avoidance** (Jiang, Zhao, ... Zhang; arXiv 2606.15311; 13 Jun 2026) **[2026 - CONCURRENT]**. BACKGROUND. "Since directly minimizing over all actions suffers from out-of-distribution optimistic bias, reversed expectile regression (RER) is employed". Same in-support fix as FISOR, driving application, no comparison to data. — [arXiv 2606.15311](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15311)
- **SafeFQL** (Tayal, Tayal, Prakash; RLJ 2026; arXiv 2603.15136) **[2026]** and **V-OCBF** (Tayal et al.; TMLR Apr 2026; arXiv 2512.10822) **[2026]**. BACKGROUND. Both restrict critic learning to dataset support: "All critic learning is performed entirely within the support of D, so that no out-of-distribution action queries are required" (SafeFQL); V-OCBF "never querying the barrier on out-of-distribution actions". V-OCBF's related work notes uncertainty-aware approaches "often become overly conservative." Neither is a world-model filter. — [2603.15136](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15136), [2512.10822](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.10822)
- **Do Robotic World Models Really Follow Actions? (WorldEcho/WorldSync)** (Chen, Liu, Wu, Guo, Wang, ... Zhang; arXiv 2608.24885; 25 Aug 2026) **[2026 - CONCURRENT]**. BACKGROUND (strong diagnosis of off-expert action failure in WMs, not safety/HJ). "current world models reasonably execute expert actions but struggle with diverse off-expert trajectories, either ignoring the commanded actions or producing visually invalid rollouts." Their remedy is a data-led one: WorldSync "broadens the training distribution over action consequences" ("distributional coverage"), plus architecture changes. — [arXiv 2608.24885](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24885)
- **RENEW** (Bhamidipaty, Kochenderfer, Ramamoorthy; arXiv 2607.14180; 15 Jul 2026) **[2026 - CONCURRENT]**. BACKGROUND (offline MBRL, not safety). "they are vulnerable to model exploitation where data coverage is thin. Prior work addresses this either by collecting more expert demonstrations, which is often expensive, unsafe, or unavailable, or by conservative algorithms that avoid uncertain regions, which limits generalization." Fix: repair the model from preference data, with epistemic-uncertainty-directed query selection. — [arXiv 2607.14180](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14180)
- **Biased Dreams** (Berger, Frauenknecht, Trimpe, Leibe; RLJ 2026; arXiv 2604.25416) **[2026]**. BACKGROUND, relevant to why pessimism can fail in latent models: "ensemble disagreement captures local epistemic uncertainty, it does not reliably reflect global compounding model error accumulated over prolonged RSSM latent rollouts ... uncertainty diminishes despite increasing discrepancies from the true environment dynamics." (Abstract only for the quotes; full-text body not exhaustively read.) — [arXiv 2604.25416](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25416)
- **Neuro-Symbolic World Models for Counter-factual Safety in Automated Driving** (Matheus Henrique Rosado Vicente; ICML 2026 per Lacuna page; OpenReview SKXshniQQj) **[2026 - CONCURRENT]**. BACKGROUND, quotes UNVERIFIED (secondary summary page only; full text not read): "Standard world models for autonomous driving are often 'optimistic' scene completers that fail to predict danger for actions not taken in the training data"; "A model can achieve near-zero validation loss ... while still having a high 'false-safe' rate." Fix per the summary is rule-based shielding, not data or HJ. — [Lacuna summary](https://lacuna.tiptreesystems.com/work/neuro-symbolic-world-models-for-counter-factual-safety-in-automated-driving/wrk_ea8068fce46f3715217ff783adcae1b9)
- **Kim, Nakamura, Bajcsy** (arXiv 2510.06492; v2 6 Jun 2026). BACKGROUND. Studies estimation and prediction gaps from partial observability, not unseen actions. Mitigations: "privileged multimodal supervision for estimation gaps and conformal risk calibration for prediction gaps"; they note the conformal fix works "albeit with increased conservativeness" and, for RGB-only WMs unable to predict spills, "our method leads to a pessimistic safe controller." So: information (data/modality) fixes the estimation gap, caution (conformal) covers the prediction gap at a conservatism cost. Not an action-coverage study. — [arXiv 2510.06492](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06492)

### Inferences
- The "max over actions exploits model error" mechanism is established in offline RL (FISOR, CQL lineage) and re-stated for latent filters (LatentCBF, UNISafe, Sensitivity Shaping). The novel part is demonstrating it quantitatively for a latent discounted HJ value on savable states.
- The sign question (LatentSafe Fig. 3: missing action coverage makes the filter conservative; ours: nominal data makes it exploitable) needs an explicit reconciliation, otherwise a reviewer will cite LatentSafe's ablation.

### Gaps
- Bajcsy et al. 2019 (reachability-based navigation in unknown environments): NOT read; it is analytic HJ with online map updates, so I expect BACKGROUND at most. The DeepReach paper (2021) itself was not read; only follow-ups below.
- No paper found that reports the max-over-actions exploitation rate over a ground-truth-savable state set.

## Q2. Which papers propose pessimism / uncertainty fixes, and do they acknowledge limits (residual unsafety, over-conservatism, needing coverage)?

### Takeaway
Every pessimism/uncertainty paper admits it is not a guarantee and several admit it can fail. UNISafe's own Table 2 shows 20% residual failure with its best uncertainty variant, and Cao and Bansal admit the conformal guarantee breaks if recovery actions are missing from calibration. None frames this as "caution cannot replace coverage"; they call it future work.

### Cited Findings
- **UNISafe** (as above). Limitations section: "Component vs. System-level Safety Assurances. ... it does not formally guarantee zero failure rates." "Challenges in Uncertainty Quantification. ... Even within regions that are nominally in-distribution, world model predictions can still be imprecise or biased ... while a transition may be classified as in-distribution, this does not guarantee the correctness of the model's prediction". Block-plucking Table 2 (Safe-success / Failure / Incompletion / %filtered): no filter 0.58/0.41/0.01; LatentSafe 0.68/0.30/0.01/7.2% filtered; UNISafe-JRD 0.72/0.20/0.08/37.7% filtered (column order read off the table; the failure column for JRD, 0.20, is residual unsafety). So pessimism roughly halves relative failure but intervenes about 5x as often. Overconservatism is explicit: "targeting aleatoric uncertainty (TotalUncertainty and MaxAleatoric) tends to be overly conservative, resulting in higher incompletion rates". Also: "Offline RL with conservative losses performs even worse than LatentSafe, indicating that conservatism alone cannot replace failure set identification." Appendix E.3 analysis of the CQL-style conservative objective: "it introduces bias into the learned safety values ... conservative objectives are effective in standard offline RL tasks ... they are less suitable for safety analysis. Safety-critical applications require accurate specification of the safe and unsafe sets, which conservative regularization alone fails to guarantee." — [arXiv 2505.00779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00779)
- **Cao and Bansal 2609.34300 [2026 - CONCURRENT]**. PARTIAL. Pessimism by minimizing V over an ACI-calibrated latent ball. Conclusions and limitations (verbatim): "our method is reactive and does not provide formal safety guarantees. Solving (8) incurs additional computational cost ... Moreover, Theorem 1 assumes exchangeability between deployment and calibration trajectories, which can be violated under safety filtering if recovery actions are absent from the calibration set." Also: "A larger radius allows the filter to guard against a broader range of model errors, but may introduce excessive conservatism ... A smaller radius ... may fail to account for safety-relevant prediction errors." Their pessimism is driven by observed latent prediction error on the actually executed nominal action; the pessimism radius is initialised to q_max ("Maximum pessimism is held at the start, when data is scarce"). They criticise UNISafe's ensemble uncertainty ("ensemble uncertainty can remain low even when predictions are systematically biased"), and ACoFi's residual ("this residual may reflect self-consistency within the incorrect model rather than disagreement with the real system"). Hardware results show UNISafe "attains the lowest failure rate during deployment time WM shifts, but does so at the cost of substantial conservatism". This is the closest published statement that calibration cannot see actions absent from calibration data. — [arXiv 2609.34300](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34300)
- **ACoFi** (Huriot, Tabbara, Sibai; arXiv 2604.18482; 20 Apr 2026) **[2026]**. BACKGROUND. ACI on the Q-value vs bootstrapped target; "a soft safety guarantee rather than a hard safety guarantee"; reports up to 7x fewer violations under OOD speed/steering perturbations but "reached fewer goals ... by 30% at most". No limitation about unseen actions. — [arXiv 2604.18482](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18482)
- **SLS2 / Pixels to Proofs** (Nath, Srinivasan, Yin, Jiang, Fang, Chou; arXiv 2606.15594; 14 Jun 2026) **[2026]**. BACKGROUND. Conformal latent error sets + conformalised "in-domain latent support" constraint + robust MPC. Honest caveat: "This controls support mismatch, while the CP guarantee itself assumes that closed-loop residual scores are exchangeable with the calibration scores. In practice, we support this assumption by collecting calibration transitions from representative randomized rollouts". That is a coverage-of-calibration-data assumption stated in passing. — [arXiv 2606.15594](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15594)
- **Dyna-SAuR** (Eisele, Frauenknecht, Solowjow, Trimpe; arXiv 2604.25508; 28 Apr 2026) **[2026]**. PARTIAL (supports "pessimism plus data"). "The filter avoids failures and high uncertainty regions. Thus, better models expand the set of safe and certain states, reducing filter conservatism." Figure 1 caption: collected data "improve[s] the dynamics model, which expands the certain area and reduces conservatism in the next iteration." So uncertainty-avoidance buys safety and data buys back permissiveness; the paper states a limitation: "efficiently expanding E in high-dimensional spaces is a limitation of the current approach." — [arXiv 2604.25508](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25508)
- **Kim, Nakamura, Bajcsy 2510.06492**: conformal calibration gives pessimism "albeit with increased conservativeness"; limitations: "how best to inject the missing safety information remains unclear." (see Q1.)
- **LatentCBF**: limitations: "Its reliance on learned latent representations, dynamics, and RL approximations precludes formal safety guarantees ... in our data-driven setting, the filter can push the system into out-of-distribution (OOD) states, reducing task performance. Future work should integrate uncertainty estimation or OOD detection (Seo et al., 2025...)". This is a latent-filter paper that explicitly defers caution to UNISafe-type tools while using a data-mixing fix itself.
- **CrossSafe** (Tabbara, Yang, Sibai; arXiv 2609.28984; 24 Sep 2026) **[2026]**. BACKGROUND. "As a learned filter, CrossSafe provides no formal guarantee, and filtered collision rates remain relatively high"; future work: "calibrating the learned value function using conformal prediction ... training across a larger and more diverse set of robot embodiments and tasks." Data-diversity remedy (embodiments), not action coverage. Relevant to our bimanual setting (whole-body collision avoidance across five bimanual embodiments) but not to the claim. — [arXiv 2609.28984](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28984)
- **Safety value-constrained MPC** (Wang, Nguyen, Jordana, Righetti, Bansal; arXiv 2604.23863; 26 Apr 2026) **[2026]**. BACKGROUND. Limitation: "The learned safety value function is not perfect, and the feasible set ... is not guaranteed to be control invariant. One could seek to provide probabilistic guarantees ..., but formal guarantees on the learned safety value function are not available." Uses DeepReach with MPC-computed supervision points; nothing on action coverage. Note for the user's repo: this is the paper behind the "value-constrained MPC terminal constraint" memory note. — [arXiv 2604.23863](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23863)
- **PROCO** (see Q3) lists conservative-estimation baselines failing.

### Inferences
- The residual-failure pattern (UNISafe 0.20, Cao and Bansal "reactive and no formal guarantee") is consistent with our "caution fixes are partial", but these papers present it as incremental, not as a structural limit.
- Cao and Bansal's own admitted exchangeability gap ("recovery actions are absent from the calibration set") is the closest published mechanism for why Cao-and-Bansal-style pessimism would be unchanged (our 79%): its radius is set from prediction error on actions the nominal policy actually took, so it does not respond to errors on the evasive actions the max would select. This is my inference, not something they state.

### Gaps
- Cao and Bansal do not test a nominal-only-data vs counterfactual-data comparison; their Dubins shift (speed x1.5 in a zone) is a dynamics shift, not an action-coverage shift.
- Whether Cao and Bansal's method is evaluated on any state set where the max-over-action fallback is the failure source: not found.

## Q3. Does any paper show empirically that data/coverage beats pessimism for a safety value, or that pessimism fails without coverage?

### Takeaway
Partial yes, in two forms, neither identical to our claim. (i) UNISafe E.1: an ablation over data amount where pessimism helps only when data is thin and the gap vanishes with data (data substitutes for caution, not the reverse). (ii) PROCO 2026: conservative estimation and support-restriction both fail without unsafe/counterfactual samples, and model-generated counterfactual unsafe samples fix it (offline safe RL, not latent HJ filters). No paper shows that caution fails to recover safety lost to missing action coverage while counterfactual data does, on one value, with all the cautions side by side.

### Cited Findings
- **UNISafe E.1 "How do dataset size and failure classifier performance affect the safety filter?"**. PARTIAL. Dubins, 1000 expert trajectories plus N random trajectories, N in {0,10,50,100,500,1000}. Balanced accuracy of the learned value (monitor) (Table 14): N=0: LatentSafe 0.50 vs UNISafe 0.84; N=10: 0.79 vs 0.93; N=50: 0.84 vs 0.93; N=100: 0.75 vs 0.92; N=500: 0.97 vs 0.97; N=1000: 0.97 vs 0.97. Their conclusion: "incorporating OOD failures via uncertainty quantification is particularly beneficial when the dataset does not adequately cover the true failure set. When fewer random trajectories are available, safety filters trained without OOD modeling exhibit significantly degraded performance due to unmodeled epistemic uncertainty." And: "As the dataset size increases ... reducing the size and impact of the OOD failure set." Reading: pessimism and data are substitutes in their Dubins sweep (pessimism closes most of the gap at low N; at N>=500 data alone reaches the same 0.97). That is the OPPOSITE of "caution cannot substitute for coverage" on this metric, and it must be confronted in any writeup. Differences to exploit: (a) the random trajectories are on-policy-ish random coverage of the failure region, not counterfactual evasive branches from visited states; (b) the metric is monitor balanced accuracy on a state grid, not closed-loop fallback success on savable states; (c) pessimism there is training-time augmented-state reachability (OOD failure set inside the Bellman backup), not deployment-time caution on a nominal-trained value. — [arXiv 2505.00779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00779)
- **UNISafe E.4 / Table 2**: "Offline RL with conservative losses performs even worse than LatentSafe, indicating that conservatism alone cannot replace failure set identification." Closest verbal match to our slogan, but about training-time CQL/COMBO conservatism, and "failure set identification" there means the labelled/OOD failure set, not action coverage. Also in E.4: "SafeOnly shows limited effectiveness, showing OOD detection from success-only data is insufficient in complex settings."
- **PROCO** (Xue, Yuan, Cheng, Yang, Yu; arXiv 2605.01356; 2 May 2026) **[2026 - CONCURRENT]**. PARTIAL (closest to the data-vs-caution framing, wrong setting). Offline safe RL with hard (HJ-style feasibility) constraints on Safety-Gymnasium, datasets with few or no unsafe samples. Quotes: "The soft-constraint baseline CCAC exhibits the weakest safety, showing that conventional TD learning with conservative estimation fails to adequately constrain the policy, causing distributional shift and severe violations. BC-based methods improve safety by restricting policies to safe-only data but still cannot reliably distinguish feasible from infeasible states". Mechanism: "Using the cost function and dynamics model, PROCO simulates future state evolution to generate diverse and informative unsafe samples" ("counterfactual unsafe samples" via branched model rollouts), reporting "improvements of over 400% in safety performance" on safe-only datasets. Differences: counterfactuals are model-synthesised (so exposed to the same exploitation we diagnose), states are low-dimensional, the safety value is an offline HJ-style cost value and not a latent-WM discounted HJ; no ablation that equalises caution strength vs data amount. Checked in full text: the two conservative/support baselines (CCAC; BC) are not the same as conformal / epistemic-pessimism / in-support max in a latent WM. — [arXiv 2605.01356](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01356)
- **Sibai, Tabbara, Yang, "Statistically Assuring Safety of Control Systems using Ensembles of Safety Filters and Conformal Prediction"** (arXiv 2511.07899; 11 Nov 2025). PARTIAL. Shows that adding caution (smaller alpha -> more conservative switching) does NOT monotonically improve safety, and attributes it to the fallback's training distribution: "the distribution shift issue can manifest at any chosen alpha. For example, when alpha is very small, we are likely to switch from pi^nom to pi^safe when the ego vehicle is still very far from the two cars and road boundaries. During training, the trajectories used to learn pi^safe were collected using the fixed switching condition ... This means pi^safe was primarily exposed to states where the nominal controller was about to enter the unsafe set. When deployed with a more conservative switching threshold (small alpha), pi^safe encounters out-of-distribution states at which it might have unpredictable outputs and sometimes cause unsafe behavior." Limitations (verbatim): "Future work should explore training with diverse datasets that include trajectories collected using different switching thresholds to improve robustness to the threshold adjustments induced by conformal prediction." That is: caution (conformal threshold) cannot fix a fallback that lacks coverage; remedy = diverse data. Evidence is 50 trials per setting on a 10-D highway task, with one member model (q=2.37) failing 22%; it is an observation plus future work, not a controlled data-vs-caution comparison. — [arXiv 2511.07899](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07899)
- **LatentCBF**: mixing nominal-policy actions into critic training (a data/coverage fix) is shown to matter (NoMix 20% spills in hardware; "LatentCBF more effectively guides the diffusion policy toward safe and successful lifts (80% vs. 38%)" is for the whole method vs the switching filter, not NoMix vs Mix; do not over-read). No caution baseline was compared. — [arXiv 2511.18606](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.18606)
- **Dyna-SAuR 2604.25508 [2026]**: iterative data collection "expands the certain area and reduces conservatism" (see Q2). Pessimism is the safety mechanism and data its cost-reducer; again complementary, not a head-to-head.
- **WorldSync 2608.24885 [2026]**: off-expert action failure fixed by "distributional coverage" training data (WM fidelity metric, not a safety value). Relevant evidence that action coverage of the WM is the lever, from outside the HJ literature.
- **Offline MBRL pessimism literature (BACKGROUND, local PDFs, not re-read in depth):** MOPO (2005.13239), MOReL (2005.05951), COMBO (2102.08363), RAMBO (2204.12581). Those show uncertainty penalties help in expected return with enough support; they do not study hard safety values.

### Inferences
- "Data beats pessimism for a safety value, head-to-head, with caution baselines held at their best setting": no exact precedent found. UNISafe E.1 is the nearest precedent but points toward "pessimism helps when data is thin, data eventually matches it".
- Your claim that caution is flat (79% -> 79% for Cao-and-Bansal-style adaptive conformal) is consistent with Cao and Bansal's own stated exchangeability caveat; UNISafe-style pessimism helping (40%) is consistent with UNISafe's E.1. So our numbers are plausible given the literature; they are not contradicted. The headline framing should therefore be "caution is insufficient and has a huge false-intervention cost", not "caution is useless".
- Sibai et al.'s observation (more conservative switching sends the fallback OOD) is a distinct mechanism from ours (the max over actions exploits unseen actions inside the value). Cite as related but different.

### Gaps
- Could not verify whether any 2026 paper not surfaced by search runs an explicit data-vs-pessimism ablation for latent HJ values. Google Scholar "cited by" for LatentSafe/UNISafe was not accessible through the available tools (web search only); coverage of 2026 follow-ups is by keyword search and the lit/pdfs folder, so a miss is possible.

## Q4. Does any paper find that the data-selection rule (directed vs random) matters little for safety-value data?

### Takeaway
No paper found that shows selection-rule insensitivity for safety-value data. The evidence I found leans the other way for state sampling in known-dynamics HJ learning (directed sampling helps), and the offline-RL literature emphasises coverage over how it was gathered. Our "selection rule did not matter" result is therefore not contradicted by a direct precedent but is also unsupported by one, and will be challenged by Boundary Sampling.

### Cited Findings
- **Boundary Sampling to Learn Predictive Safety Filters via Pontryagin's Maximum Principle** (James Dallas, Thomas Lew, John Talbot, Jonathan DeCastro, Somil Bansal, John Subosits; arXiv 2604.13325; 14 Apr 2026) **[2026 - CONCURRENT]**. BACKGROUND but adverse. "Boundary trajectories that barely avoid constraint violation are informative, but rare under typically-used uniform sampling"; "PMP sampling reduces failure rates, particularly for limited compute. At a terminal epoch of 30k, failure rate statistically significantly decreases from 0.66 to 0.3 (2-layer) and from 0.50 to 0.45 (3-layer). As we train for longer the gap narrows". Directed (boundary) sampling beats uniform significantly for a learned CBVF, but the gap narrows with more training, and this is about collocation/state sampling with known dynamics, not action coverage under a learned WM. Authors' count: 4 seeds, one-sided t-test. — [arXiv 2604.13325](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.13325)
- **Forward Trajectory Steering for HJ Reachability** (Sungje Park, Stephen Tu; arXiv 2608.11480; 11 Aug 2026; CDC 2026) **[2026]**. BACKGROUND. Adaptive collocation sampling steered by the current value's optimal control and disturbance; abstract-only (UNVERIFIED for any comparison against random). — [arXiv 2608.11480](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11480)
- **RENEW 2607.14180 [2026]**: uncertainty-directed preference queries beat undirected ones for sample efficiency ("collecting preferences over uniformly sampled rollouts is inefficient: most trajectory pairs will cover regions where the model is already accurate"), again adverse to "selection does not matter" for WM repair, though a different object. — [arXiv 2607.14180](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14180)
- **UNISafe E.1**: adds N random trajectories (undirected) and sees steady improvement up to N=500; no directed alternative was compared. Consistent with "coverage, not selection", but no selection comparison. — [arXiv 2505.00779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00779)
- **LatentSafe**: data = "successes, random exploration, and unsafe demonstrations" of "varying quality"; no selection study. — [arXiv 2502.00935](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00935)
- **Offline-RL coverage framing (UNVERIFIED, search-summary only):** a search surfaced offline-RL theory stating the optimality gap depends on data coverage and that collective coverage suffices; I did not read these full texts and they are not safety values.

### Inferences
- The only direct empirical precedent on directed vs undirected data for learned HJ values (Dallas et al.) favours directed sampling at low compute and converges with more training, which could be framed as "selection matters for efficiency, not asymptotically". Our claim should be scoped to the counterfactual-branch regime and budgets tested (3-10k), and ideally reproduce a compute/branch-count curve showing where directed vs random converge.

### Gaps
- No paper found testing random vs uncertainty-directed vs value-directed counterfactual action data for a safety value in a learned WM. SAFE-GIL (2404.05249, adversarial-perturbation data collection for imitation learning) surfaced in search but was not read; likely BACKGROUND.

## Q6. (Added scope) Conformal / scenario calibration of a learned HJ value or of a safety filter's outcomes

### Takeaway
Three distinct calibration targets exist in the literature: (a) latent prediction error (Cao and Bansal, SLS2), (b) the learned VALUE (Sibai et al. Phase 1, ACoFi-style, Reachability Barrier Networks), and (c) closed-loop OUTCOMES of the actual switched policy (Lin and Bansal, Sibai Phase 2, Sinha et al.). Only (c) accounts for a wrong fallback action, and it needs a true simulator or deployment rollouts. Conservatism cost is reported by Lin and Bansal and ACoFi. Exchangeability failure from the filter's own induced distribution is acknowledged by Cao and Bansal, Sibai et al., SLS2 and, most thoroughly, Lindemann et al. 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **Lin and Bansal, Verification of Neural Reachable Tubes via Scenario Optimization and Conformal Prediction** (Albert Lin, Somil Bansal; L4DC 2024; arXiv 2312.08604; v2 10 Apr 2024). PARTIAL for this sub-question. Calibrates closed-loop OUTCOMES: "we first sample N independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) states x_1:N from S ... We next compute the costs J^pi(x_i, 0) ... by rolling out the system trajectory from x_i under pi~(x, t). Let k refer to the number of 'outliers' - samples that are empirically unsafe". So it verifies the learned value together with the learned fallback policy against true dynamics (requires a simulator), and therefore does see a wrong fallback action, unlike latent-error calibration. Conservatism: prior scenario method "is highly sensitive to outlier errors in the learned solutions. This can lead to highly conservative reachable tubes and a severe loss of recovery"; their robust variant trades "a small decrease in safety level (from 99.999% to 99.974%) ... we are able to significantly increase the assured safe volume from 0.56 to 0.81." They also weight training loss to "focus on reducing optimistic errors ... which are more safety-critical". Limitation noted: the value over a constrained state space "affects both learning-based methods and traditional grid-based methods." The guarantee is over states sampled i.i.d. from the safe set, not over deployment distribution. — [arXiv 2312.08604](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.08604)
- **Sibai et al. 2511.07899** (see Q3). Calibrates the VALUE (V(x,alpha) = V_theta(x) - q-hat(alpha)) in Phase 1 and closed-loop OUTCOMES in Phase 2 (Theorem 1: P[J^pi(x0) > 0] ~ Beta(N_cert - k, k + 1) over i.i.d. initial states). Admits exchangeability break: "We acknowledge that this assumption may be violated due to distribution shift: the switching behavior between pi^nom and pi^safe during calibration dataset collection can differ from that induced by the deployed policy". Conservatism cost: "Hypothetically, this increased conservatism should lead to monotonically decreasing violation rates as alpha decreases. However, we do not observe this monotone trend in our experiments". — [arXiv 2511.07899](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07899)
- **Cao and Bansal 2609.34300**: calibrates latent prediction error (ACI + trajectory-level q_max); no false-intervention accounting beyond "frequent incompletions"; Theorem 1 holds only "with high probability over calibrated trajectories" and admits the exchangeability gap quoted in Q2. Nominal-trained WM error is measured on executed (nominal) actions only.
- **Lindemann et al., "Robust Conformal CBF and CLF Controllers via Iterative Policy Updates"** (Omid Mirzaeedodangeh, Eliot Shekhtman, Nikolai Matni, Lars Lindemann; arXiv 2606.15366; 13 Jun 2026) **[2026 - CONCURRENT]**. PARTIAL, strongest explicit statement of the filter-induced shift. Abstract: "Such CP bounds can then be embedded into robust CLF/CBF frameworks. However, such an approach does not retain stability/safety guarantees because of the distribution shift between the closed-loop trajectory distribution under the deployed CLF/CBF policy and the trajectory distribution from which the CP bound and its guarantees were derived." Remedy: episodic re-calibration with adversarially robust CP and a "distribution shift budget" derived by closed-loop sensitivity; so the fix is iterated on-policy data, i.e. DAgger-flavoured. Related-work line: "These works require exchangeable data and do not apply in settings with distribution shifts." Model-error (not unseen-action) setting with Lipschitz assumptions; the bound grows "exponentially in T, so it can be conservative for long horizons." — [arXiv 2606.15366](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15366)
- **Jahanshahi and Chen, Control Barrier-Value Functions under Partial Observability via CP** (arXiv 2608.13819; 13 Aug 2026) **[2026]**. BACKGROUND. CP of the estimation error gives an error radius used to tighten the HJ margin (obstacle inflation) and a QP filter "against bounded disturbance". Admits conservatism: "Larger bounds enlarge ... tighten (27), and shrink the certified safe set." Assumes "Exchangeability of rollouts." Estimation error, not unseen actions. — [arXiv 2608.13819](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13819)
- **Reachability Barrier Networks** (Sharpless, Jeong, Tonkens, Bansal, Herbert, Kim; arXiv 2505.11755; May 2025). BACKGROUND. Conformal prediction guarantees for discounted-HJ CBFs; claims "5.5x safer and 1.9x less conservative than the neural CBFs." Abstract-level read only. — [arXiv 2505.11755](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11755)
- **Sinha, Schmerling, Pavone, Closing the Loop on Runtime Monitors with Fallback-Safe MPC** (arXiv 2309.08603; 2023). BACKGROUND. CP-calibrated runtime monitor with a perception-independent fallback plan; end-to-end assurance claimed under the CP assumptions. Abstract-level read only. — [arXiv 2309.08603](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08603)
- **ACoFi 2604.18482 [2026]**: calibrates the Q-value vs its bootstrapped target ("range of possible safety values of the nominal policy's output"); reports safe-policy usage and goal-reaching cost but no false-intervention rate on a savable-state set. Cao and Bansal argue this residual is self-consistent with a wrong model. — [arXiv 2604.18482](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18482)
- **SLS2 2606.15594 [2026]**: calibrates latent error and in-domain support; exchangeability assumed and "supported" by randomized calibration rollouts.

### Inferences
- Answers to the specific questions. (i) What is calibrated: latent error (Cao-Bansal, SLS2), value (Sibai Phase 1), outcomes (Lin-Bansal, Sibai Phase 2). (ii) Conservatism reported: Lin-Bansal (safe volume vs confidence), UNISafe (filtered % and incompletion), Cao-Bansal (qualitative, incompletions), Jahanshahi-Chen ("shrink the certified safe set"); none reports a false-intervention multiplier like our 28x. (iii) "Calibration doesn't fix a wrong fallback action": only implied (Sibai: fallback sees OOD states; Cao-Bansal: recovery actions absent from calibration); outcome-level calibration (Lin-Bansal, Sibai Phase 2) would detect it but not repair it. (iv) Filter-induced shift breaking exchangeability: explicitly acknowledged by Cao-Bansal, Sibai et al., SLS2, and studied as the main topic by Lindemann et al. 2026.
- The strongest prior art against "calibration is flat": none quantifies it on the failure source we isolate. Outcome-level calibration is a better-positioned alternative baseline than latent-error calibration and is worth adding to the comparison, since it is the only family that can see the fallback.

### Gaps
- No paper found that applies conformal calibration to the value of a latent-WM discounted HJ and then measures the max-over-actions-driven failure on a savable set. Lin and Bansal need the true simulator; not applicable to a learned WM without ground truth.

## Q7. (Added scope) Game / robust formulations where an adversary stands in for model error

### Takeaway
Game forms (ISAACS, Gameplay Filters, Q-CBF) set the disturbance bound by a designer-chosen ODD, not by calibrated model error, and rely on a runtime forward rollout (not the learned value) to retain guarantees, because the learned value and learned disturbance are admitted to be unreliable. Conservatism is reported (ISAACS 20.4% false-unsafe) but not value collapse. I found no paper that combines a conformal error bound with the disturbance player of a latent-WM HJ game; the nearest are Jahanshahi-Chen (CP error radius into an HJ margin plus a bounded-disturbance QP filter, estimator-space) and Lindemann et al. (CP error into robust CBF).

### Cited Findings
- **ISAACS** (Hsu, Nguyen, Fisac; L4DC 2023; arXiv 2212.03228; v3 7 Jun 2024). PARTIAL for the structure. Disturbance = "worst-case realization of model error and training-to-deployment discrepancy allowed by the designer's uncertainty", i.e. a bound set by the designer. Important admission of the same exploitation structure on the min-player side: "while a suboptimal controller policy would merely result in a more conservative filter, a suboptimal disturbance policy may lead us to erroneously conclude that a state is safe, when in reality there exists a different uncertainty realization that could drive the state to the failure set. To obtain a robust safety guarantee under possibly suboptimal ISAACS learning, we therefore treat the controller and the disturbance differently." Fix is a runtime rollout check with forward reachable sets, not more data. Reported conservatism: the critic "achieves 1.4% false-safe rate ... This contrasts with a more conservative 20.4% false-unsafe rate"; the learned controller "loses safety from 10.4% of true safe states, where the oracle controller succeeds"; the filter had "moderate conservativeness relative to the optimal Hamilton-Jacobi solution". No value collapse reported; bounds are small (delta = 0.03 slice). Latent WM: not used (state-space, known simulator). — [arXiv 2212.03228](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.03228)
- **Gameplay Filters** (Nguyen, Hsu, Yu, Tan, Fisac; CoRL 2024; arXiv 2405.00846; v4 Jan 2025). PARTIAL/BACKGROUND. Admits the learned value is unreliable and the learned adversary is only approximate: "In practice, we have found that [the learned reach-avoid critic is] often unreliable and/or overly conservative. A key advantage of the gameplay rollout is that ... all uncertainty falls on the learned disturbance policy." Limitations: "we do not have strong theoretical guarantees on convergence of offline gameplay learning, and therefore learned disturbance policy can in general be expected to behave suboptimally in at least certain regions of the state space." Also argues worst-case is not intrinsically too conservative: "this depends entirely on what set and distribution we choose to run these assessments against." Sim dynamics, not a learned latent WM. — [arXiv 2405.00846](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00846)
- **Q-CBF / Maximal Robust CBFs through Adversarial RL** (Oh, Nguyen, Hu, Fisac; arXiv 2604.13192; v2 17 Sep 2026) **[2026]**. BACKGROUND. Isaacs-equation value as a robust CBF; "most robust CBFs certify only conservative subsets of the maximal robust safe set"; 100% safe over 50 quadruped trials vs 38% for least-restrictive filter. Bounded uncertainty set designer-chosen; no calibration, no latent model. — [arXiv 2604.13192](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.13192)
- **LEAP-CBF** (So, Yu, Fan; arXiv 2609.28364; 23 Sep 2026) **[2026]**. BACKGROUND (abstract level): robust CBFs "can be difficult to construct and can be overly conservative"; certificate quantifies "effort required by the disturbance to cause failure". — [arXiv 2609.28364](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28364)
- **Jahanshahi and Chen 2608.13819 [2026]** and **Lindemann et al. 2606.15366 [2026]** (see Q6): the nearest combinations of conformal error bounds with a disturbance-style robust safety value, in estimator-space HJ and robust-CBF forms respectively; neither is a latent-WM HJ game.
- **S2C** (Wu, Yang, Oh, Nguyen, Hu; arXiv 2609.27312; 23 Sep 2026) **[2026]**. BACKGROUND. Robust safety filter via adversarial RL embedded in multi-agent RL environment; irrelevant to WM error.

### Inferences
- "Calibrated robust value" (adversary power = conformal error bound, learned jointly with the WM) is unoccupied as far as I could find, but it is a natural extension and a likely reviewer baseline; Cao and Bansal's pessimistic minimization over a calibrated ball is a one-step version of exactly this (min over a calibrated set instead of a full game). Our data fix is complementary: a robust game still needs the WM to be right on the fallback's actions.
- Value collapse (V<0 almost everywhere, or V ~ l) is not reported by any paper read; ISAACS's 20.4% false-unsafe and UNISafe's 5x intervention rate are the closest conservatism numbers. Our measured 28x false-intervention figure for epistemic pessimism has no published analogue.

### Gaps
- Robust latent HJ with a disturbance player on a learned WM: not found. Whether a 2026 paper exists that I missed: possible (see Q5 gaps). No paper in the local folder reports V<0 collapse.
