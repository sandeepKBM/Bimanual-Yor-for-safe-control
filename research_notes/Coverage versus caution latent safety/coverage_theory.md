# Theory of coverage vs pessimism (offline RL / OPE / learned-model control) and its applicability to HJ safety values

Scope: theory only, 2018 to 2026-10-01. Empirical offline-RL and latent-HJ papers are left to other researchers and only mentioned as BACKGROUND.

Evidence convention. VERIFIED-FT means I read the quoted text in a local full-text PDF/TXT (in /common/users/ss5772/bimanual_data/lit/pdfs/, new downloads saved there as arxiv_<id>.pdf/.txt). ABSTRACT-ONLY means I read the abstract or a search snippet but not the theorem. UNVERIFIED means not read at all or recalled from memory.
Closeness labels: SCOOPS / PARTIAL / BACKGROUND.

Headline: no paper found SCOOPS the claim. Nothing found states a coverage-necessity or pessimism-cannot-substitute theorem for HJ/Fisac max-min safety values or latent safety filters. But the general principle (pessimism only competes with covered policies; lower bounds scale with the coverage of the comparator; policy-induced data cannot supply coverage that state-action sampling can) is established in offline RL. As a theorem the candidate claim is an instantiation, not new.

## 1. Lower bounds: what do offline-RL theory papers say about pessimism vs coverage?

### Takeaway
Offline-RL theory says two separate things. (i) Pessimism removes the need for ALL-policy coverage but not for coverage of the comparator: every pessimism guarantee is "competes with the best policy the data covers" (single-policy concentrability C*), and the minimax lower bounds in C* are matched by pessimistic algorithms. (ii) Even with coverage, function approximation needs more than realizability (Foster et al., Wang-Foster-Kakade). If the comparator is uncovered, C* is infinite and the lower bounds are vacuous-to-infinite, so no algorithm (pessimistic or not) can succeed.

### Cited Findings
- Jin, Yang, Wang, "Is Pessimism Provably Efficient for Offline RL?", arXiv 2012.15085 (ICML 2021). PARTIAL. VERIFIED-FT. Abstract: "Without assuming the sufficient coverage of the dataset, we establish a data-dependent upper bound on the suboptimality of PEVI for general Markov decision processes (MDPs). When specialized to linear MDPs, it matches the information-theoretic lower bound up to multiplicative factors of the dimension and horizon. In other words, pessimism is not only provably efficient but also minimax optimal. In particular, given the dataset, the learned policy serves as the 'best effort' among all policies, as no other policies can do better." Theorem 4.2: "SubOpt(Pess(D); x) <= 2 * sum_{h=1}^H E_{pi*}[Gamma_h(s_h,a_h) | s_1 = x]" (Gamma = xi-uncertainty quantifier). Corollary 4.5 "Sufficient 'Coverage'" gives the K^{-1/2} rate "as long as the trajectory induced by pi* is 'covered' by D sufficiently well". The bound is evaluated along the comparator pi*'s own trajectory: if pi* visits (s,a) the data never touched, Gamma is O(1) there and the bound is vacuous. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.15085)
- Rashidinejad, Zhu, Ma, Jiao, Russell, "Bridging Offline RL and Imitation Learning: A Tale of Pessimism", NeurIPS 2021 / IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory, arXiv 2103.12021. PARTIAL. VERIFIED-FT. Defines the class MDP(C*) = {(rho, mu, P, R) | max_{s,a} d*(s,a)/mu(s,a) <= C*}. Theorem 7 (information-theoretic limit, MDP): "For any C* >= 1, gamma >= 0.5, one has inf_{pi_hat} sup_{MDP(C*)} E_D[J(pi*) - J(pi_hat)] >~ min{ 1/(1-gamma), sqrt(S/((1-gamma)^2 N)) + sqrt(S(C*-1)/((1-gamma)^3 N)) }." They also note LCB "matches the performance of behavior cloning for the extreme case" C* ~ 1 (pessimism on expert-like data is imitation). The rate is only meaningful for finite C*. The statement "pessimism needed" is about the empirical-best-arm failing (their Proposition 1, constant error even when C* ~ 1), not about pessimism creating coverage. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.12021)
- Buckman, Gelada, Bellemare, "The Importance of Pessimism in Fixed-Dataset Policy Optimization", ICLR 2021, arXiv 2009.06799. PARTIAL (best structural statement of "competes only with covered policies"). VERIFIED-FT. Theorem 3 (uncertainty-aware pessimistic FDPO): "SubOpt(O_ua(D)) <= inf_pi { E_rho[v^{pi*}_M - v^pi_M] + (1+alpha) E_rho[mu^pi_{D,delta}] + (1-alpha) sup_pi E_rho[mu^pi_{D,delta}] }". Abstract: "for naive approaches ... we may need the dataset to be informative of the value of every policy. To avoid this, algorithms can follow the pessimism principle ... We show why pessimistic algorithms can achieve good performance even when the dataset is not informative of every policy". The infimum over pi is the comparator: the gap v^{pi*}-v^pi is the irreducible price of the best covered policy. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.06799)
- Liu, Swaminathan, Agarwal, Brunskill, "Provably Good Batch RL Without Great Exploration", arXiv 2007.08202. PARTIAL. VERIFIED-FT (abstract). "In certain settings, they can find the approximately best policy within the state-action space explored by the batch data, without requiring a priori assumptions of concentrability." This is the cleanest statement that conservative updates return the best SUPPORTED policy. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.08202)
- Uehara & Sun, "Pessimistic Model-based Offline RL under Partial Coverage", ICLR 2022, arXiv 2107.06226. PARTIAL (model-based version, closest to a learned world model). VERIFIED-FT (abstract): "CPPO can learn with the offline data only providing partial coverage, i.e., it can learn a policy that competes against any policy that is covered by the offline data". [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.06226)
- Yu et al. MOPO, arXiv 2005.13239, Theorem 4.4. BACKGROUND (empirical paper with a theory result). VERIFIED-FT: "eta_M(pi_hat) >= sup_pi { eta_M(pi) - 2 lambda u(pi) }" and "pi_hat can be as good as any policy pi with u(pi) <= delta". The sup over pi is again "any policy with small model uncertainty on its own distribution". [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.13239)
- Kidambi et al. MOReL, arXiv 2005.05951, Proposition 4 (lower bound). PARTIAL (closest to an explicit "no algorithm can do better" with a support-mismatch parameter). VERIFIED-FT: "For any discount factor gamma in [0.95,1), support mismatch eps ... there is an MDP M ... such that i) d^{pi*,M}(U_D) <= eps ... and any policy pi_hat that is learned solely using the dataset collected with pi_b satisfies: J(pi*,M) - J(pi_hat,M) >= R_max/(4(1-gamma)^2 log(1/(1-gamma))) * eps", with U_D = state-action pairs not in the dataset. I.e. a small fraction of optimal-policy occupancy on unseen (s,a) costs a (1/(1-gamma)^2)-amplified suboptimality for ANY learner. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.05951)
- Foster, Krishnamurthy, Simchi-Levi, Xu, "Offline RL: Fundamental Barriers for Value Function Approximation", COLT 2022, arXiv 2111.10919. PARTIAL. VERIFIED-FT. Abstract: "even if both concentrability and realizability are satisfied, any algorithm requires sample complexity polynomial in the size of the state space to learn a non-trivial policy. Our results show that sample-efficient offline reinforcement learning requires either restrictive coverage conditions or representation conditions that go beyond supervised learning". Theorem 1.1: all-policy realizability + C_conc <= 16 ... "Any algorithm using less than c*S^{1/3} samples must have J(pi*) - E[J(pi_hat)] >= c0/(1-gamma)". Theorem 1.2: also holds for "admissible" (policy-induced) data distributions with concentrability C >= 64. Introduces "over-coverage" (spurious correlations from data on irrelevant states). Relevance: coverage is necessary but not sufficient; with learned (neural) value classes the guarantee requires extra structure. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.10919)
- Wang, Foster, Kakade, "What are the Statistical Limits of Offline RL with Linear Function Approximation?", ICLR 2021, arXiv 2010.11895. PARTIAL. VERIFIED-FT (abstract): "even if i) we have realizability in that the true value function of every policy is linear in a given set of features and 2) our off-policy data has good coverage over all features (under a strong spectral condition), then any algorithm still (information-theoretically) requires a number of offline samples that is exponential in the problem horizon in order to non-trivially estimate the value of any given policy ... sample-efficient offline policy evaluation is simply not possible unless significantly stronger conditions hold; such conditions include either having low distribution shift (where the offline data distribution is close to the distribution of the policy to be evaluated) or significantly stronger representational conditions". [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11895)
- Chen & Jiang, "Information-Theoretic Considerations in Batch RL", ICML 2019, arXiv 1905.00360. PARTIAL (the original "is coverage necessary" paper). VERIFIED-FT: "To show that low concentratability is necessary, we prove a hardness result, where both realizability and completeness hold, and an algorithm has the freedom to choose any data distribution mu that is favorable, yet no algorithm can achieve poly(...) sample complexity. Crucially, the concentratability coefficient of any data distribution on the worst-case MDP is always exponential in horizon". [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.00360)
- Xie, Cheng, Jiang, Mineiro, Agarwal, "Bellman-consistent Pessimism for Offline RL", NeurIPS 2021, arXiv 2106.06926. BACKGROUND/PARTIAL. ABSTRACT-ONLY: "overly pessimistic reasoning can be equally damaging in precluding the discovery of good policies, which is an issue for the popular bonus-based pessimism". Their point is that pessimism has a price (over-conservatism) even when coverage exists, a different failure from our no-coverage failure. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06926)
- Zhan, Huang, Huang, Jiang, Lee, "Offline RL with Realizability and Single-policy Concentrability", COLT 2022 (PMLR 178). BACKGROUND. ABSTRACT-ONLY (search snippet; arXiv ID not retrieved): "polynomial sample complexity with only single-policy realizability and single-policy concentrability". Again the guarantee is for the covered comparator. [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v178/zhan22a.html)
- Jiang & Xie, "Offline RL in Large State Spaces: Algorithms and Guarantees", arXiv 2510.04088 (Oct 2025, survey). BACKGROUND, best one-paragraph source for the framing. VERIFIED-FT: "by using pessimistic algorithms, we can relax all-policy coverage to single-policy coverage: we can compete with any policy that receives good coverage by the offline data." and "pessimism encourages exploitation (i.e., staying within the current data distribution)". [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04088)
- Levine, Kumar, Tucker, Fu, "Offline RL: Tutorial, Review, and Perspectives", arXiv 2005.01643. BACKGROUND. VERIFIED-FT (one sentence): "at its core, offline reinforcement learning is about making and answering counterfactual queries ... The fundamental challenge with making such counterfactual queries is distributional shift". [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.01643)
- Xiao, Lee, Dai, Schuurmans, Szepesvari, "The Curse of Passive Data Collection in Batch RL" (arXiv title now "The Curse of Working with Policy-induced Data in Batch RL"), AISTATS 2022, arXiv 2106.09973. PARTIAL (closest to the "policy-induced vs counterfactual data" separation we observe; see section 4). VERIFIED-FT. Abstract: "even with the best (but passively chosen) logging policy, Omega(A^{min(S-1,H)}/eps^2) episodes are necessary (and sufficient) to obtain an eps-optimal policy". Theorem 1: "any (eps,delta)-sound algorithm needs at least Omega(A^{min(S-1,H_{gamma,2eps}+1)} ln(1/delta)) episodes of any length with policy-induced data collection ... The result remains true if the MDPs are restricted to have deterministic transitions." [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09973)

### Inferences
- Every pessimism upper bound has the form "min over comparators pi of [approximation gap of pi + uncertainty along pi]". Pessimism changes WHICH comparators are allowed (covered ones), it does not create coverage. So pessimism cannot beat the best covered comparator, and lower bounds (Rashidinejad Thm 7, MOReL Prop 4) say no algorithm can do better when the comparator's occupancy sits on unseen (s,a).
- "Pessimism = best effort given the data" (Jin et al.) is therefore the formal version of "caution cannot substitute for coverage", applied to ordinary reward-sum values.
- Caveat for applicability: these are worst-case minimax statements. They do not by themselves say that any given learned model in a smooth, low-dimensional system (like a pendulum) must fail; a model class that extrapolates correctly (physics-structured, Lipschitz) can sidestep tabular coverage. Wang-Foster-Kakade and Foster et al. show the converse caveat: even coverage plus realizability may not be enough for generic function classes.

### Gaps
- Zhan et al. arXiv ID and theorem text not retrieved (abstract/snippet only).
- Xie et al. theorem text not read (abstract only; local PDF downloaded as arxiv_2106.06926 but I did not extract the theorem).
- I did not find a single published theorem of the exact form "for any pessimistic penalty/ensemble/conformal estimator, if the comparator is uncovered then suboptimality >= constant". The result must be assembled from (a) the comparator-restricted upper bounds and (b) minimax lower bounds in C*; the assembly is easy but is not itself stated in the sources I read.

## 2. Does pessimism give the best policy SUPPORTED by the data? Safety / constrained MDPs? Safe-by-inaction?

### Takeaway
Yes: this is the standard reading of pessimism guarantees (Liu et al.; Uehara-Sun; Buckman; Jin et al.; Jiang-Xie). For constrained MDPs the literature says the same (reliable optimization only for policies the data supports). I found NO paper that states "when no good evasive action is covered a pessimistic safety filter is safe-by-inaction at best" for safety filters or HJ values; the nearest are the offline CMDP coverage papers and SPIBB (baseline fallback). That conclusion is an inference from combining these.

### Cited Findings
- Ko, Tewari, Hong, "Offline Constrained RL under Partial Data Coverage", arXiv 2505.17506 (v2 May 2026). PARTIAL (safety/CMDP). VERIFIED-FT. "The main obstacle in offline CMDPs is data coverage: reliable optimization is only possible for policies whose state-action occupancy is sufficiently supported by the dataset." Result: "Under this condition and partial coverage of an optimal policy, PDOCRL returns a near-optimal, near-feasible policy with a O~(eps^-2) sample guarantee". So safe/feasible guarantees are conditional on coverage of an optimal (feasible) policy; no claim about the case where it is uncovered, and no lower bound in this paper (I grepped for impossibility and found none). Also CMDP, not HJ. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17506)
- Laroche, Trichelair, Tachet des Combes, "Safe Policy Improvement with Baseline Bootstrapping" (SPIBB), ICML 2019, arXiv 1712.06924. PARTIAL (explicit "fallback to behaviour when uncertain"). VERIFIED-FT (abstract): "it bootstraps the trained policy with the baseline when the uncertainty is high"; guarantee is "at least as well as the baseline policy used to collect the data". The safety notion here is performance relative to the baseline, which is meaningless when the baseline itself is the unsafe behaviour (see Inferences). [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.06924)
- Cui & Du, "When is Offline Two-Player Zero-Sum Markov Game Solvable?", NeurIPS 2022, arXiv 2201.03522. PARTIAL for max-min structure only (the min is an adversary, not a time-min). VERIFIED-FT (abstract and Theorem 3.3): "single strategy concentration assumption is insufficient ... we propose ... unilateral concentration ... we show that the unilateral concentration assumption is necessary for learning an NE strategy." Theorem 3.3 constructs bandit games where all but one deterministic strategy pair is covered and "the output of the algorithm ALG is at most a 0.25-approximate NE strategy no matter how many data are collected". Related: Zhong et al. PMVI arXiv 2202.07511 ("information-theoretical lower bound, which suggests that the data-dependent term in the upper bound is intrinsic", ABSTRACT-ONLY for the lower-bound claim) and Yan, Li, Chen, Fan arXiv 2206.04044 (unilateral clipped concentrability, abstract-level). Relevance: if the HJ game has a disturbance player (Isaacs), coverage must be unilateral (cover best-response deviations), strictly stronger than single-policy. For a deterministic single-agent HJ value (our setting) the single-policy form should suffice. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.03522)
- Huang, Yang, Liang, "Safe Exploration Incurs Nearly No Additional Sample Complexity for Reward-free RL", ICLR 2023, arXiv 2206.14057. BACKGROUND. ABSTRACT-ONLY: assumes "a safe baseline policy is known beforehand"; safety exploration then costs almost nothing extra. Note the assumption: a known safe baseline exists. This is the online-exploration analogue of "needs a safe seed", opposite to our data-lacks-evasive-action case. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.14057)
- Oh, Nguyen, Hu, Fisac, "Provably Optimal RL under Safety Filtering", IASEAI 2026. BACKGROUND. ABSTRACT-ONLY: "enforcing safety with a sufficiently permissive safety filter does not degrade asymptotic performance." The theorem presupposes a filter that is permissive, i.e. a correct safe set; it does not address learning the filter from data. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18082)

### Inferences (my own reasoning; not from a source)
1. Pessimism (any version) turns the Fisac value into the value of the support-restricted problem. Formal sketch: T V(z) = (1-g) l(z) + g min(l(z), max_u V(f(z,u))) is monotone and a g-contraction in sup norm (Fisac et al. ICRA 2019, see section 3). Restrict max_u to covered actions A_D(z) (or penalize uncovered ones to l_min). The fixed point V_D <= V*. Take two models M, M' that agree on supp(D) and differ only at (z0, u*) with u* uncovered, where u* is the only evasive action in M'. Both generate the same data law, so any estimator's output on (z0, .) has the same law under both; by a Le Cam two-point argument one of the two has error >= half the gap, and the gap does not shrink with N. This is the safety instance of the Rashidinejad/MOReL lower bound; I am not aware of it written out for max-min operators.
2. "Safe-by-inaction at best" is not even guaranteed in the safety case. In reward RL pessimism falls back to the behaviour policy, and guarantees (SPIBB, MOPO Thm 4.4 "pi_hat should perform at least as well as the behavior policy") use the behaviour policy's own value as the floor. In a safety filter the states where the filter fires are exactly those where the nominal (behaviour) action heads to failure, so the "fallback to covered behaviour" floor is the unsafe floor. In a pessimistic value with an uncertainty penalty, uncovered actions get the worst value and the argmax is taken over a set of covered, near-nominal actions whose values are all pessimistically low and nearly tied; the argmax among them is not an evasive maneuver. This matches the described empirical failure (conservative but useless fallback) but I have no source proving it.
3. The right object is one-sided: pessimism preserves soundness (V_hat <= V*, certified-safe set is inside the true viability kernel) but destroys completeness and, more importantly, the argmax extraction. A certificate guarantee does not transfer to a constructive guarantee about the executed fallback.
4. Uncertainty-penalty argmax is attracted to LOW-uncertainty actions, i.e. in-data actions. So pessimism actively biases the fallback toward the data distribution, which is the structural reason that the "in-data-restricted max" and "ensemble pessimism" variants coincide in behavior. (Consistent with Jiang & Xie: "pessimism encourages exploitation (i.e., staying within the current data distribution)".)

### Gaps
- No source found stating the safe-by-inaction / pessimistic-argmax-not-evasive statement for CMDPs, shields or filters.
- Offline CMDP papers other than Ko et al. were not read in full text; "feasibility-aware pessimistic estimation" (arXiv 2505.08179) is withdrawn and has no usable theory.

## 3. Theory specific to learned safety values / HJ reachability / safe exploration

### Takeaway
The learned-HJ theory I found proves (a) contraction of the discounted max-min operator, (b) probabilistic verification of a given (V, policy) pair against the true system, and (c) safe-exploration guarantees that ASSUME a safe seed set or initial safe policy plus smoothness. None proves that coverage is necessary for a non-trivial safe set, but (c) implicitly shows it: the learnable safe set is the reachable expansion of the seed, i.e. what exploration can reach. No error-propagation-through-max-min bound with coverage was found.

### Cited Findings
- Fisac, Lugovoy, Rubies-Royo, Ghosh, Tomlin, "Bridging Hamilton-Jacobi Safety Analysis and Reinforcement Learning", ICRA 2019. BACKGROUND. ABSTRACT-ONLY (snippet; local PDF 1705.17342 was a bad download and removed; the correct arXiv ID is, I believe, 1812.02038, UNVERIFIED): "a time-discounted modification of the problem of maximizing the minimum payoff over time ... through a modified dynamic programming equation that induces a contraction mapping". This is the operator in our setting. [Princeton listing](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/bridging-hamilton-jacobi-safety-analysis-and-reinforcement-learni/)
- Lin & Bansal, "Verification of Neural Reachable Tubes via Scenario Optimization and Conformal Prediction", L4DC 2024, arXiv 2312.08604. BACKGROUND (verification, not coverage necessity). VERIFIED-FT. Procedure: "we sample N independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) states x_1:N from S ... compute the costs J(x_i,0) by rolling out the system trajectory from x_i under pi~(x,t)". Theorem 3: "P_{x in S}(J_pi~(x,0) > 0) ~ Beta(N-k, k+1)". The guarantee is on the fraction of the learned safe set S that is truly safe under the stated policy pi~, calibrated against true-system rollouts of that same policy. So it presupposes data on the fallback's own action support (rollouts of pi~ from sampled states): it can DETECT that a fallback is inadequate (large k) but cannot make it adequate. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.08604)
- Tibshirani, Barber, Candes, Ramdas, "Conformal Prediction Under Covariate Shift", NeurIPS 2019, arXiv 1904.06019. BACKGROUND. ABSTRACT-ONLY: weighted conformal gives valid intervals "when the likelihood ratio between test and training distributions is known or can be reliably estimated". For conformal pessimism calibrated on nominal data and used on fallback-action inputs the needed likelihood ratio is unbounded/undefined on unsupported actions, which is the concentrability coefficient again. This link is my inference. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06019)
- Cao & Bansal, "When World Models Lie: Adaptive Safety Analysis Under Wrong Imaginations", arXiv 2609.34300 (28 Sep 2026). BACKGROUND (latent-HJ, other researchers). VERIFIED-FT (abstract): uses Adaptive Conformal Inference on "discrepancies between predicted and observation-inferred latent states, then evaluates safety pessimistically by minimizing the learned value function over these sets"; "We provide a finite-time coverage guarantee for the adaptive uncertainty radius." The guarantee is on the world-model error radius at VISITED transitions, not on the quality of the argmax fallback. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34300)
- Seo, Nakamura, Bajcsy, "Uncertainty-aware Latent Safety Filters for Avoiding OOD Failures", arXiv 2505.00779. BACKGROUND (empirical). VERIFIED-FT (abstract): "obtaining comprehensive coverage of all safety-critical scenarios during world model training is extremely difficult ... safety filters ... may miss novel hazards"; remedy is epistemic-uncertainty detection calibrated by conformal prediction. This is the detection side (flag OOD), not recovery. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00779)
- Safe offline RL / filter papers with explicit support restriction: V-OCBF (arXiv 2512.10822) "restricting the updates to the dataset-supported action set"; SafeFQL (arXiv 2603.15136) "no out-of-distribution action queries are required". BACKGROUND, empirical only, checked by grep; no theorem about coverage necessity. These are algorithmic versions of the "in-data-restricted max" we tested.
- Sui, Gotovos, Burdick, Krause, "Safe Exploration for Optimization with Gaussian Processes" (SafeOpt), ICML 2015. PARTIAL (safe-learning theory with an explicit reachability benchmark). VERIFIED-FT: Theorem 1: "Assume that f is L-Lipschitz continuous ... Also, assume that S0 != empty, and f(x) >= h, for all x in S0 ... SafeOpt guarantees safety, and identifies at least one eps-optimal decision within the eps-reachable set after at most t* iterations." The benchmark is the eps-reachable set R_eps(S0): what can be certified is limited to the Lipschitz-expansion of the seed, not the whole true safe set. Same structure for GoSafe (arXiv 2105.13281) and Turchetta et al. 2016 (ergodicity/reachability, from search snippet). [PDF local safeopt_icml2015.pdf]
- Berkenkamp, Turchetta, Schoellig, Krause, "Safe Model-based RL with Stability Guarantees", NeurIPS 2017, arXiv 1705.08551. PARTIAL. VERIFIED-FT. Assumption: "we assume that we have an initial policy pi0 that renders the origin ... asymptotically stable within some small set of states S0x". Theorem 4(iii): "R_eps(S0) subset S_n subset R_0(S0)", after finitely many points "the algorithm explores the largest region of attraction possible for a given Lyapunov function with residual uncertainty about v(f(.)) smaller than eps". Again safe-set learning is bounded by seed plus GP smoothness. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08551)
- Brogat-Motte, Rudi, Bonalli, "Safely Learning Controlled Stochastic Dynamics", arXiv 2506.02754 (v3 Feb 2026). BACKGROUND. VERIFIED-FT (abstract): "iteratively expanding an initial known safe control set using kernel-based confidence bounds ... requires only mild smoothness assumptions and access to an initial safe control set". Same pattern. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.02754)
- Bennett, Misra, Kallus, "Provable Safe RL with Binary Feedback" (SABRE), arXiv 2210.14492. BACKGROUND. VERIFIED-FT (abstract; grep of text): assumes an oracle for binary safety of (s,a) and a known safe policy pi_safe; uses active learning ("concepts from active learning") to bound oracle queries; guarantee "SABRE never takes unsafe actions during training, and is guaranteed to return a near-optimal safe policy". Safe-learning results of this type buy safety of exploration by assuming oracle/seed access, i.e. replacing coverage by an assumed structure. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.14492)
- Kaynama, Mitchell et al. viability kernel computation: BACKGROUND, snippet only. The viability kernel is "the largest set of states from which safety can be ensured".

### Inferences
- In all of these the learnable safe set is the seed-reachable one; nothing in them contradicts, and SafeOpt/Berkenkamp theorems support, the view that a nominal-only dataset yields a certified safe set only as large as what the covered actions can certify. They do not prove necessity: they prove sufficiency given a seed and Lipschitz/RKHS regularity.
- Structural priors (Lipschitz, RKHS norm, physics-latent smoothness) can substitute for coverage in these theorems. So any necessity claim must be stated relative to the model class: coverage is necessary unless the class extrapolates action effects. This is the safety-specific form of the Foster et al. caveat and is probably the honest scope for the paper's claim.
- Error propagation through max-min (UNVERIFIED, my derivation): because |min(l,a) - min(l,b)| <= |a-b| and max_u is 1-Lipschitz in sup norm, the Jin-et-al.-style telescoping along the comparator trajectory should go through with the discounted-sup structure, giving an error bound of the form sum_h g^h Gamma(z_h,u_h) along the evasive comparator. I did not find this written for HJ operators. Stochastic transitions break it (min of expectation is not an expectation of min), so it is clean only for deterministic/learned-deterministic latent dynamics.

### Gaps
- No paper found with error-propagation or sample-complexity bounds for the discounted HJ operator from offline data with coverage coefficients. (Searches: HJ reachability learned error propagation; offline safety filter coverage; viability kernel data-driven lower bound.)
- Fisac et al. abstract only via snippet; DeepReach / Hsu reach-avoid (arXiv 2112.12288, downloaded to local, not read for theory) were not checked for any coverage statement.

## 4. Active-learning / experimental-design theory: does targeted data beat uniform?

### Takeaway
Theory predicts exactly the split we saw: data collected by a policy (passive/on-policy, including DAgger-style) cannot supply coverage of actions that policy never takes, while state-action ("counterfactual") sampling can; once coverage of the relevant (s,a) holds, the guarantee depends on a coverage coefficient and the selection rule changes constants, not the qualitative outcome. Active-vs-passive separations in supervised learning are regime-dependent and often vanish (high noise/agnostic), which also fits "selection rule matters little".

### Cited Findings
- Xiao et al. (arXiv 2106.09973), cited above. VERIFIED-FT. Contrast of data regimes: for policy-induced data the cost is A^{min(S-1,H)}; "For SA-sampling, the sample complexity becomes polynomial": Theorem 4 gives a lower bound "at least c1 * SA ln(1/(4 delta)) / (eps^2 (1-gamma)^3)" under SA-sampling (N samples per state-action pair), matched by plug-in methods (Agarwal et al. 2020). Their abstract: "in simple cases, such as in bandits, passive and active data collection are similarly effective, the price of passive sampling can be much higher when collecting data from a system with controlled states." The nominal dataset = policy-induced data; the counterfactual branches on the fallback's action support = SA-sampling. This is the closest published analogue to our finding. PARTIAL.
- Xie, Jiang, Wang, Xiong, Bai, "Policy Finetuning: Bridging Sample-Efficient Offline and Online RL", NeurIPS 2021, arXiv 2106.04895. PARTIAL. VERIFIED-FT (abstract): offline reduction with reference policy mu finds an eps-optimal policy in O~(H^3 S C*/eps^2) episodes where C* is the single-policy concentrability between mu and pi*; lower bound Omega(H^3 S min{C*, A}/eps^2) "for any policy finetuning algorithm, including those that can adaptively explore the environment. This implies that ... the optimal policy finetuning algorithm is either offline reduction or a purely online RL algorithm that does not use mu." So, when the reference policy covers the comparator you gain nothing from adaptivity; when it does not (C* >= A) you need full exploration. Supports "what matters is coverage C*; selection rule is second order". [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04895)
- Song et al., "Hybrid RL: Using Both Offline and Online Data Can Make RL Efficient", ICLR 2023, arXiv 2210.06718. BACKGROUND. VERIFIED-FT (abstract): efficient "whenever the offline dataset supports a high-quality policy". Same support condition. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.06718)
- Ross & Bagnell, "Agnostic System Identification for Model-Based RL" (DAgger-for-models), ICML 2012, arXiv 1203.1007. PARTIAL for the DAgger-style arm. VERIFIED-FT (abstract): "any no-regret online learning algorithm can be used to obtain a near-optimal policy, provided some model achieves low training error and access to a good exploration distribution." The guarantee is explicitly relative to an exploration distribution nu; on-policy aggregation corrects distribution shift toward the learner's own distribution but needs nu to cover the comparator. This is why on-policy data under the pessimistic fallback does not reach the evasive action. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1203.1007)

### Inferences
- "Selection rule matters little" is predicted whenever each candidate rule achieves finite coverage of the fallback's relevant (z,u) region: upper bounds scale with the coverage coefficient, and targeted vs uniform designs differ by instance-dependent factors (e.g. Wagenmaker-Jamieson-type gains), not by the feasible/infeasible dichotomy. I know of no theorem predicting "no gain from targeting" in RL; it is regime-dependent (SA-sampling vs policy-induced is the big separation, within SA-sampling the gain from targeting is a factor).
- The failure of DAgger-style on-policy data is predicted by Xiao et al. Thm 1 (policy-induced data exponentially bad) and Ross-Bagnell (needs good exploration distribution), provided the on-policy fallback does not itself take evasive actions.

- No RL paper found proving "targeted counterfactual data gives no gain over uniform counterfactual data on the same support"; our empirical observation is consistent with theory but not a theorem.

## 5. Honest verdict: is "caution cannot substitute for coverage" a direct corollary of known offline-RL lower bounds?

### Takeaway
Mostly yes as a principle. Pessimism guarantees compete only with covered comparators (Jin et al. Thm 4.2, Buckman Thm 3, Uehara-Sun, Liu et al., MOPO Thm 4.4, Jiang-Xie survey), minimax lower bounds in the single-policy concentrability C* (Rashidinejad Thm 7, MOReL Prop 4, Chen-Jiang, Foster et al.) become vacuous when the comparator is uncovered, and policy-induced data cannot manufacture coverage while SA-sampling can (Xiao et al. Thm 1 vs Thm 4; Ross-Bagnell exploration distribution). Nobody SCOOPS the safety/HJ instance, but a theory reviewer will call the bare claim a corollary. The defensible novelty is in the instantiation and diagnosis, not a new theorem.

### What is a direct corollary (cite, do not claim)
1. Pessimism (conformal, ensemble, penalty, restricted-max) cannot beat the best covered policy (all references above).
2. When the evasive comparator is uncovered (infinite C*), no learner can recover it (two-point/Le Cam argument; Rashidinejad Thm 7, MOReL Prop 4, Cui-Du Thm 3.3 style constructions).
3. On-policy/DAgger-style data under a fallback that never takes evasive actions does not add coverage (Xiao Thm 1; Ross-Bagnell condition on the exploration distribution).
4. Counterfactual/SA-style data on the right support removes the barrier (Xiao SA-sampling; Policy-finetuning C* dependence), and selection beyond coverage is a constants question.

### What may be non-trivial for safety (my assessment; not in the literature I found)
1. The comparator is structurally uncovered, not accidentally uncovered. In reward RL, behaviour policy is a decent covered comparator, which is why "pessimism = fall back to behaviour" is a good outcome (SPIBB, MOPO "at least as good as behavior"). A safety filter is invoked precisely where nominal behaviour fails, and nominal data avoids those states/actions by construction, so C* for the safety-optimal fallback is unbounded as an artefact of how nominal data is generated; pessimism's floor is the unsafe floor. This converts "partial coverage" from a benign assumption into a near-certain violation. The formalization (data law selection bias near the failure set) is not in any source I found.
2. The "useless but conservative fallback": pessimism keeps soundness of the certificate but, with argmax extraction, picks a low-uncertainty (nominal-like) action. Reward-RL theory only reports suboptimality in value; it does not analyze the quality of an argmax executed as an emergency maneuver. A suboptimality-in-value statement for V does not give a safety guarantee for the executed action sequence.
3. Calibration cannot rescue it: conformal guarantees at nominal inputs do not extend to fallback-action inputs without density-ratio weights (Tibshirani et al.), and Lin-Bansal verification calibrates against true rollouts of the fallback, i.e. it requires the very data we say is missing. That makes the claim "conformal pessimism is a verifier of coverage, not a substitute" precise and testable.
4. Max-min operator specifics: pure single-agent HJ (no adversary) needs single-policy-type coverage; with a disturbance (Isaacs game) coverage must be unilateral (Cui-Du), which is strictly stronger. Under the min-with-l structure the error should propagate as a discounted maximum rather than a sum along the comparator (my derivation, UNVERIFIED), so the constants differ from reward-sum theory but not the coverage requirement.
5. Scope caveat from theory: lower bounds are worst-case over model classes. In a smooth low-dimensional proxy (pendulum), a latent model that extrapolates action effects could in principle succeed, so the pre-registered result should be framed as "for the model classes and penalties tested, and not implied universally". Wang-Foster-Kakade and Foster et al. further imply that coverage may be necessary but not sufficient with generic function approximation, so "off-policy counterfactual data recovers it" is an empirical finding consistent with, not implied by, theory.

### Recommended framing for the paper (from the evidence)
- Do not claim a new theorem. State: "Our result instantiates the partial-coverage barrier (single-policy concentrability) for discounted HJ max-min values learned in a latent model, and shows empirically that standard deployment-time cautions are covered-comparator pessimism and therefore cannot recover an uncovered evasive action." Cite Jin et al., Rashidinejad et al., Buckman et al., Xiao et al., MOReL Prop 4, Cui-Du, Foster et al.
- Possible cheap contribution: a short proposition (two-point indistinguishability for the Fisac operator with restricted/penalized max, plus the "floor is the unsafe nominal action" argument) stated as an instantiation, with the proof in an appendix.
