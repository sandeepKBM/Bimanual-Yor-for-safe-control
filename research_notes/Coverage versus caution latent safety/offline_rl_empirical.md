Researcher: offline-RL empirical lane. Date of coverage cut: 2026-10-01.

Reading/verification conventions:
- "READ" means I read the paper's full text locally (PDF -> pdftotext) and the quote is copied from it (whitespace-normalised; hyphenation fixed).
- "UNVERIFIED" means the quote came only from an abstract page / search snippet / secondary summary and I did not read the full text.
- All local PDFs/txt are in /common/users/ss5772/bimanual_data/lit/pdfs/ (new this session: arxiv_2201.13425, 2111.04714, 2306.09303, 2107.09003, 2204.08957, 2407.14653, 2412.08794, 2412.15429, 2412.18946, 2505.00908, 2402.12527, 2506.19643, 2504.16680, 2406.09329, 2509.05735, 2312.05742, 2410.20092, 2607.02288, 2512.04341, 2502.12391, 2309.15178, survival_instinct_neurips23.pdf, and others).
- Closeness labels: SCOOPS = already shows (or states) our candidate claim for learned safety values; PARTIAL = shows a transferable ingredient (data > algorithm, or pessimism fails w/o coverage) but not for a learned HJ safety value / world model with a max over actions; BACKGROUND = context only.
- Recent/concurrent = 2025-2026, flagged "[RECENT]".

Bottom line up front (adversarial verdict):
1. The generic slogan "change the data, not the algorithm" is ESTABLISHED in generic offline RL (ExORL 2022; OGBench 2024; Park et al. 2024; Chen et al. 2025 for world models). It is folklore-level for returns/policy performance.
2. I found NO paper that SCOOPS the specific claim "for a learned reachability/HJ safety value, deployment-time caution (conformal/ensemble pessimism, in-support restriction, DAgger) cannot substitute for counterfactual action coverage, while ~3-10k off-policy counterfactual branches fix it." Nearest neighbours are PROCO (2605.01356, May 2026 [RECENT]) and V-OCBF/CCBF/SafeFQL (offline HJ/CBF with in-support pessimism), which assert "unsafe/uncovered data is the bottleneck" as motivation but then ship an algorithmic fix, not a data-coverage test.
3. The literature is NOT one-sided. Several empirical papers show pessimism/in-support restriction WORKS (Survival Instinct; FISOR; DRCORL; Jeen et al.; Lu et al.; RWM-U). A reviewer will cite these. Our claim must be scoped: nominal data lacking evasive actions + max_u in a learned model + filter-fallback use.
4. Our "caution cannot substitute for coverage" wording is close to prior MOPO/RAMBO/DSRL rhetoric ("mistakes in the dynamics will not be corrected with additional data collection"), so the novelty is the quantified, pre-registered head-to-head on a safety value, not the idea.

## Q1. "Don't Change the Algorithm, Change the Data" (ExORL) and similar: what exactly was shown? Does it cover safety/constraint values?

### Takeaway
ExORL (Yarats, Brandfonbrener, Liu, Laskin, Abbeel, Lazaric, Pinto; ICML 2022; arXiv:2201.13425) showed on DMC locomotion/manipulation that vanilla TD3 on diverse reward-free exploratory data matches or beats offline-specific (pessimistic/constrained) algorithms, i.e. coverage substitutes for conservatism. It says nothing about safety, constraints, or value functions of a different type (reachability); the evaluation metric is task return. Label: PARTIAL (strongest generic precedent for "data > algorithm"), not SCOOPS.

### Cited Findings
- ExORL abstract: "We find that exploratory data allows vanilla off-policy RL algorithms, without any offline-specific modifications, to outperform or match state-of-the-art offline RL algorithms on downstream tasks. Our findings suggest that data generation is as important as algorithmic advances for offline RL and hence requires careful consideration from the community." READ — [arXiv:2201.13425](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.13425)
- ExORL main finding 1: "With sufficiently diverse exploratory data, vanilla off-policy RL agents can effectively learn offline, and even outperform carefully designed offline RL algorithms. This suggests that advances in data collection are as important as algorithmic advances for offline RL." READ — same
- ExORL Q1/A1: "vanilla TD3 consistently outperforms more sophisticated offline RL algorithms (TD3+BC, CRR, and CQL). This is a somewhat surprising result because the offline RL algorithms were designed specifically for the offline setting while TD3 was not." Their hypothesis: "ExORL produces diverse datasets that allow for more aggressive trajectory stitching and less severe extrapolation than prior datasets." READ — same
- ExORL discussion: "using diverse data can greatly simplify offline RL algorithms by removing the need to fight the extrapolation problem." READ — same
- ExORL explicitly disclaims being a better benchmark ("we are not saying that these new datasets are better offline RL benchmarks") and found failure modes of coverage: Jaco random data beat unsupervised exploration because exploring "low-reward positions outside of the workspace" wasted data; Cheetah Run "insufficient exploration of the higher-dimensional state space in regions of high reward". READ — same. (Note for us: undirected coverage can be wasteful; our finding that random vs directed selection didn't matter is consistent with the first half but ExORL is a task-return metric.)
- Safety/constraints: grep of full text finds no safety or constraint-value experiments (only task rewards). READ.
- MoCoDA (Pitis, Creager, Mandlekar, Garg; NeurIPS 2022; arXiv:2210.11287): "We use MoCoDA to train an offline RL agent to solve an out-of-distribution robotics manipulation task on which standard offline RL algorithms fail." (Counterfactual augmentation of actions/states via a locally factored dynamics model.) UNVERIFIED (abstract only). Label: PARTIAL (closest generic analogue of "counterfactual action branches", but task returns, requires known local factorization, no safety). — [arXiv:2210.11287](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.11287)
- OGBench (Park, Frans, Eysenbach, Levine; ICLR 2025; arXiv:2410.20092): "Datasets must be noisy enough. The results are quite remarkable: they show that having the right amount of noise (i.e., state coverage) is very important for achieving good performance. For example, the performance drops from 99% to 6% if there is no noise in expert actions, even on the most basic cube pick-and-place task. This suggests that, we may need to prioritize coverage much more than optimality when collecting datasets for offline GCRL." READ — [arXiv:2410.20092](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.20092). Label: PARTIAL [data-collection ablation, not pessimism-vs-coverage head-to-head; not safety]. (Note: very close in spirit to "nominal data without action noise fails": our nominal vs counterfactual.)
- Park, Frans, Levine, Kumar, "Is Value Learning Really the Main Bottleneck in Offline RL?" (NeurIPS 2024; arXiv:2406.09329): "a big barrier to improving offline RL performance is often imperfect policy generalization on test-time states out of the support of the training data, rather than policy learning on in-distribution states. We then show that the use of suboptimal but high-coverage data or test-time policy training techniques can address this generalization issue in practice." READ — [arXiv:2406.09329](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09329). Label: PARTIAL (data coverage vs algorithm; test-time states, not unseen actions; no safety).
- Chen, Shi, Sancaktar, Frey, Martius, "Offline vs. Online Learning in Model-based RL: Lessons for Data Collection Strategies" (RLJ/RLC 2025; arXiv:2509.05735) [RECENT]: "We identify a key challenge behind performance degradation of offline agents: encountering Out-Of-Distribution states at test time. This issue arises because, without the self-correction mechanism in online agents, offline datasets with limited state space coverage induce a mismatch between the agent's imagination and real rollouts, compromising policy training... We also showcase that incorporating exploration data helps mitigate the performance degradation of offline agents. Based on our insights, we recommend adding exploration data when collecting large datasets, as current efforts predominantly focus on expert data alone." And in the intro they position against MOPO: "While prior works, such as MOPO (Yu et al., 2020), focus on mitigating distributional shift by penalizing model uncertainty during policy deployment, our study takes a fundamentally different approach: rather than addressing OOD errors post hoc, we investigate how data diversity..." READ — [arXiv:2509.05735](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05735). Label: PARTIAL (strongest: learned world models, imagination/real mismatch from limited coverage, exploration data fixes it; 31 envs; task performance, no safety/reachability value, no head-to-head with a pessimistic penalty). It ALSO says expert-only data "exacerbates OOD issues" (their 3.5.3), matching our "nominal data" setting.
- He, Zhang, Shao, Jiang, Ji, "Unsupervised Data Generation for Offline RL: A Perspective from Model" (arXiv:2506.19643, 2025) [RECENT]: "Previous offline RL research focuses on restricting the offline algorithm in in-distribution even in-sample action sampling. In contrast, fewer work pays attention to the influence of the batch data... with mild assumptions, the distance between the state-action pair distribution generated by the behavioural policy and the distribution generated by the optimal policy, accounts for the performance gap" (model-based offline RL, MOPO-style bound re-derived w.r.t. data). READ — [arXiv:2506.19643](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.19643). Label: PARTIAL (theory + task-return experiments; same "data not algorithm" thesis for model-based offline RL).

### Inferences
- "Data coverage > algorithmic conservatism" is already an established empirical thesis for task return (ExORL, OGBench, Park 2024, Chen 2025). A reviewer will say our headline is a restatement unless we stress what is new: (a) the object is a safety/reachability value with a max_u, (b) a pre-registered comparison against four specific deployment-time cautions including conformal and ensemble pessimism, (c) failure of caution is quantified (79%, 40% + 28x false alarms, 21%, 10-23%) and the fix is quantified (3-10k off-policy counterfactual branches -> 1-3%).
- ExORL's "random data beat directed exploration on Jaco" and our "random vs uncertainty- vs value-directed did not matter" are in the same vein; cite as consistent, not as new.
- Nobody in this lane tested ExORL-style data for HJ/CBF/constraint values.

- No ExORL follow-up with safety constraints found. Search was not exhaustive for "exploratory data + constrained RL" (e.g., safe-exploration data generators for offline safe RL).

## Q2. Dataset-characteristic studies (Schweighofer et al.; D4RL composition; "data diversity vs pessimism"): conclusions on pessimism vs coverage

### Takeaway
Dataset-characteristic studies consistently find that algorithm ranking depends on dataset coverage, that unconstrained (non-pessimistic) value methods need high state-action coverage, and that policy-constraint/pessimistic methods do fine only when the data are high-quality or high-coverage. None of them show that coverage can replace pessimism for safety. Label: PARTIAL/BACKGROUND.

### Cited Findings
- Schweighofer, Radler, Dinu, Hofmarcher, Patil, Bitto-Nemling, Eghbal-zadeh, Hochreiter, "A Dataset Perspective on Offline Reinforcement Learning" (CoLLAs 2022; arXiv:2111.04714): "we show that the unconstrained off-policy Deep Q-Network family requires datasets with high SACo [state-action coverage] to find a good policy. Furthermore, experiments show that policy constraint algorithms perform well on datasets with high TQ and SACo. Finally, the experiments show, that purely dataset-constrained Behavioral Cloning performs competitively to the best Offline RL algorithms for datasets with high TQ." READ — [arXiv:2111.04714](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.04714). Also: "In Offline RL, dataset generation is neither harmonized, nor thoroughly investigated" and notes CQL performance changed by the behavioral policy (Kumar et al. 2020). Label: PARTIAL (documents that data coverage and algorithm interact; no pessimism-vs-coverage head-to-head; discrete envs; no safety).
- Mediratta, You, Jiang, Raileanu, "The Generalization Gap in Offline Reinforcement Learning" (ICLR 2024; arXiv:2312.05742): "Behavioral cloning is a strong baseline, outperforming state-of-the-art offline RL and sequence modeling approaches when trained on data from multiple environments and tested on new ones. Finally, we find that increasing the diversity of the data, rather than its size, improves performance on new environments for all offline learning algorithms." READ (abstract + intro) — [arXiv:2312.05742](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.05742). Label: PARTIAL/BACKGROUND (diversity > size; conservative algorithms don't generalise; Procgen/WebShop; no safety).
- Weltevrede, Spaan, Bohmer, "Generalization in offline RL: The structure is more important than the amount of pessimism" (RLC 2026 Finding-the-Frame workshop; arXiv:2607.02288) [RECENT]: "In offline RL, the structure of the pessimism is determined by the structure of the dataset coverage... We prove that a mildly pessimistic, non-symmetric value function can generalize worse than an overly pessimistic, symmetric one." Their fix is data augmentation applied through a consistency loss. READ — [arXiv:2607.02288](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02288). Label: PARTIAL (explicitly ties pessimism to coverage and advocates augmentation; theory + small reacher experiment with IQL/CQL; no safety). Workshop paper only.
- D4RL composition: Park et al. (OGBench) and Mediratta et al. (above) are the modern successors; I did not locate a separate D4RL "dataset-composition ablation" paper that directly pits pessimism against coverage. (Original D4RL paper, arXiv:2004.07219, is in the local pdf folder? Not checked in this pass.)
- Counter-evidence (pessimism helps when coverage is poor): Jeen, Bewley, Cullen, "Zero-Shot RL from Low Quality Data" (NeurIPS 2024; arXiv:2309.15178): "conservative zero-shot RL algorithms outperform their non-conservative counterparts on low quality datasets, and perform no worse on high quality datasets." READ (abstract) — [arXiv:2309.15178](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.15178). Label: BACKGROUND (adversarial: shows conservatism is valuable on small/homogeneous ExORL data, so "caution never helps" is false for returns).
- Park et al. 2024 itself also reports that behaviour-constrained policy extraction (DDPG+BC) beats weighted BC, i.e. algorithm choice matters a lot; the "data vs algorithm" dichotomy is not clean. READ.

### Inferences
- The empirical consensus is "coverage determines which algorithm works", not "coverage always beats pessimism". Our claim is stronger in one respect (no deployment-time caution rescued the safety value) and narrower in another (one proxy, one value class).
- Weltevrede et al. (2026) is the closest recent conceptual statement that dataset coverage determines pessimism structure; worth citing as concurrent.

### Gaps
- No full-text check of D4RL (2004.07219) dataset-composition ablations in this pass.
- No paper found that varies BOTH pessimism strength and data coverage in a factorial design for a safety value.

## Q3. Offline SAFE RL (CPQ, COptiDICE, FISOR, DSRL, LSPC, VOCE, OASIS, CAPS, ...): constraint violations from poor coverage of unsafe/evasive actions; do they claim conservatism suffices or admit it doesn't?

### Takeaway
The offline safe RL literature widely states that OOD action extrapolation corrupts cost values and that datasets with few unsafe/safe-recovery samples break existing methods, but its fixes are algorithmic (OOD penalties, in-support min, diffusion, data synthesis from the same data). The benchmark (DSRL) shows data density/size affects safety and that Q-learning methods "vacillate between excessive conservatism and riskiness". The closest paper in claim structure is PROCO (May 2026), which says existing pessimistic methods "critically rely on abundant unsafe samples". No paper tests deployment-time caution against off-policy counterfactual data for a learned safety value. None SCOOPS; several PARTIAL.

### Cited Findings

#### PARTIAL, closest
- **PROCO**: Xue, Yuan, Cheng, Yang, Yu, "Model-Based Proactive Cost Generation for Learning Safe Policies Offline with Limited Violation Data" (arXiv:2605.01356, 2 May 2026; Nanjing Univ.) [RECENT/CONCURRENT]. Quotes (READ): "Both approaches [conservative cost value functions; cost-advantage-weighted BC] critically rely on abundant unsafe samples for accurate cost estimation and effective policy optimization. However, in many safety-critical high-stakes scenarios... yielding dataset with only few or no unsafe samples. In such cases, existing methods may fail to enforce safety, as they treat all samples equally safe and overlook safe-yet-infeasible states." and "The root cause of the aforementioned challenge is, due to the absence of unsafe samples, the dataset lacks a crucial piece of knowledge-namely, which behaviors are unsafe or risky." Abstract: "Under this limitation, existing approaches often treat all data as uniformly safe, overlooking safe-but-infeasible states-states that currently satisfy constraints but inevitably violate them within a few steps-leading to deployment failures." Method: learns a dynamics model, rolls out unsafe samples, and defines a conservative feasible Bellman operator in the HJ-reachability form "B*Qh(s,a) := (1-gamma)h(s) + gamma max{h(s), max_s' min_a' Qh(s',a')}" over model-predicted next-state sets. — [arXiv:2605.01356](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01356). Label: PARTIAL, high. Why not SCOOPS: it never compares deployment-time caution against off-policy action coverage; its fix is model-generated unsafe rollouts + LLM-written conservative cost, not counterfactual action branches executed in the true simulator; the "uncovered" quantity is unsafe states, not evasive actions. But it states the "missing coverage of what is unsafe" premise in the same HJ-feasibility vocabulary. MUST be cited as concurrent and positioned against.
- **DSRL** (OSRL benchmark): Liu, Guo, Lin, Yao, Zhu, Cen, Hu, Yu, Zhang, Tan, Zhao, "Datasets and Benchmarks for Offline Safe Reinforcement Learning" (DMLR 2024; arXiv:2306.09303). READ. Quotes:
  - "all Q-learning-based algorithms, including BCQ-Lag, BEAR-Lag, and CPQ, as well as COptiDICE, display performance inconsistencies, vacillating between excessive conservatism and riskiness. CPQ, for example, obtains high rewards at significant safety compromise in Button tasks, while achieving almost zero cost with low rewards in MetaDrive tasks."
  - "Under-estimating cost Q values could result in negligible safety penalties, causing overly risky policies, while overestimations can lead to overly conservative behaviors."
  - "BC-All achieves higher rewards but fails on safety constraints; BC-Safe, fed with only safe trajectories, satisfies most safety requirements, although with conservative performances and lower rewards. This comparison underlines the essential trade-offs between safety and utility in offline safe RL, largely dictated by the training dataset used."
  - "applying a density filter to vary dataset sizes reveals a trend: most algorithms exhibit a decrease in cost values as more data is employed. This finding underscores the pivotal role of dataset size in influencing algorithmic performance. Interestingly, the safety performance of BCQ-Lag and CPQ are noticeably influenced by data sizes... In contrast, CDT showcases robustness against sparse data."
  - Dataset design: datasets are generated by agents trained under varying cost thresholds, then diversified over the cost-reward return space; filters for density, partial-data discarding, noise. i.e. DSRL data DO contain many constraint-violating trajectories. 
  Label: PARTIAL (benchmark-level evidence that data composition determines safety outcomes and that algorithmic conservatism swings between over-cautious and unsafe; no coverage-vs-pessimism intervention; no reachability values). Adversarial point: since DSRL data include violating trajectories, DSRL results do not test our regime (nominal data, no evasive actions).
- **FISOR**: Zheng, Li, Yu, Yang, Li, Zhan, Liu, "Safe Offline RL with Feasibility-Guided Diffusion Model" (ICLR 2024; arXiv:2401.10700). READ. Uses an HJ-style feasible value with an in-support minimum: "the min_a' operation in Eq. (7) could query the out-of-distribution (OOD) actions, potentially leading to severe underestimation issues in offline setting (Fujimoto et al., 2019). To mitigate this, we consider a data support constrained operation min_{a', s.t. pi_beta(a'|s')>0}". Ablation: for the variant w/o HJ, "the presence of approximation errors renders the estimated infeasible region pretty large and inaccurate, which leads to severe policy conservatism and hurt final returns." Baselines Saute-TD3BC and RC-TD3BC (HJ + behaviour regularisation) "fail to obtain good results even with carefully swept hyper-parameters due to the intricate coupling objectives." Also criticises CPQ: "assigns high-cost values to both OOD and unsafe actions, which distorts the value function and may cause poor generalizability." Label: PARTIAL as a baseline/method (this is the in-support HJ value our "FISOR/IQL-style" arm copies); it claims in-support restriction SUFFICES on DSRL, so it is COUNTER-EVIDENCE to our claim in a regime where data contain unsafe trajectories. Note in-support min is the safe-direction (pessimistic) choice for a reachability value (a min over a' in the Bellman backup for safety), which differs from our max_u-for-the-filter use; flag to the writer that FISOR's min is over the adversary-style/safe-Q direction, so check the sign convention before arguing equivalence.
- **CPQ**: Xu, Zhan, Zhu, "Constraints Penalized Q-learning for Safe Offline RL" (AAAI 2022; arXiv:2107.09003). READ. Treats OOD actions as unsafe (pushes up Q_c of OOD actions, via CVAE-based OOD detection). It calls a naive combination of safe RL + offline RL "too conservative and will lead to a sub-optimal solution", and notes the pessimism distorts: "Q_c-values of OOD actions are likely to be larger than their true values". Label: BACKGROUND (an in-support/pessimism baseline; coverage not studied).
- **COptiDICE**: Lee, Paduraru, Mankowitz, Heess, Precup, Kim, Guez (ICLR 2022; arXiv:2204.08957). READ. "offline RL algorithms commonly adopt the pessimism principle, encouraging staying close to the data support. We can observe that such pessimism principle is also effective in offline constrained RL". Label: BACKGROUND/COUNTER (pessimism is claimed effective for constraints on their benchmarks, which assume reasonable data support).
- **OASIS**: Yao, Cen, Ding, Lin, Liu, Zhang, Yu, Zhao, "OASIS: Conditional Distribution Shaping for Offline Safe RL" (arXiv:2407.14653, NeurIPS 2024). READ. ### Inferences
- Nobody in offline safe RL has isolated "coverage of evasive actions" as the controlled variable. DSRL's density/partial-discarding filters are the nearest knobs, but they act on trajectories (return space), not on counterfactual actions at fixed states.
- Pessimism-for-safety is the dominant algorithmic answer and several 2023-2026 papers claim it works (VOCE, DRCORL, FISOR, V-OCBF, SafeFQL). Our result would be the first (to my search) to report a pre-registered failure of FOUR cautions and a data-side fix on a learned safety value. The "data contains unsafe/violating trajectories" condition is the likely reason those papers succeed; that is a testable explanation to state in our paper.
- PROCO (2026) is the one competitor a reviewer will raise: same HJ Bellman form, same diagnosis that missing unsafe knowledge is the root cause. Distinguish on (i) action-level counterfactual coverage vs unsafe-state generation, (ii) true-simulator branches vs model rollouts + LLM cost, (iii) head-to-head against conformal/ensemble caution, (iv) latent WM trained on pixels/latents.
- "Over-conservatism without restoring safety" is documented for cost-critic/CBF pessimism (DSRL, V-OCBF, FISOR w/o HJ), but always with a different cause (value mis-estimation), not "missing evasive actions".

## Q4. Do any papers report that pessimism fails exactly where safety matters (rare evasive actions), or that pessimism causes over-conservatism without restoring safety?

### Takeaway
Yes for over-conservatism-without-safety (DSRL; V-OCBF's critique of CCBF/iDBF; FISOR's w/o-HJ ablation; OASIS; CPQ). For "pessimism fails because the rare evasive action was never in the data", the claim is made as motivation (PROCO, OASIS, FOSP, MOPO/RAMBO rhetoric) but never isolated and tested against the data fix. Label: PARTIAL.

### Cited Findings
- DSRL (above): Q-learning-based methods "vacillating between excessive conservatism and riskiness"; Q-cost misestimation both ways. READ — [arXiv:2306.09303](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09303)
- V-OCBF (above): "Uncertainty-aware approaches address distributional mismatch but often become overly conservative"; CCBF "overly cautious certificates that substantially underestimate the true safe region". READ — [arXiv:2512.10822](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.10822)
- FISOR (above): w/o HJ "severe policy conservatism and hurt final returns" (inaccurate infeasible-region estimate). READ — [arXiv:2401.10700](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10700)
- OASIS (above): regularising toward a skewed dataset "worsens the situation when the dataset is imbalanced and biased". READ — [arXiv:2407.14653](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14653)
- PROCO (above): existing conservative-cost methods "treat all samples equally safe and overlook safe-yet-infeasible states". READ — [arXiv:2605.01356](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01356). Note its example: data collected with external interventions stop right before collision, leaving "terminal states that are currently safe but would soon violate constraints due to inertia" — i.e. exactly missing evasive/recovery behaviour. Closest textual match to our "nominal data lacks evasive actions".
- Survival Instinct (Li, Misra, Kolobov, Cheng; NeurIPS 2023): "pessimism endows the agent with a survival instinct, i.e., an incentive to stay within the data support in the long term, while the limited and biased data coverage further constrains the set of survival policies." and "offline RL is safe as long as the data only contains safe states; thus safe RL in the offline setup can be achieved without specialized algorithms." and "collecting a large set of diverse data might not be necessary or helpful." READ — [NeurIPS 2023 PDF](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/c3e969ea20542a6a11e6caeac736a0b9-Paper-Conference.pdf). Label: PARTIAL, but as ADVERSARIAL counter-evidence: pessimism + safe data => safe policy (stay in the data support); the opposite emphasis from ours ("more coverage of off-nominal actions needed"). Reconcile: their safety = staying in-support when the data's support is itself safe; ours = a filter that must intervene OUT of the nominal support, where in-support restriction has nothing evasive to choose. The writer should discuss this explicitly; it is the most likely "folklore" counter-argument.
- Uncovered-region failure for generic returns: Park et al. 2024 (test-time states out of support) and Chen et al. 2025 (OOD at test time with expert-only data) above.
- Lu et al. (below) and RAMBO (below) report pessimism/penalties help, i.e. pessimism does not "fail" in model-based offline RL on D4RL.

### Inferences
- The specific pattern "pessimism fails exactly where rare evasive actions are needed" is NOT shown empirically anywhere I found; it is argued conceptually (PROCO, MOPO, Survival Instinct's mirror image). That gap is where our result sits.
- Over-conservatism is documented, but over-conservatism is a different failure than ours (our conformal arm is 79% failure, i.e. NOT conservative enough; the ensemble arm is 40% failure with 28x false interventions, i.e. both over-conservative AND unsafe). The "both at once" outcome is reported qualitatively by DSRL ("vacillating between excessive conservatism and riskiness") but not for a latent reachability value.

### Gaps
- No paper found with a metric equivalent to "fraction of savable states where the filter fails vs hold-still baseline".
- Real-world rare-event offline safe RL (e.g. autonomous driving near-miss data) not searched.

## Q5. Model-based offline RL (MOPO, MOReL, COMBO, RAMBO, Lu et al., critiques): do uncertainty penalties fail to fix model exploitation without data?

### Takeaway
Mixed. MOPO/MOReL/RAMBO state the premise ("the model will exploit regions where it is inaccurate; in the offline setting mistakes will not be corrected with additional data collection"), COMBO empirically finds ensemble uncertainty "not accurate", Edge-of-Reach (Sims et al. 2024) shows existing penalties address something else, and recent robot work (RWM-U) shows penalties DO work on real robots. No MBRL-offline paper tests whether adding off-policy action coverage beats a penalty on a safety value; the closest data-side evidence is Chen et al. 2025 (exploration data helps offline world models) and Lu et al. (penalties help). Label: PARTIAL/BACKGROUND.

### Cited Findings
- MOPO (Yu, Thomas, Yu, Ermon, Zou, Levine, Finn, Ma; NeurIPS 2020; arXiv:2005.13239): "models will become increasingly inaccurate further from the behavioral distribution, and vanilla model-based policy optimization algorithms may exploit these regions where the model is inaccurate. This concern is especially important in the offline setting, where mistakes in the dynamics will not be corrected with additional data collection." Also admits narrow-data weakness: future work "to improve the performance of MOPO on narrow data distributions (such as the 'medium' datasets in D4RL)." READ — [arXiv:2005.13239](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.13239). Label: BACKGROUND (premise statement; penalty = their remedy).
- MOReL (Kidambi, Rajeswaran, Netrapalli, Joachims; NeurIPS 2020; arXiv:2005.05951): "a naive MBRL approach that directly plans with the learned model may over-estimate rewards in unfamiliar parts of the state space, resulting in a highly sub-optimal policy". Remedy: pessimistic MDP with a known/unknown detector. READ — [arXiv:2005.05951](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.05951). BACKGROUND.
- COMBO (Yu, Kumar, Rafailov, Rajeswaran, Levine, Finn; NeurIPS 2021; arXiv:2102.08363): "practical variants of such model-based algorithms rely on explicit uncertainty quantification for incorporating conservatism. Uncertainty estimation with complex models, such as deep neural networks, can be difficult and unreliable. We empirically find that uncertainty estimation is not accurate and leads to poor performance in certain scenarios in offline model-based RL." READ — [arXiv:2102.08363](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08363). Label: PARTIAL (explicit empirical statement that ensemble-style uncertainty penalties are unreliable, supporting our "UNISafe-style ensemble pessimism fails" arm; but COMBO's remedy is an algorithm (value regularisation on model-generated data), not data).
- RAMBO (Rigter, Lacerda, Hawes; NeurIPS 2022; arXiv:2204.12581): "if the dataset does not cover the entire state-action space, the model will inevitably be inaccurate for some state-action pairs. Thus, naive policy optimisation on a learnt model in the offline setting can result in model exploitation." Remedy: adversarially modify the transition model (robust MDP). READ — [arXiv:2204.12581](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.12581). BACKGROUND/PARTIAL (the max-over-actions-exploits-model-error mechanism we cite; algorithmic remedy).
- Lu, Ball, Parker-Holder, Osborne, Roberts, "Revisiting Design Choices in Offline Model-Based RL" (ICLR 2022; arXiv:2110.04135): "Contrary to common wisdom, conducting significantly longer rollouts inside the model, coupled with larger uncertainty penalties, typically improves performance." and "Penalties that use canonical forms of uncertainty estimation achieve better correlation with OOD measures." READ — [arXiv:2110.04135](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04135). Label: COUNTER-EVIDENCE (penalty tuning helps on D4RL MuJoCo; they do not vary coverage; MuJoCo returns, not safety). Do not cite as "uncertainty penalties fail".
- Edge-of-Reach: Sims, Lu, Foerster, Teh, "The Edge-of-Reach Problem in Offline Model-Based RL" (NeurIPS 2024; arXiv:2402.12527): "if the learned dynamics model is replaced by the true error-free dynamics, existing model-based methods completely fail... rollout truncation results in a set of edge-of-reach states at which we are effectively 'bootstrapping from the void.' This triggers pathological value overestimation and complete performance collapse... prior model-based methods are primarily addressing the edge-of-reach problem, rather than model inaccuracy as claimed." Fix: RAVL (value pessimism). READ — [arXiv:2402.12527](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12527). Label: PARTIAL (mechanism: unvisited/edge states + max in the value backup produce overestimation that dynamics-uncertainty penalties don't address; fix is algorithmic value pessimism). Useful for us: their diagnosis (value overestimation at states/actions never backed up) is analogous to our max_u V exploiting unseen actions; but it is not about coverage-vs-pessimism.
- Ni et al., "Long-Horizon Model-Based Offline RL Without Explicit Conservatism" (arXiv:2512.04341; ICML 2026?) [RECENT]. Skimmed first paragraph only: argues popular methods "rely on explicit conservatism, penalizing out-of-dataset actions or restricting rollout horizons." UNVERIFIED beyond abstract fragment. Label: BACKGROUND (alternative: Bayesian/meta-RL view; no coverage study).
- Li, Krause, Hutter, "Uncertainty-Aware Robotic World Model Makes Offline MBRL Work on Real Robots" (RWM-U, arXiv:2504.16680 v3 Jan 2026) [RECENT]: "The resulting policies consistently outperform model-free and uncertainty-unaware model-based baselines, and fusing real-world data in model learning further yields robust policies." READ (abstract). Label: COUNTER-EVIDENCE/BACKGROUND (uncertainty penalty helps on real robots; they also report that adding real-world data helps, i.e. both levers help).
- Chen et al. 2025 (above, arXiv:2509.05735): the only world-model paper I found that frames "data diversity instead of post-hoc OOD penalties" and shows exploration data restores performance; but no head-to-head with MOPO-style penalties; no safety.

### Inferences
- Model-based offline RL says clearly: (i) argmax over a learned model exploits errors in uncovered regions (MOPO, MOReL, RAMBO, Edge-of-Reach), (ii) ensemble uncertainty is unreliable (COMBO) or reliable if tuned (Lu et al., RWM-U). So "uncertainty penalties fail without data" is NOT established; it is contested. For a reviewer, our ensemble-pessimism result (40% fail, 28x false alarms) lands in a literature split between COMBO (unreliable) and Lu et al./RWM-U (works).
- Everyone's remedy is algorithmic; Chen et al. (2025) and He et al. (2025) are the only model-based data-side arguments, neither on safety values.

### Gaps
- Did not find a controlled MBRL-offline experiment that holds the algorithm fixed, sweeps uncertainty-penalty strength, and compares it to adding counterfactual action data on the same model class.
- Did not read COMBO/MOReL experiments for narrow-data failure tables.
